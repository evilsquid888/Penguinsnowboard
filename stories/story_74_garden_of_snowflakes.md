# Story 74: Garden of Snowflakes
### *Inspired by Prayer in Gethsemane — Matthew 26*
**Characters:** Gabe Goat, Nora Snowmane  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Olive Glade  
**Theme:** Prayer in hard moments

---

## Opening Wink

Even the bravest snowboarder in Frostpeak Valley sometimes needs a quiet place to sit, a friend nearby, and permission to say, "I'm scared."

## The Story

The moon hung low over Frostpeak Valley like a giant silver coin somebody had tossed into the sky and forgotten to catch. Snowflakes drifted down in lazy spirals, each one glowing faintly blue in the moonlight, and the whole world smelled like pine needles and cold, clean air.

Bjorn Bigpaws stood at the edge of Olive Glade, his massive paws resting on his ski poles, and stared at the path that wound up toward Ridgeback Summit. Tomorrow was the biggest day Frostpeak Valley had ever known. Tomorrow, Bjorn had promised to lead the entire valley through the Great Avalanche Corridor--a treacherous route that no one had attempted in fifty winters--because a group of young seal pups had gotten stranded on the far side, and the only way to reach them was straight through the danger zone.

He had volunteered. Of course he had. That was what Bjorn did. He was the dignified leader, the steady one, the bear who always said, "I will go first."

But tonight, standing in Olive Glade where the ancient snowflake willows drooped their crystal branches low enough to form a kind of glittering tent, Bjorn did not feel dignified. He felt small. Smaller than Willa Wobble, and Willa was barely taller than a cocoa mug.

"Would you three come with me?" he had asked earlier that evening, turning to Piper Paddlefoot, Barnaby Beaksworth, and Finnegan Flipsworth. "Just to the glade. Just to sit nearby while I... think."

"Absolutely!" Piper had declared, thumping her flippers together. "Slide into it! Er--slide into sitting. We can do that."

"I brought snacks," Barnaby announced, hoisting a backpack that clinked and rustled with what sounded like fourteen different kinds of fish crackers. "Nobody thinks well on an empty stomach."

"I'll guard the perimeter!" Finnegan said, striking a dramatic pose and immediately toppling sideways into a snowdrift. "From down here! I'll guard it from down here!"

So the four of them had trekked to Olive Glade together, the snow crunching softly under their feet and flippers, the moonlight painting long blue shadows across the path.

Now Bjorn settled onto a large, flat stone beneath the biggest snowflake willow. The branches hung around him like a curtain of tiny ice chandeliers, tinkling softly whenever the breeze stirred. He could see his three friends about twenty paces away, huddled together at the edge of the glade.

"Stay awake for me?" Bjorn called softly. "Just... be here. I need to know you're here."

"We're not going anywhere!" Piper called back, giving a firm flipper-salute.

"Wide awake!" Barnaby confirmed, already unwrapping a packet of sardine crunchies.

"Alert as a hawk!" Finnegan declared, pulling his woolly hat down more snugly over his rockhopper crest.

Bjorn turned away from them and looked up through the crystal branches at the moon. The snowflakes fell on his nose, on his ears, on his broad shoulders. He closed his eyes.

"I'm scared," he whispered, and the words came out in a little cloud of frost that hung in the air like a tiny ghost before dissolving. "I know I said I'd go. I know the seal pups need help. I know I'm the only one big enough to break trail through the corridor. But the avalanche warnings are real, and the ice is unstable, and..."

He paused. A snowflake landed on his eyelash.

"What if I'm not strong enough?" he said, even quieter. "What if I let everyone down?"

The wind hushed. The snowflake willows tinkled. Somewhere, an owl hooted a single soft note, as if to say, *I'm listening.*

Bjorn talked for a long time. He talked about being afraid. He talked about how heavy it felt to be the one everyone counted on. He talked about how he wished there were another way--any other way--but there wasn't, and he knew it, and that was the hardest part. He talked about the seal pups' little faces and how they must be so cold and so frightened right now, and that was the thing that kept him going even when his own heart trembled.

"If this is what I'm meant to do," he said finally, his voice rough and honest, "then I'll do it. But I needed to say the scared part out loud first. I needed someone to hear it."

He wiped his eyes with the back of one enormous paw, took a long, shaky breath, and stood up. The glade felt quieter now, as if even the snowflakes had paused to listen and were only now remembering to fall again.

Bjorn walked back toward his friends.

Piper was slumped against a tree trunk, her beak tucked into her scarf, fast asleep. Little *zzzzz* sounds escaped from under the orange wool.

Barnaby was curled in a ball on the snow, a half-eaten fish cracker still clutched in one flipper, snoring gently. A trail of cracker crumbs led from his backpack to his beak like a tiny breadcrumb road.

Finnegan was lying flat on his back, his hat over his face, making a sound that was somewhere between a snore and a whistle, like a tiny teakettle that had given up on life.

Bjorn looked at them. A hundred feelings flickered across his face--disappointment, loneliness, a flash of frustration. He had asked them to stay awake. Just to *be here*.

But then he looked more closely. Piper's flipper was stretched out toward the glade, as if even in sleep she'd been reaching for him. Barnaby had shared his last sardine crunchie with Finnegan before dozing off--the wrapper was still between them. And Finnegan's hat had "GO BJORN" scribbled on it in Cleo's glitter paint.

They had tried. They had come. They just... couldn't quite manage the staying-awake part.

"Hey," Bjorn said softly, nudging Piper with his paw. "Hey, wake up."

Piper startled awake with a snort. "I wasn't sleeping! I was resting my eyes! Strategically! With my eyes closed! In a horizontal position!"

Barnaby sat up, cracker crumbs cascading from his chest like tiny edible snowflakes. "What'd I miss? Is it breakfast? Did someone say breakfast?"

Finnegan rolled over, sat up, and his hat fell off, revealing his crest sticking in seven different directions. "I was awake the whole time," he said with absolute, shameless confidence. "I was doing surveillance. With my eyes shut. It's an advanced technique."

Bjorn looked at the three of them--rumpled, crumb-covered, sleepy-eyed, and so completely, wonderfully *there*--and his heart swelled.

"I asked you to do one thing," he said, and his voice was gentle, not angry.

Piper's beak wobbled. "We're sorry, Bjorn. We really, really tried."

"I know you did," Bjorn said. He sat down heavily in the snow between them, and they all scooted closer until they were pressed against his warm fur. "I know you did. And you came. That matters."

They sat there in the moonlit glade, four friends in a pile, the snowflake willows singing their tiny ice-crystal lullaby above them. Nobody said anything for a while. Piper rested her head against Bjorn's shoulder. Barnaby quietly offered him a fish cracker. Finnegan, for once, was still.

"Tomorrow's going to be hard," Bjorn said.

"We know," Piper whispered.

"We'll be right behind you," Barnaby said.

"Every step," Finnegan added, and for once there was no joke in his voice at all.

The moon drifted higher. The snowflakes kept falling. And somewhere in the glade, a single snowflake willow branch chimed--one clear, bell-like note--as if the whole valley were saying, *You are not alone. You were never alone. Even when your friends fall asleep, even when the path is dark, even when you're scared all the way down to your bones--you are held.*

Bjorn closed his eyes. He breathed in the cold, sweet air. And though he was still afraid, something warm had settled in his chest alongside the fear--something that felt like being known, and being loved anyway.

"Okay," he said quietly, to the snow, to his friends, to the moon. "Okay. I'm ready."

A seal popped up from behind a snowbank where it had apparently been napping. It clapped its flippers twice--*flap flap*--gave Bjorn a solemn nod, and disappeared again.

"Was that a standing ovation?" Finnegan whispered.

"Lying-down ovation," Piper corrected. "But it counts."

It counted.
