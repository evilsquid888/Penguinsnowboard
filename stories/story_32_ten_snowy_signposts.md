# Story 32: Ten Snowy Signposts
### *Inspired by The Ten Commandments — Exodus 20*
**Characters:** Granite Ram, Bjorn Bigpaws  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Summit Switchbacks  
**Theme:** Boundaries that protect

---

## Opening Wink

Ever noticed how the best snow days have a few rules? Like "don't eat yellow snow" and "always share your cocoa." Tonight, the valley learned that rules aren't fences -- they're guardrails on the best ride of your life.

## The Story

The trouble started, as trouble often does in Frostpeak Valley, with Finnegan Flipsworth going the wrong direction at top speed.

It was the first Saturday of Deep Winter, and every creature in the valley had gathered at Summit Switchbacks for the Grand Slope Opening. The morning air tasted like peppermint and pine sap. Fresh powder blanketed the mountain so thickly that the trail markers had vanished overnight, and the whole mountainside looked like one enormous, unmarked birthday cake frosted in white.

"Slide into it!" Piper Paddlefoot hollered, launching herself off a mogul before anyone had even buckled their boots.

Within seconds, the slopes turned into a circus without a ringmaster. Finnegan carved hard left when the trail bent right and shot straight through Barnaby Beaksworth's snack station, sending fish crackers flying like tiny golden fireworks. Two seal pups on toboggans collided with a snowbank and popped out giggling. Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff, the bear cubs, had invented a game they called "sledgehammer bowling" which involved rolling snowballs the size of igloos downhill toward anyone who wasn't paying attention.

"Somebody is going to lose a mitten!" Nora Snowmane called from the warming lodge, her calm voice somehow carrying over the pandemonium.

"Somebody already lost TWO mittens!" Finnegan yelled back, wiggling his bare flippers.

Bjorn Bigpaws stood at the top of the switchbacks, watching the chaos unfold with the expression of a grandfather who has just discovered marker drawings on his best quilt. He planted his ski poles and took a very long, very slow breath through his enormous black nose.

"Friends," he announced in his deepest rumble, "we need to talk."

"About WHAT?" Piper skidded to a stop beside him, spraying snow across his belly fur. "Everything's fine! It's just a little... energetic."

As if to illustrate her point, a snowball the size of a washing machine rolled past them, followed by Teddy Powderpuff shouting, "FORE! Or whatever you say when it's snow!"

Bjorn looked at Piper. Piper looked at the snowball. The snowball crashed into Herschel the Walrus's cocoa cart, and for one terrible moment, the entire valley held its breath as seventeen mugs of peppermint cocoa wobbled on the edge of disaster.

Herschel caught every single one. Because Herschel always catches the cocoa.

"Perhaps," Bjorn said quietly, "we could use a few guidelines."

"RULES?" Finnegan gasped, as though Bjorn had suggested they all eat broccoli for dessert. "Rules are for... for... places that aren't fun!"

"I dunno," Willa Wobble said, waddling up the hill at her own careful pace. The little blue penguin barely reached Bjorn's knee, but she had a way of saying things that made everyone lean in to listen. "Roads have lines painted on them, and roads are still fun to drive on."

"She makes a point," Barnaby said through a mouthful of the fish crackers he'd salvaged from the snow. "Without lane markings, everyone would just crash into each other."

"We ARE crashing into each other!" Magnus Meltsworth growled from behind a tree, where he'd retreated after Tilda had accidentally ski-jumped over his head. A clump of snow sat on his ear like a lopsided beret, and he looked deeply offended about it.

Bjorn raised one massive paw. "Here is what I propose. We each think of one important guideline -- one thing that would make the mountain better for everyone -- and we'll post them as signposts along the switchbacks. Not to spoil the fun. To protect it."

A murmur rippled through the crowd. Cleo Coldwater, perched on a rock with her sketchbook, raised her wing. "Can I paint them? I've been wanting to try my new frost-blue pigment."

"Wouldn't have it any other way," Bjorn smiled.

And so the Great Signpost Summit began.

They gathered in a wide circle near the warming lodge, cocoa in paws and flippers, breath puffing out in little clouds. Herschel distributed cinnamon sticks for stirring and thinking.

Bjorn went first. "Signpost one: Look out for each other on the mountain. No rider left behind."

Everyone nodded. That one was easy.

"Signpost two," Nora said gently. "Tell the truth, even when it's awkward. If you broke somebody's ski pole, say so."

Finnegan suddenly became very interested in the bottom of his cocoa mug.

"Signpost three!" Piper jumped up. "Be brave for the scared ones. If someone's afraid of a jump, ride beside them."

Willa smiled up at Piper. She remembered three weeks ago when Piper had held her flipper at the top of Glacier Drop and said, "I won't let go until you're ready." Willa had been ready sooner than she expected.

"Signpost four," Barnaby said, surprising everyone by putting down his snack bag. "Share what you've got. Even the last fish cracker."

"Even the LAST one?" Finnegan looked horrified.

"Especially the last one," Barnaby said firmly. "The last one matters the most."

Magnus grumbled his way to standing. "Signpost five. Respect the mountain. Don't go carving up trails just because you can. The snow was here before us, and it'll be here after."

The crowd went quiet for a moment, because Magnus rarely spoke, and when he did, it landed like a boulder in a pond -- heavy and impossible to ignore.

Cleo held up her sketchbook. She'd already painted a tiny signpost with a golden border. "Signpost six: Make room for beauty. Don't rush past the sunset just to get one more run in."

"Signpost seven," Willa said softly. "Be patient with the slow ones. Not everyone rides at the same speed, and that's perfectly okay."

Teddy and Tilda looked at each other. Tilda raised a small paw. "Signpost eight! Say sorry when you bonk someone with a snowball. Even if it was really, really funny."

"It WAS really funny," Teddy whispered.

"It was," Magnus admitted, brushing the snow from his ear. "But the sorry part matters too."

Herschel leaned on his cocoa cart, his magnificent whiskers twitching. "Signpost nine, young friends: Rest when you're tired. The mountain doesn't run out of snow overnight. There will always be tomorrow."

Nine signposts. Everyone looked around. Who had the tenth?

Finnegan Flipsworth, of all creatures, stood up. His yellow crest feathers were matted with snow, one goggle lens was fogged, and he had a fish cracker stuck to his belly. He looked ridiculous and completely sincere.

"Signpost ten," he said. "Have fun. Like, actual fun -- the kind where everyone's laughing, not just the one doing the bonking. Fun that you'd want to tell your pillow about before you fall asleep."

For a long moment, nobody spoke.

Then Barnaby started clapping his flippers. Then Piper stomped her feet. Then the seal pups barked, and Herschel banged his cocoa ladle against the cart, and the whole valley erupted in the warmest, loudest, most mitten-muffled applause Summit Switchbacks had ever heard.

Cleo spent the rest of the afternoon painting ten beautiful signposts in frost-blue and gold, each one decorated with tiny snowflakes and the pawprints of whoever had spoken the words. Bjorn and Magnus drove the posts into the snow along the switchbacks, spacing them so that every rider would pass each one on the way down.

And when the slopes reopened, something wonderful happened. The riding wasn't slower. It wasn't less exciting. If anything, the runs felt faster, smoother, and infinitely more joyful -- because nobody was watching their back for runaway snowballs, and everyone knew the creature beside them would help if they fell.

Finnegan still went the wrong direction once, but he apologized to the snowbank, which everyone agreed was progress.

As the aurora painted the sky in ribbons of green and violet, the friends gathered one last time around the warming lodge. The signposts glowed faintly in the twilight, ten little beacons dotting the mountainside like a constellation of good ideas.

"You know what's funny?" Piper said, leaning against Bjorn's enormous arm. "I thought rules would make things smaller. But these made everything bigger."

Bjorn looked out at the valley -- at the fresh tracks in the powder, at the lanterns flickering to life, at the cocoa steam rising into the cold air like tiny prayers.

"The best boundaries," he said, "aren't walls. They're guardrails on the best ride you'll ever take."

Willa yawned. It was the kind of yawn that starts small and takes over your whole face, and within seconds, every penguin, every bear, and even Herschel the Walrus was yawning too.

"Goodnight, signposts," Willa whispered as Nora carried her toward the lodge.

The signposts didn't answer. But the wind chimes on the warming lodge played a soft little tune, and the snow fell gently, gently, gently -- covering the valley in a blanket as warm as ten good promises.
