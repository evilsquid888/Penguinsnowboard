# Story 6: Piper and the Parting Snow
### *Inspired by Moses and the Red Sea — Exodus 14*
**Characters:** Piper Paddlefoot, Aurora Frostholm, all the animals of Frostpeak Valley
**Setting:** The Glacier Wall at the far end of the valley
**Theme:** Faith when the way seems impossible, step forward and the path appears

---

> *Pull the blanket up. This is a story about a moment when the mountain itself stepped aside.*

---

## Opening Wink

When the path looks impossible, Frostpeak experts recommend deep breaths, linked flippers, and at least one friend who yells encouraging nonsense.

## The Story

There was a day when the animals of Frostpeak Valley found themselves in a very impossible situation.

Not the *fun* kind of impossible — like "it's impossible to eat just one of Barnaby's fish crackers." The genuinely difficult kind: a wall of solid glacier ice, a hundred feet high, standing directly in their path, and behind them, the most terrifying thing in all of Frostpeak Valley was coming.

The Melt-Rush.

Every year, in the height of deep winter's ending, the high glacier above the valley sent a great sheet of dense, heavy, surging slush down the mountain — not a flood like before, but a wall of fast-moving ice-slush ten feet tall, taking everything in its path and depositing it, ungently, at the bottom of the valley. Usually the animals had all cleared out by then. Usually.

This year, something had gone wrong with the schedule. The Melt-Rush was two days early.

And the animals had been on a very long valley walk, celebrating the Festival of Fresh Powder, and now they were here — at the far end of the valley, a hundred feet of glacier wall in front of them, and the Melt-Rush roaring up behind them, and no one could see any way through.

---

The panic was spectacular.

Finnegan was saying "WOOOH — wait, no, NOT WOOOH — " and running in a small circle. The glacier foxes were climbing each other. Teddy and Tilda had started crying simultaneously then immediately stopped to stare at each other in surprise, which at least distracted them. Even Bjorn Bigpaws, thermos clutched to his chest, was looking between the glacier wall and the approaching roar with an expression that suggested his chamomile was not working.

Magnus said, "Fine. We're all going to die. I always knew it would be like this."

"We are NOT going to die," said Nora firmly.

"We MIGHT," said Magnus.

"We are NOT," said Nora.

Everyone looked at each other. Everyone looked at the wall. Everyone looked at the roar of the Melt-Rush getting louder.

Then a small clear voice cut through the noise.

*"Step forward."*

Everyone turned.

**Piper Paddlefoot** stood at the front of the crowd, facing the glacier wall. She had her glittery blue snowboard under one flipper. Her goggles were pushed up on her head. Her expression was the quietest anyone had ever seen it — not bold, not brash, not impulsive. Just... *still*.

"Piper," said Aurora carefully. "That is a glacier wall. It is *solid ice*."

"I know," said Piper.

"You can't ride through solid ice."

"I know," said Piper.

"The Melt-Rush is four minutes away."

"I know," said Piper.

She turned around and looked at all of them — at Bjorn with his thermos and Nora with her steady eyes and Finnegan mid-circle and the little bear cubs with matching tearstains. She looked at all of them with a love that was too big for her small body.

She said: *"I don't know what's going to happen. But I know we're supposed to go forward. And I know I trust that. So I'm going first."*

She turned back to the wall.

She strapped on her glittery blue board.

She pushed off.

---

Here is what happened.

When Piper's board touched the ice at the base of the glacier wall, a sound went through the wall — not a crack, not a break, but a *sigh*, deep and enormous and ancient, like the mountain itself breathing out.

And the ice began to move.

Not crumble. Not fall. *Part.* Slowly, then faster — the glacier wall split down the center from the ground up, and each half rolled back like a great curtain being drawn aside, and where there had been solid ice there was now a corridor: clear and dry-floored, the glacier ice forming glittering blue walls on either side, smooth as glass, shot through with deep green light where the sun came through.

It was the most beautiful thing any of them had ever seen.

Aurora breathed: "*Oh.*"

Piper looked back at them all. "Come on," she said simply, and kept riding.

---

They ran.

They ran and slid and tumbled and Finnegan fell twice and Magnus carried three penguins under each arm and Nora brought up the rear making sure no one was left, and the walls of ice on either side of them were the blue of the deepest ocean and the green of the deepest forest and they shone like the inside of a cathedral.

Behind them, the Melt-Rush hit the entrance of the corridor — and the ice walls closed.

With a sound like a great, satisfied *hmmm*, the glacier wall sealed itself again behind the last of them. The Melt-Rush crashed against it and spread harmlessly to either side.

On the other side of the wall, all the animals of Frostpeak Valley stood in a wide, clear meadow of perfect fresh snow, breathing hard, hearts pounding.

And then — because Finnegan was Finnegan — he let out a whoop that echoed off every peak in the range.

Everyone else joined in.

Even Magnus, very quietly, made a sound that might have been a whoop if you weren't listening too carefully.

Piper sat down in the snow and put her head in her flippers. Her whole body was shaking. Not from cold or fear — just from the enormous *aliveness* of it.

Aurora sat beside her and put an arm around her shoulders.

"How did you know?" Aurora asked.

Piper thought about it.

"I didn't know," she said honestly. "I just trusted."

*"That's the same thing,"* said Aurora.
