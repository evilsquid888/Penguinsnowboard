# Story 30: Frogs on the Halfpipe
### *Inspired by The Plagues (soft retelling) — Exodus 7–12*
**Characters:** Finnegan Flipsworth, Phineas Frog  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Halfpipe Hollow  
**Theme:** Let go of stubbornness

---

## Opening Wink

*Revision Pass 3 Spark:* One frog had somehow gotten inside Finnegan's helmet before anyone noticed, and now everyone was taking this more seriously.

Frostpeak had one rule for evenings like this: cocoa first, courage second, and snacks always nearby. Frostpeak did NOT have a rule for frog invasions, because frog invasions had never happened before, and the valley was doing its best.

## The Story

It started with seventeen frogs in the halfpipe.

Nobody knew where they came from. One morning the halfpipe was empty and beautiful, its curved walls perfectly groomed and gleaming, and the next morning **Finnegan Flipsworth** arrived for his dawn training session to find seventeen green frogs sitting along the top lip of the pipe, blinking at him with the patient, slightly smug expression of creatures who know they have made an excellent choice of location.

"Oh no," said Finnegan.

"Bwaaap," said the frogs, which seemed to mean yes.

He tried to shoo them. They hopped in a circle and came back.

He tried to tempt them with snacks. They ate the snacks and stayed.

He got Barnaby to try, because Barnaby had a gift with animals. Barnaby tried for twenty minutes and reported back that the frogs were "unmoved, possibly philosophical, and seemed to have a spokesperson."

The spokesperson was a large frog named **Phineas**, who sat exactly in the center of the halfpipe lip and regarded everyone who approached with the calm of a creature who has made peace with its choices.

"Phineas," said Barnaby, sitting across from him. "What would make you leave?"

"Bwaaap," said Phineas.

"I don't know what that means," said Barnaby.

"I think it means 'no,'" said Finnegan.

"Or 'ask me nicely,'" said Barnaby.

"Or 'I am the frog now,'" said Piper, who had arrived and was looking at the situation with the intense expression of someone who had not had enough coffee yet.

The next morning there were thirty-one frogs.

The halfpipe was officially closed.

---

The frogs spread.

By Day Three, there were frogs in Halfpipe Hollow, frogs on the practice slope, frogs sitting on every bench of the Warming Hut's outdoor section. One frog had gotten inside Finnegan's helmet while it was hanging up in the gear room. Finnegan had not checked before putting it on. This was not his best morning.

"There was a FROG," he announced at breakfast.

"We know," said everyone.

"INSIDE. MY HELMET."

"Where were you for the last three days?" said Piper.

By Day Five, the frogs were in the equipment room, in the boot dryers, lined up along the window ledges of the Warming Hut as if waiting for a show. The gear room smelled definitively of pond.

A formal meeting was called.

"We need them gone," said Piper.

"We need them gone," agreed Bjorn.

"They seem happy here," said Cleo, who had been sketching them and was experiencing some complicated feelings about the situation.

"Cleo."

"I'm just saying. From an artistic perspective—"

"*Cleo.*"

"Gone," said Cleo. "Yes. They should go."

The trouble was that nobody was sure how to make them go. You couldn't ski them away. You couldn't shoo them away. You couldn't tempt them away. Phineas the spokesman frog sat in his position of honor and blinked at all delegations with complete serenity.

It was Willa who finally said the thing everyone was thinking but hadn't said yet.

"Do they belong to anyone?"

A silence.

"What do you mean?" said Piper.

"Frogs this organized — seventeen of them appearing all at once, a spokesperson, they're *staying* somewhere specific — these are not random frogs. These are frogs with a home. Do they belong to someone who's holding onto something they should let go of?"

Another silence.

Finnegan squinted. "You think someone in the valley is... frog-cursed?"

"I think," said Willa carefully, "that sometimes things pile up in ways that ask us to change."

---

The answer came from an unlikely direction.

**Old Ramses Reindeer**, who ran the equipment rental booth and was notoriously the most stubborn creature in Frostpeak Valley, had been insisting for three seasons that he had a monopoly on snowboard rental in the entire south section of the mountain. This was not true — Herschel had documentation to the contrary — but Ramses maintained his position with the tenacity of a reindeer who had decided something and would not be un-deciding it.

The south section had been without proper rental services because no one could build a new booth while Ramses's claim was unresolved.

On Day Six of the frog invasion, Willa went to see Ramses.

She sat across from him at his booth and looked at him steadily with her small, serious eyes. "Ramses. The frogs started appearing the same week you filed the exclusion claim."

Ramses harrumphed. "Coincidence."

"There are frogs in Finnegan's helmet."

"That is also a coincidence."

"There are forty-three frogs in Halfpipe Hollow."

Ramses was quiet.

"If you released the claim," Willa said, "everyone could build the booth that the south section needs. Travelers are having to walk an extra mile for rentals. Cubs can't get to equipment. Families are giving up and going home."

Ramses looked at his rental booth. He looked at his paperwork. He looked at Willa.

"Fine," he said. He said it the way large stubborn things say things when they've been waiting for permission to soften. "Fine. I'll withdraw it."

He signed the form. He slid it across the counter.

Willa took it, said "thank you," and left.

The next morning, the halfpipe was empty.

Every single frog: gone. Vanished in the night like a dream of a dream of a pond.

Finnegan arrived for his training session, stared at the empty halfpipe for a long time, and said: "I don't think I'll ever understand this valley."

Piper stood beside him, looking at the clean, frog-free halfpipe. "Did you check your helmet?"

He checked his helmet. It was empty.

"We're good," he said. "We're completely good."

He launched himself down the pipe. The valley was quiet. The frogs were gone. The south-section rental booth opened three weeks later, and the line of grateful travelers wrapped twice around the lodge.

And Phineas — wherever he was — presumably blinked with satisfaction.
