# Story 105: Lazarus and the Longest Nap
### *Inspired by The Raising of Lazarus — John 11:1–44*
**Characters:** Barnaby Beaksworth, Piper Paddlefoot, Bjorn Bigpaws, Teddy & Tilda Powderpuff, Lars the Lemming
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Lars's snow burrow, the Spring Thaw
**Theme:** Hope when all seems lost; love calls us back from the coldest places

---

> *One last story. This one is about the friend everyone thought was gone — and the love that brought him home.*

---

## Opening Wink

*Revision Pass 3 Spark:* When Lars finally opened his eyes, the first thing he saw was Finnegan's face two inches away, and the second thing he heard was Finnegan screaming "HE'S ALIVE!" directly into his ear, which was not the gentle awakening anyone had planned.

Sometimes the deepest sleep just needs the warmest voice to end it.

## The Story

**Lars the Lemming** was the smallest animal in Frostpeak Valley who wasn't a bug, and he was very proud of this distinction.

He was round. He was fuzzy. He had tiny ears and enormous enthusiasm and a laugh that sounded like a squeaky hinge. He wasn't a snowboarder or a skier — his legs were far too short — but he was the valley's unofficial cheerleader. He stood at the bottom of every slope with a little flag and cheered for *everyone*, even the ones who crashed, especially the ones who crashed, because Lars believed that trying was the bravest thing in the world and he wanted everyone to know he'd noticed.

"GREAT CRASH!" he would shout, waving his flag. "VERY DRAMATIC! TEN OUT OF TEN! YOU'RE MY HERO!"

Everyone loved Lars.

So when Lars didn't show up at the slopes one Tuesday morning, people noticed.

"Has anyone seen Lars?" asked Piper, looking around.

"He wasn't at the Warming Hut this morning," said Barnaby, frowning. "He always gets cocoa at sunrise. Always."

"Maybe he slept in?" said Cleo.

But Lars never slept in. Lars was up before the sun, every day, because he said the sunrise was "the world's way of saying good morning back."

---

They found him in his little snow burrow at the edge of the meadow. He was curled up in his nest of dried grass and tiny blankets, completely still.

"Lars?" said Piper, kneeling at the entrance. "Lars, wake up."

He didn't move.

Bjorn arrived, and because the burrow was far too small for a polar bear, he lay flat on his belly in the snow and peered inside with one enormous eye.

"He's breathing," Bjorn said quietly. "Very slowly. Very faintly."

"What happened?" asked Barnaby, his voice cracking.

Old Herschel came waddling up, took one look, and shook his great walrus head.

"Deep cold sleep," he said. "It happens to lemmings sometimes. When the cold gets into their bones just right, they go down deep. Very deep. Deeper than regular sleep."

"How do we wake him up?" asked Piper.

Herschel was quiet for a moment. "Sometimes you can't," he said gently. "Sometimes you just have to wait and hope."

---

Three days passed.

The valley was different without Lars. The slopes were quieter. Nobody stood at the bottom with a flag. Nobody shouted "GREAT CRASH!" when Finnegan wiped out (which was often). The space where Lars's cheerful voice should have been was a silence that everyone felt.

Teddy and Tilda visited the burrow every afternoon. They brought tiny gifts — a smooth pebble, a frost-daisy, a drawing Tilda had made of Lars holding his flag. They set them at the entrance like offerings.

"We didn't mean to—" said Teddy.

"—miss him this much," said Tilda.

"It snuck up on us," they finished together, and their little bear eyes were wet.

Barnaby made cocoa every morning and left a small cup at the burrow entrance. It was always cold by afternoon. He replaced it anyway.

---

On the fourth morning, Piper had an idea.

"What if he can hear us?" she said. "What if he's down there in the deep sleep and he just needs to hear something to find his way back?"

"That's not how deep cold sleep works," said Herschel carefully.

"How do you know?" said Piper. "Have you ever *asked* a lemming what they hear when they're asleep?"

Herschel opened his mouth. Closed it. Conceded the point with a walrus shrug.

So they tried.

Piper sat outside the burrow and talked to Lars. She told him about the slopes. She told him Finnegan had invented a new move called "The Accidental Backflip" (it was just a regular crash, but Finnegan was committed to the branding). She told him the sunrise had been especially pink that morning.

"I noticed it because you always notice it," she said. "And I thought — someone should tell Lars."

Bjorn came next. He didn't talk much. He lay beside the burrow in the snow and hummed — a deep, low, warm rumble, like the mountain itself was singing. Polar bears hum when they feel things too big for words, and Bjorn was feeling something very big.

Cleo brought her sketchbook and sat by the entrance and described what she was drawing. "I'm drawing you, Lars. You're holding your flag. You look very brave."

Barnaby brought fish crackers and talked about recipes. "I'm working on a new cloudberry tart, Lars. You're going to love it. You have to wake up and try it. You *have* to."

---

On the fifth morning, Teddy and Tilda went to the burrow at sunrise. The sky was pink and gold — the kind of sunrise Lars would have called "the world's biggest smile."

Tilda placed a tiny frost-daisy at the entrance. Teddy leaned in close.

"Lars?" he said. "It's us. We miss you. The slopes miss you. Nobody is cheering and it's wrong. Please come back."

Nothing.

And then — so faintly that Teddy almost missed it — a tiny squeak.

"TILDA!" Teddy gasped.

They both leaned in. Two sets of small bear ears, pressed to the entrance of the burrow, holding their breath.

Another squeak. Louder this time. And then a rustle of dried grass. And then two tiny eyes — bright, blinking, impossibly alive — appeared in the darkness of the burrow.

*"...what did I miss?"* said Lars, in a voice like a squeaky hinge that hadn't been oiled in five days.

---

"HE'S ALIVE!" screamed Finnegan from somewhere. (Nobody knew where Finnegan had come from. He had simply materialized, as Finnegan does in moments of maximum emotion.) "LARS IS ALIVE! EVERYONE! THE LEMMING IS BACK!"

And they came.

From every slope and lodge and warming hut and ski lift in the valley, they came running and sliding and tumbling. Piper arrived first, skidding to a stop in a spray of snow. Bjorn arrived second, which was impressive for a polar bear. Barnaby arrived third, carrying a tray of fresh fish crackers and the cloudberry tart he'd been saving.

Lars crawled out of his burrow. He was wobbly. He was blinking in the sunlight. His tiny flag was clutched in one paw, slightly crumpled but still there.

Everyone was crying.

"Guys," said Lars, looking around at all of them with enormous confused eyes. "Guys, I just took a nap."

"IT WAS THE LONGEST NAP IN HISTORY!" wailed Finnegan.

"It was five days," said Herschel, but even he was wiping his whiskers.

Barnaby scooped Lars up and held him in his wings and didn't say anything for a while because he couldn't. Bjorn put one massive paw gently over both of them. Piper wrapped her flippers around as much of the group as she could reach.

The twins squeezed in at the bottom of the pile.

"We didn't mean to—" said Teddy.

"—start a group hug," said Tilda.

"But we're glad we did," they said together.

---

That afternoon, Lars stood at the bottom of the main slope with his little flag. He was bundled in three scarves and two hats and a blanket that Barnaby had insisted on. He could barely move. He looked like a very round, very fuzzy caterpillar.

Finnegan came down the slope, attempted something ambitious, and crashed magnificently into a snowdrift.

Lars raised his flag.

"GREAT CRASH!" he squeaked. "VERY DRAMATIC! TEN OUT OF TEN! YOU'RE MY HERO!"

And every animal on the mountain cheered.
