# Story 63: The Rooftop Sled Rescue
### *Inspired by Friends Lower a Man Through Roof — Mark 2*
**Characters:** Rory Reindeer, Finnegan Flipsworth  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Roofline Village  
**Theme:** Friends who carry each other

---

## Opening Wink

Sometimes the bravest thing you can do on a snowy night is not ride a mountain -- it's carry a friend up one.

## The Story

The trouble started with a patch of black ice and a very surprised reindeer.

Rory Reindeer had been trotting along the path to Herschel's Warming Hut -- the big one near Roofline Village with the stone chimney and the door that stuck -- when his left front hoof hit a slick of ice hidden under fresh powder. His legs went four different directions. He heard a *crack* that turned out to be his favorite ski pole snapping clean in half, and then he was on the ground, his right rear leg twisted under him at an angle that made his stomach flip.

"Ow," Rory said to nobody in particular. "Ow, ow, *ow*."

He tried to stand. His leg screamed at him. He tried again. Nope. He lay back in the snow, breathing hard, the cold seeping through his fur.

That was when Finnegan Flipsworth found him.

Finnegan had been doing what Finnegan usually did on a Thursday evening -- bouncing down the ridge trail at twice the recommended speed, his red-and-gold scarf streaming behind him like a tiny flame. He nearly rockhopped right over Rory before he noticed the lump in the snow.

"Rory?" Finnegan skidded to a stop, spraying powder everywhere. "What are you doing down there?"

"Interpretive dance," Rory said through gritted teeth. "What does it *look* like?"

Finnegan crouched. His wild eyebrows knitted together, which was the Finnegan version of deep concern. "Can you walk?"

"If I could walk, I wouldn't be making snow angels against my will."

"Right. Okay. Don't move." Finnegan stood up. "I'm getting help."

He was gone before Rory could point out that *not moving* was very much the only option.

Finnegan tore through Roofline Village like a small feathery comet. He found Piper Paddlefoot waxing her snowboard outside the gear shed. "Piper! Rory's hurt on the ridge trail. He can't walk. We have to get him to the warming hut."

Piper dropped her wax brick immediately. "How bad?"

"Bad enough that he made a joke, which means he's trying not to cry."

Piper nodded. She knew exactly what that meant. "Get Barnaby and Willa. I'll grab sleds."

Seven minutes later, the four of them stood in a circle on the ridge trail while Rory looked up at them with an expression that was equal parts grateful and embarrassed.

"You really don't have to --"

"Shush," said Willa Wobble, already inspecting his leg with her small, careful flippers. "It's not broken, but it's badly twisted. He needs to be warm, and he needs to stay off it."

"The warming hut is just down there." Barnaby pointed. Through the trees, they could see the amber glow of Herschel's big stone chimney, smoke puffing from the top like a friendly dragon breathing in its sleep.

"Perfect," Piper said. "Let's get him on the sled."

They eased Rory onto the widest toboggan, padding it with scarves and Barnaby's emergency blanket (Barnaby always carried an emergency blanket and an emergency sandwich; tonight both proved critical). The four friends pulled and pushed the sled down the slope, navigating between pine trees, Finnegan calling out directions that were only sometimes accurate.

"Left! No, my left! No, *actual* left! The one with the tree!"

"Finnegan, there are trees on BOTH sides," Piper said.

"Then go between them! Slide into it!"

They made it to the warming hut in one piece, which felt like a miracle. But when Piper grabbed the door handle and pulled, it didn't budge. She pulled harder. Nothing.

"It's frozen shut," she said.

Barnaby tried. He threw his whole chinstrap-penguin body weight against it. The door didn't even rattle.

"The ice storm last night," Willa said. "The whole frame must be sealed. It happens when the temperature drops that fast."

Rory, shivering on the sled, pulled the blanket tighter. "Guys, it's okay. You can just leave me out here. I'll be fine. Probably."

"We are absolutely not leaving you out here," Finnegan said, sounding offended by the very idea.

"The windows?" Piper tried every window. Frozen solid, every one.

The four friends stood in the snow, breath puffing in silver clouds, staring at the locked-up warming hut with its cheerful smoke and its glowing windows and its absolutely useless door. Inside, they could see the fireplace blazing, the blankets stacked on the bench, Herschel's cocoa pot sitting on the stove. Everything Rory needed, sealed behind ice.

Willa looked up. Way up.

"The roof hatch," she said.

Everyone followed her gaze. There, on the sloped roof of the warming hut, was the ventilation hatch -- a square panel that Herschel used in summer to let out heat. It was above the main room, directly over the big braided rug in front of the fireplace.

"That's... on the *roof*," Barnaby said.

"And Rory can't climb," Piper said.

"No," Willa agreed. "But we can. And we have sleds. And ropes."

The plan came together the way Frostpeak plans always did -- half engineering, half chaos, and entirely fueled by stubbornness.

Piper scaled the pine tree next to the hut and leaped to the roof, landing with the sure-footed confidence of a penguin who had never met a slope she didn't like. Finnegan followed, slipping twice and grabbing the chimney once, but making it. Willa, the lightest, scrambled up a snowdrift that had piled against the back wall and rolled herself onto the eaves. Barnaby stayed below with Rory.

On the roof, the three penguins pried open the hatch. Warm air rushed out, smelling of pine smoke and cinnamon, and Finnegan nearly fell in from sheer longing.

"Focus," Piper said, grabbing his scarf.

They lashed two sleds together with their scarves and Willa's emergency rope (Willa also carried emergency supplies, though hers tended toward rope, bandages, and a very tiny first-aid book). The result was a wobbly but surprisingly sturdy platform -- a sled stretcher.

"Barnaby!" Piper called down. "Get him on the stretcher!"

Below, Barnaby helped Rory roll onto the sled platform. "This is the most ridiculous thing that's ever happened to me," Rory said.

"The night is young," Barnaby replied cheerfully, tucking the blanket around him.

"Ready?" Piper called.

"Ready!" Barnaby called back.

The three penguins on the roof hauled on the rope. The sled stretcher rose off the ground, swaying gently, Rory gripping the sides with all four hooves. He rose past the window, where he caught a glimpse of the cocoa pot and thought, *almost there*. He rose past the eaves, where a little icicle broke off and bonked him on the nose.

"Sorry!" Finnegan squeaked.

Higher. Higher. The rope creaked. Piper's flippers burned. Willa wedged her tiny feet against the chimney base for leverage. Finnegan pulled with every ounce of rockhopper determination he had, making a face so scrunched it looked like he was trying to sneeze and lift a boulder at the same time.

The sled cleared the hatch.

"Swing him in!" Willa commanded.

They guided the stretcher through the opening, and Rory descended gently -- well, mostly gently; there was one bump against the hatch frame and a small *oof* -- into the warming hut. He landed on the braided rug in front of the fireplace with a soft *whump*.

Warmth. Glorious, bone-deep, toe-tingling warmth. Rory lay on the rug and felt the fire's heat soak into his fur, and his eyes went blurry with something that wasn't just from the smoke.

The three penguins dropped through the hatch after him, one by one -- Piper graceful, Willa careful, Finnegan in a tumbling heap that ended with him sitting in the kindling box. Barnaby came last, hauled up on the rope by the other three, arriving on the rug like a very dignified penguin-shaped package.

Willa wrapped Rory's leg with bandages from her kit. Barnaby put the cocoa pot on to heat. Piper stacked extra blankets around Rory until he looked like a reindeer burrito. Finnegan tried to help and mostly knocked things over, but his enthusiasm was its own kind of medicine.

When the cocoa was ready, they sat in a circle around the fire -- four penguins and one very loved reindeer -- and Rory held his mug in both hooves and said, very quietly, "You came through the *roof* for me."

"Well," Finnegan said, "the door was locked."

"You could have gone home. You could have said it was too hard."

Piper shook her head. "That was never an option."

"Not even slightly," Willa agreed.

Barnaby just handed him the emergency sandwich and said, "Eat. You look cold."

Rory took a bite. It was peanut butter and honey, slightly squished, and it was the best sandwich he had ever tasted in his entire life.

Outside, the snow fell soft and steady, filling in their tracks, covering the place where Rory had fallen, smoothing the world into something clean and new. Inside, the fire crackled, the cocoa steamed, and four friends sat with the one they'd carried, not because it was easy, not because anyone asked them to, but because that is simply what friends *do*.

"Finnegan?" Rory murmured, his eyes getting heavy.

"Yeah?"

"Next time, maybe check the door *before* you climb the roof."

Finnegan grinned. "Where's the fun in that?"

They laughed. The fire popped. The snow kept falling. And every creature in that warm little hut slept safe and sound, knowing that no door -- frozen or otherwise -- could ever stop the kind of friends who would climb a roof for you.
