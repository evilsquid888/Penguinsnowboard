# Story 62: The Picnic on Powder Hill
### *Inspired by Sermon on the Mount — Matthew 5–7*
**Characters:** Teacher Tern, Barnaby Beaksworth  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Powder Hill  
**Theme:** Blessed are the gentle

---

## Opening Wink

Nobody planned the most important picnic in Frostpeak history. It just sort of happened -- the way all the best things do -- on a Tuesday, with leftover sandwiches and a walrus who forgot his reading glasses.

## The Story

The morning started with a rumor.

"Somebody," Finnegan Flipsworth announced, bursting through the door of Herschel's Cocoa Lodge with snow in his eyebrows and his scarf on backwards, "is setting up a *picnic* on top of Powder Hill."

Barnaby Beaksworth looked up from his bowl of fish crackers. "A picnic? In winter? On a *hill*?"

"That's what I said!" Finnegan slapped the table for emphasis, accidentally launching a cracker into Willa Wobble's cocoa. It landed with a tiny *plink*.

Willa fished it out, inspected it, and ate it. "Who's setting it up?"

"Teacher Tern," Finnegan said, eyes wide. "And he's got *blankets*."

Now, Teacher Tern was a tall, graceful Arctic tern who had flown to every corner of the world and back again. He rarely raised his voice, but when he spoke, even the wind seemed to lean in and listen. He had arrived in Frostpeak Valley at the start of winter, and already half the valley adored him and the other half was deeply curious.

So when word spread that Teacher Tern was laying out quilted blankets and thermoses on the broad, flat summit of Powder Hill, creatures came from every ridge and hollow. Piper Paddlefoot snowboarded up the south face, carving long swooping turns and shouting "Slide into it!" to anyone she passed. Cleo Coldwater hiked the east trail with her sketchbook tucked under one flipper, pausing twice to draw the way sunlight turned the snow to diamonds. Bjorn Bigpaws trudged up the north slope with Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff riding on his enormous shoulders, the cubs pelting each other with tiny snowballs the whole way.

By the time the sun sat fat and golden above the peaks, Powder Hill was covered with animals. Penguins nestled in rows on the quilts. Polar bears sat in the back so everyone could see. A family of seals had flopped up the western slope and arranged themselves like glossy, whiskered bowling pins. Even Magnus Meltsworth showed up, grumbling that he was only there because someone mentioned free sandwiches.

Teacher Tern stood on a small rocky outcrop at the very top, the wind ruffling his feathers. Below him, the valley spread out in every direction -- frosted pines, frozen streams, the winding ribbon of the toboggan run, and far away, the warm amber glow of the lodge.

"I didn't bring a megaphone," he said, "so you'll have to scoot closer."

Everyone scooted. Magnus pretended he didn't.

Teacher Tern smiled. "I want to tell you who the happiest creatures in this valley really are."

"Ooh!" Teddy Powderpuff bounced. "Is it the ones with the most fish crackers?"

"Hush," Bjorn rumbled gently.

Teacher Tern tilted his head. "Happy are the ones who know they don't have everything figured out -- because they're still open to learning wonderful things."

Willa Wobble, small and steady in the second row, felt her heart glow like a lantern turned up.

"Happy are the ones who feel sad sometimes," Teacher Tern continued, "because they'll find comfort they never expected."

Cleo looked down at her sketchbook. She had drawn a picture of her grandmother that morning and cried a little. Now she felt a warm softness settle over that sadness, like a quilt laid gently on her shoulders.

"Happy are the gentle ones." Teacher Tern looked right at Bjorn, who blinked his great brown eyes. "They don't need to be the loudest. The whole valley already belongs to them."

"Ha!" Tilda whispered to Teddy. "He means Papa."

Bjorn's ears turned pink.

Teacher Tern went on. "Happy are those who get hungry and thirsty for what's *right* -- not just for lunch, Barnaby, though lunch is also important."

Barnaby, who had been unwrapping a sandwich, froze mid-bite. Everyone laughed -- including Barnaby, who laughed the loudest.

"Happy are the ones who show mercy," Teacher Tern said, his voice soft now, like snow falling on snow. "Because mercy will come back to them like an echo off the mountains."

Nora Snowmane, sitting at the edge of the crowd with her silver-white fur catching the light, nodded slowly. She had spent the morning helping a young fox who had gotten tangled in a ski-lift rope, speaking to him so gently that he stopped crying before she even got the knot undone.

"Happy are the peacemakers," Teacher Tern said. "Oh, the peacemakers. Do you know what they are?"

"Tired?" Magnus muttered.

Teacher Tern laughed -- a bright, clear sound like a bell over water. "They're family. The peacemakers are the ones who make the valley feel like home."

Finnegan, who had been fidgeting the whole time, suddenly went very still. He thought about the argument he'd broken up between two fox kits yesterday, and how they'd both ended up sharing a sled. He hadn't thought it was a big deal. Maybe it was.

The sun dropped lower. The sky turned the color of peach jam and lavender honey. Teacher Tern kept talking, and the animals kept listening, and something extraordinary happened that nobody could quite explain later: the hilltop felt *warm*. Not because of the thermoses or the quilts or even the sandwich Barnaby was generously passing around (he had brought eleven, which was generous even for Barnaby). The warmth came from the *listening*.

"Be like salt," Teacher Tern said. "You know what happens to a hill of snow with no salt? It's slippery and nobody can walk on it. But sprinkle a little salt and suddenly everyone has their footing. Be the salt. Be the thing that helps others stand."

"I want to be pepper," Teddy whispered.

"Shh," said Tilda, who was actually listening now.

"And be like a lantern," Teacher Tern continued. "Nobody lights a lantern and stuffs it under a toboggan. You set it up high so everyone can find their way home. Let your kindness *shine*."

Piper Paddlefoot looked out over the valley. From up here she could see the paths that wound between the trees, the little lodges with smoke curling from their chimneys, the halfpipe where she practiced her tricks. She thought about how every morning she woke up and decided what kind of day to have. She could be a lantern. She could *absolutely* be a lantern.

The stars came out one by one, like someone was poking tiny holes in a dark blue blanket. Herschel the Walrus, who had been quietly setting up a cocoa station behind a snowbank (because of course he had), began pouring steaming mugs and passing them forward, flipper over flipper, until every creature on Powder Hill held something warm.

"One last thing," Teacher Tern said. "Don't worry so much. Look at the snowbirds -- do they fret about tomorrow's weather? They sing anyway. Look at the pine trees -- do they stress about whether their needles match? They grow anyway. You are more loved than you can possibly imagine. So rest. Be kind. And let tomorrow take care of tomorrow."

Nobody spoke for a long moment.

Then Magnus Meltsworth -- grumpy, gruff, sandpaper-voiced Magnus -- cleared his throat and said, very quietly, "That was... not terrible."

From Magnus, that was a standing ovation.

The creatures of Frostpeak Valley sat together on Powder Hill as the aurora began its slow green dance across the sky. Barnaby shared the last sandwich. Cleo sketched the scene by lantern light. Willa leaned against Piper, and Piper leaned against Finnegan, and Finnegan leaned against Bjorn, who was sturdy enough for all of them.

"Teacher Tern?" Willa called softly.

"Yes?"

"Can we do this again next Tuesday?"

Teacher Tern folded his wings and smiled. "I'll bring more blankets."

And from somewhere in the back, Barnaby's voice drifted up: "I'll bring *twelve* sandwiches."

The whole hill laughed, soft and warm, as the stars kept watch and the snow kept falling and every creature in the valley felt, for that one perfect evening, exactly where they belonged.
