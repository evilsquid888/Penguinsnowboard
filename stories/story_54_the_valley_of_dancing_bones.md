# Story 54: The Valley of Dancing Bones
### *Inspired by Valley of Dry Bones — Ezekiel 37*
**Characters:** Zeke Finch, Cleo Coldwater  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Rattle Valley  
**Theme:** Hope for dry places

---

## Opening Wink

Frostpeak had one rule for evenings like this: cocoa first, courage second, and snacks always nearby.

## The Story

**Rattle Valley** had not been skied in four years.

It had been, once, one of the finest runs in the whole of Frostpeak — a long, sweeping descent through a narrow canyon carved by an ancient glacier, with excellent powder and a view at the bottom that made you stop and stand and breathe for a minute whether you meant to or not. Old creatures in the valley still talked about Rattle Valley the way you talk about a song you can't quite remember but know was beautiful.

Four years ago, a drainage problem had turned the lower run into ice flats — fast and dangerous — and the season after that, the course markers had been damaged in a late storm and not replaced, and the season after *that* the access path had been overgrown, and somewhere in all of those seasons the run had slipped from *deferred* into *forgotten*, which is a harder place to come back from.

**Zeke Finch** was a small green finch who had a very specific relationship with Rattle Valley: he had learned to ski there, on his very first winter in Frostpeak, before the problems started. His first successful carve had happened on the second curve down. He could still close his eyes and feel it — the shift of weight, the board responding, the canyon walls going past in a blur of white.

He thought about Rattle Valley the way you think about something you've lost that nobody else seems to miss.

The dream came on a Tuesday.

It was the kind of dream that's hard to shake — vivid and full of detail and somehow more real-feeling than most waking things. He was standing in Rattle Valley, but it was empty. Not abandoned-empty. Just *waiting*-empty, the way a cleared table is empty before a meal. And something in the dream said: *Speak to it. Tell it to come back.* And in the dream, Zeke did — he called out to the empty valley and the snow shifted and the markers reappeared and the course reformed under a clean sky.

He woke up at four in the morning convinced it was possible.

He called **Cleo Coldwater** at a reasonable hour and told her about the dream.

"Interesting," said Cleo. She got out her sketchbook.

"I know it sounds—"

"No, it doesn't," said Cleo. "Rattle Valley was wonderful. Everyone's just forgotten to do anything about it." She paused. "What would it actually take?"

They worked it out over a week of mornings at the Warming Hut. The drainage problem needed two days of work with the right equipment — Bjorn knew how. The course markers needed to be replaced — Barnaby had a contact in the supply valley. The access path was overgrown but passable; it needed clearing, which meant a volunteer work party. The whole thing was achievable. It was not even particularly expensive. It had just needed someone to make the list.

---

The work party happened on a Saturday, eleven creatures strong.

It was cold and a little bit physically demanding and Finnegan got his scarf caught in a drainage channel in a way that took twenty minutes to resolve and was extremely funny, and at one point Beryl the narwhal — who had insisted on coming despite the narrow path being challenging for a narwhal — became briefly stuck between two snow-laden pines and had to be guided through by Willa Wobble talking to her through the branches like an air traffic controller.

("Left. More left. Your other left, Beryl. No — actually, turn completely around — that's it — now back up slowly—")

By afternoon, the drainage was corrected, the markers were in place, the access path was clear.

Zeke stood at the top of Rattle Valley and looked down.

It looked like it always had. The canyon walls, the gentle first curve, the view of the valley at the bottom framed between the stone walls. Waiting.

He pushed off.

The first carve came at the second curve, the same place it always had, his weight shifting exactly right, the board reading the snow that had been sitting there for four years waiting to be useful again.

He came out at the bottom and stopped and looked back up.

Behind him, the rest of the work party came down in a loose, laughing wave — Bjorn first, then Willa, then Cleo with her sketchbook safely zipped away, then Finnegan extremely fast and loudly triumphant, then Beryl, who came down at a measured pace with complete dignity and arrived to enormous applause.

"Well," said Cleo, arriving beside Zeke and looking up at the canyon walls. "That's still a very good run."

"Yes," said Zeke.

"Who else needs to know about this?"

He thought about everyone in the valley who'd been skiing the same routes for four years without knowing Rattle Valley was waiting.

"Everyone," he said.

Cleo started writing it in her sketchbook. Zeke watched the light in the canyon go gold.

Places that seem finished sometimes just need someone to believe they're not.
