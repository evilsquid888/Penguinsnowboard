# Story 26: Joey's Many-Colored Mittens
### *Inspired by Joseph's Dreams — Genesis 37*
**Characters:** Cleo Coldwater, Barnaby Beaksworth  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Mittens Meadow  
**Theme:** Jealousy and purpose

---

## Opening Wink

Some mittens keep your flippers warm. Some mittens change your whole life. Tonight's story has a pair that does both.

## The Story

Joey Paddlefoot was the youngest of twelve penguin siblings, and the only one who could do a triple-spin on a snowboard without spilling his cocoa. This impressed exactly nobody in his family.

"Show-off," muttered his brother Reggie, adjusting his plain gray mittens.

"Teacher's pet," grumbled his sister Greta, zipping up her plain gray scarf.

You see, in the Paddlefoot family, everybody wore plain gray winter gear. That was the tradition. Plain gray mittens, plain gray scarves, plain gray everything. It was very... gray.

But one frosty morning, their grandmother Nana Paddlefoot waddled into the burrow carrying a package wrapped in silver birch bark. She set it down right in front of Joey and winked.

"Open it, little one. I've been knitting all autumn."

Joey tore open the paper and gasped. Inside lay the most spectacular pair of mittens Frostpeak Valley had ever seen. They were striped in every color of the aurora borealis -- violet and emerald and gold and rose and a blue so deep it looked like a piece of the midnight sky had been stitched right in. Tiny snowflake buttons fastened each cuff, and when Joey slipped them on, the wool was so warm it felt like holding a mug of Herschel's famous cocoa.

"Nana!" Joey whispered. "They're beautiful!"

"They're ridiculous," said Reggie.

All eleven siblings stared. Their plain gray mittens had never felt so... plain.

"Why does *he* get the fancy ones?" asked Greta, her beak pinched tight.

Nana Paddlefoot just smiled her mysterious grandmother smile and waddled back out into the snow.

From that day on, Joey wore his many-colored mittens everywhere -- to the slopes, to the cocoa lodge, to Mittens Meadow where the penguins practiced their tricks. And the mittens seemed to bring him luck. He landed jumps he'd never landed before. He carved turns so smooth that Cleo Coldwater painted a watercolor of him mid-flight, calling it *Rainbow on the Ridge*.

"Did you see my backflip today?" Joey asked at dinner, wiggling his colorful fingers.

"We saw," said Reggie flatly.

"Hard to miss," added another brother, "what with those mittens glowing like a lighthouse."

The siblings whispered together in corners. They rolled their eyes when Joey passed. One afternoon, when Joey was practicing on the big slope above Mittens Meadow, he overheard Greta say, "I wish those silly mittens would just disappear."

That stung worse than a face full of ice spray.

But the mittens weren't the only unusual thing about Joey. He also had dreams -- wild, vivid, strange dreams that felt as real as snow under his flippers. One night he dreamed that twelve snowboards stood in a circle, and eleven of them bowed toward his board, which was painted in rainbow stripes.

He made the terrible mistake of telling his siblings about it at breakfast.

"So you think our snowboards are going to *bow* to yours?" Reggie's voice could have frozen the cocoa pot.

"I didn't say that! The dream said that! I don't control my dreams!"

"Next he'll dream he's king of the valley," Greta snorted.

After that, the siblings stopped talking to Joey almost entirely. They'd pick teams for slope races and leave him standing alone at the bottom, his many-colored mittens bright against the gray afternoon. Barnaby Beaksworth noticed one day and waddled over with a bag of fish crackers.

"You look like you could use a snack and a friend," Barnaby said, settling his round belly into the snow beside Joey.

"My brothers and sisters hate me," Joey said quietly.

"They don't hate you," Barnaby said, crunching a cracker thoughtfully. "They just haven't figured out yet that your sparkle doesn't take away from theirs. Here, have a cracker. Cheddar flavor. Fixes everything temporarily."

Joey laughed, but his heart still felt heavy as a snow-packed boot.

Then came the night of the Great Frost. Temperatures plummeted so fast that the cocoa in Herschel's lodge froze solid in the mugs. The wind howled like a confused walrus. All the trails were buried under six feet of fresh powder, and nobody could find the path markers anywhere.

"We're going to miss the Valley Winter Festival!" Cleo cried, her macaroni crest drooping with worry. "All the decorations, the lights, the hot chocolate fountain -- everything's set up at Mittens Meadow, but nobody can find the way there in this whiteout!"

Bjorn Bigpaws tried to lead a search party, but even his massive paws sank into drifts up to his belly. Willa Wobble studied her maps, but the snow had changed every landmark. Finnegan Flipsworth attempted three different routes and ended up back where he started each time, once with a pinecone stuck to his tail.

"This is hopeless!" Finnegan declared, pulling the pinecone free with a *pop*.

That night, Joey had another dream. In this dream, he stood at the top of Snowcap Ridge and looked down. He saw the valley spread below like a white quilt, and in his dream, a path glowed beneath the snow -- a winding trail of warm light, like veins of gold under ice. The path ran from the lodge all the way to Mittens Meadow, following the old creek bed where the ice was thinnest and the snow was shallowest.

Joey sat up in bed, his many-colored mittens still on his flippers. (He'd been too cold to take them off.)

"The old creek bed," he whispered. "That's the way."

He ran to tell his siblings. For a moment, when he burst into their room, he saw eleven pairs of eyes look at him with that familiar annoyance. *Here comes the dreamer again.*

But Joey spoke fast. "I know how to get to Mittens Meadow. The old creek bed -- the snow is shallow there because the water underneath keeps it thin. We can dig down just a little and follow it all the way."

Silence.

"That's actually... not a terrible idea," Reggie said slowly.

"The creek bed does run straight to the meadow," Greta admitted.

By dawn, all twelve Paddlefoot siblings plus half the valley were stomping along the creek bed trail. Joey led the way, his rainbow mittens like a beacon in the pale morning light. Barnaby brought snacks for the journey. Cleo sketched the scene as she walked. Willa confirmed the route with her compass. And Bjorn carried Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff on his shoulders because the snow was still too deep for little cubs.

When they finally burst through the last snowbank and saw Mittens Meadow sparkling with festival lights and icicle garlands, the whole valley cheered. The hot chocolate fountain was still working. The decorations swayed in the gentle breeze. It was perfect.

Reggie waddled up to Joey and cleared his throat. "So. Your dream saved the festival."

"Our legs saved the festival," Joey said. "I just had the map."

"Well." Reggie looked down at his plain gray mittens, then back at Joey's rainbow ones. "Maybe having a dreamer in the family isn't so bad."

Greta appeared on Joey's other side. "Maybe we were jealous because your mittens were so bright and ours felt so dull. But you never actually made us feel dull. We did that to ourselves."

"You know," Joey said, pulling off one many-colored mitten and holding it out, "Nana knitted extra yarn. I bet she'd make everyone a pair. Not the same as mine -- yours should be YOUR colors. Greta, you'd look amazing in silver and ice-blue."

Greta blinked. Then she smiled -- really smiled -- for the first time in weeks.

That evening, the Winter Festival was the best Frostpeak Valley had ever seen. Twelve Paddlefoot penguins rode the slopes together, and if you squinted in the twilight, you could see Joey's many-colored mittens flashing at the front of the pack, leading the way down a trail lit by lanterns and laughter.

Herschel leaned on his cocoa counter and watched them swoosh past. "Funny thing about bright colors," the old walrus rumbled. "They don't use up the light. They make more of it."

Barnaby nodded sagely and reached for another fish cracker. "That's beautiful, Herschel. Also, is the cocoa ready? I'm starving."

Herschel poured him a triple-size mug, extra marshmallows, and the two of them watched the aurora paint the sky in colors that matched Joey's mittens almost exactly.
