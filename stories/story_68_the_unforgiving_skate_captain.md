# Story 68: The Unforgiving Skate Captain
### *Inspired by Unforgiving Servant — Matthew 18*
**Characters:** Cap Captainybara, Bjorn Bigpaws  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Rink Arena  
**Theme:** Receive mercy, give mercy

---

## Opening Wink

Have you ever been forgiven for something so big that your tummy did a somersault of relief? Well, hold onto that feeling, because tonight somebody forgets to pass it along.

## The Story

The morning Piper Paddlefoot crashed into the Rink Arena scoreboard was not her finest moment.

It happened like this: she had been practicing a new trick called the Blizzard Barrel Roll, which involved spinning three times while holding a fish cracker between her flippers. The spin went beautifully. The landing did not. She plowed straight into the big wooden board where Bjorn Bigpaws kept careful track of who owed what around the valley -- borrowed equipment, shared supplies, cocoa tabs, that sort of thing.

The scoreboard exploded into a cloud of splinters and chalk dust. Numbers and tallies scattered across the ice like confetti at a party nobody wanted.

"Oh no," Piper whispered, staring at the wreckage. "Oh no, oh no, oh *no*."

Bjorn Bigpaws lumbered over, his massive frame casting a shadow the size of a small shed. He looked at the shattered scoreboard. He looked at Piper. He looked at the single fish cracker still wedged between her flippers.

"Piper," he said slowly, in that deep rumble that made the icicles on the arena roof tremble. "That board had every tally in the valley on it. Every borrowed ski pole. Every cocoa tab. Every single snowshoe rental going back three winters."

Piper's beak opened and closed like a goldfish. "I... I could glue it?"

"It's sawdust, Piper."

"Really good glue?"

Bjorn sat down heavily on the ice, which groaned under him. For a long moment he said nothing. The whole arena went quiet. Willa Wobble peeked out from behind the cocoa stand, her tiny blue body trembling. Barnaby Beaksworth froze mid-bite on a sardine sandwich. Even Finnegan Flipsworth stopped bouncing, which was practically a medical event.

Then Bjorn did something nobody expected. He laughed. It started as a low rumble, like distant thunder rolling over Powderhorn Peak, and then it grew until it shook loose a little avalanche of snow from the rafters that plopped directly onto Finnegan's head.

"You know what, Piper?" Bjorn said, wiping a tear from his big brown eye. "I've been meaning to redo that system anyway. Consider it forgotten."

"Forgotten?" Piper blinked. "But -- the records -- all those debts --"

"Gone." Bjorn waved a massive paw. "Every single one. Clean ice. Fresh start for everybody."

The arena erupted. Penguins cheered. The seals in the balcony clapped their flippers so fast they sounded like a rainstorm. Barnaby did a little dance that sent sardine bits flying. It was the most joyful mess the Rink Arena had seen in years.

Piper floated through the rest of the morning on a cloud of pure gratitude. She helped Cleo Coldwater string new lanterns around the rink. She sang while she swept. She even let Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff borrow her best snowboard without a single grumble, and those cubs had a track record of returning things with mysterious dents and jam stains.

By afternoon, Piper was captaining the afternoon skating drills, whistle around her neck, clipboard in her flipper, feeling like the luckiest penguin in the entire valley.

That was when Finnegan Flipsworth came skidding up to her, spraying ice everywhere.

"Piper! Piper! I have a small, tiny, very miniature confession!" Finnegan's yellow eyebrow tufts were vibrating with nervous energy.

"What is it, Finn?"

"So, you know that fish cracker you lent me last Tuesday?"

Piper's expression shifted. "My *special* fish cracker? The smoked salmon one with the little ridge on top?"

"That's the one! Well, here's the funny part --" Finnegan tried a smile that did not land. "I maybe possibly ate it."

Piper stared at him. "You *ate* my fish cracker?"

"In my defense, I was very hungry, and it was right there in my pocket, and it smelled *so good*, Piper, you don't even understand --"

"That was imported from Glacier Bay!" Piper's voice rose until it bounced off the arena walls. "That cracker cost three whole snowflake coins!"

"I know, I know, and I'm super sorry, and I was thinking maybe I could pay you back next --"

"No!" Piper planted her flippers on her hips. "You owe me, Finnegan Flipsworth, and I want my three snowflake coins by tomorrow morning, or you're off the skating team!"

Finnegan's beak dropped open. "Off the -- Piper, I don't *have* three snowflake coins! I spent my last one on a mitten with a hole in it!"

"Not my problem!" Piper turned her back and blew her whistle so hard that a nearby seal fell off his bench.

Willa Wobble had been watching the whole thing from her usual spot near the boards -- low to the ground, easy to overlook, impossible to fool. She waddled over to Piper with the careful steadiness that made her the best skater on rough ice in the entire valley.

"Piper," Willa said softly. "Can I ask you something?"

"Make it quick, Willa. I'm running drills."

"How big was the scoreboard you destroyed this morning?"

Piper blinked. "What?"

"The scoreboard. With three winters of tallies. All those debts Bjorn forgave. How many snowflake coins do you think that was worth?"

Piper opened her beak, then closed it. Opened it again. A cold feeling crept through her that had nothing to do with the ice.

"Hundreds," she said quietly. "Maybe thousands."

"And what did Bjorn say when you broke it?"

Piper's flippers slowly lowered from her hips. "He said... consider it forgotten."

"Mm-hmm." Willa adjusted her tiny scarf. "And how many snowflake coins does Finnegan owe you?"

"Three," Piper whispered.

Willa didn't say anything else. She didn't need to. She just gave Piper that look -- the one that was gentle and knowing and made you feel like a lantern had been turned on inside your chest, illuminating a messy corner you'd been ignoring.

Piper stood very still for a long moment. The sounds of the arena swirled around her: blades on ice, laughter, the distant whistle of wind through the rafters. She thought about the look on Bjorn's face that morning -- not angry, not grudging, just... warm. Like forgiveness was the easiest thing in the world.

And here she was, hoarding three snowflake coins' worth of grudge against her friend.

"Finnegan!" she called out, her voice cracking slightly. "FINN! Get back here!"

Finnegan poked his head around a pillar, looking like a rockhopper penguin who expected to be pelted with snowballs.

"Yeah?" he squeaked.

Piper skated over to him, and when she got close, she did something that surprised them both. She hugged him. It was an awkward, flipper-tangling, slightly off-balance penguin hug, but it was real.

"Forget the fish cracker," she said. "It's gone. Clean ice."

"Really?" Finnegan's eyes went huge. "You mean it?"

"I mean it. And I'm sorry I yelled. That was rotten of me."

Finnegan vibrated with happiness so intensely that he accidentally did a small backflip, which knocked over a bucket of spare pucks, which startled a seal, which set off a chain reaction of honking and splashing that took ten minutes to sort out. Classic Finnegan.

From across the rink, Bjorn Bigpaws watched the whole scene unfold. He caught Willa's eye and gave a slow nod, the kind that said more than a hundred words.

Nora Snowmane appeared beside him, two steaming mugs of Herschel's cocoa in her paws. "She figured it out," Nora said.

"She did," Bjorn agreed. "Took her a minute."

"Takes all of us a minute, doesn't it?"

Bjorn accepted his cocoa with a low chuckle. "Every single time."

That evening, as the aurora painted ribbons of green and purple across the sky, the whole gang gathered at the edge of the rink. Herschel the Walrus rolled out his cocoa cart, and the steam rose up like little prayers dissolving into the cold air. Piper sat between Finnegan and Willa, their scarves overlapping, their laughter easy and light.

"You know what I think?" Barnaby said, dunking a fish cracker into his cocoa (which everyone agreed was disgusting but nobody stopped him from doing). "I think the best thing about being forgiven is that it makes you want to forgive everybody else. Like it's contagious."

"Like the sniffles," Finnegan offered helpfully.

"Sure, Finn. Like the sniffles. But nicer."

Magnus Meltsworth, who had been pretending not to listen from three benches away, grumbled something that sounded suspiciously like "That's actually quite beautiful" before loudly blowing his nose into a handkerchief the size of a pillowcase.

Piper looked up at the aurora and smiled. Clean ice. Fresh start. The kind of grace you could pass on like a warm mug on a cold night.

And somewhere in the distance, a seal clapped.
