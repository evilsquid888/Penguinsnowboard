# Story 86: Love on the Chairlift
### *Inspired by Love Is Patient — 1 Corinthians 13*
**Characters:** Cori Cougar, Barnaby Beaksworth  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Chairlift Line  
**Theme:** Patient love

---

## Opening Wink

The chairlift at Frostpeak Valley was the slowest chairlift in the entire frozen world, and nobody had ever figured out whether that was a problem or a gift.

## The Story

The morning started with a line. Not a short line, either. The kind of line that wraps around the cocoa stand, past the boot-drying rack, through Herschel's front porch, and almost back to where it started.

"How long have we been standing here?" Finnegan Flipsworth asked, bouncing from foot to foot. His rockhopper crest feathers were already frosted with impatience.

"Four minutes," said Willa Wobble, checking the little sundial she kept in her coat pocket.

"That's basically forever," Finnegan groaned.

The Frostpeak Chairlift was legendary for two reasons. First, it carried riders up to the very tip-top of Powder Crown Peak, where the snow was so fine it felt like riding through a cloud. Second, it moved at the speed of a sleepy caterpillar pushing a marble uphill. Each ride took a full twenty-two minutes, and there was absolutely no way to rush it.

Today, Bjorn Bigpaws had posted a sign at the base station: **PAIRS RIDE DAY -- Choose your chairlift partner wisely. You'll be sitting together for a while.**

Piper Paddlefoot arrived at a full waddle-sprint, her snowboard tucked under one flipper and a cinnamon roll clamped in her beak. "Slide into it!" she mumbled through the pastry. "Who wants to ride with me?"

"I will," said Barnaby Beaksworth, adjusting the strap on his snack satchel. "I packed enough fish crackers for both of us. And emergency gummy worms. And a cheese sandwich, just in case."

"In case of what?" Piper asked.

"In case of cheese emergency," Barnaby said seriously.

They loaded onto the first chair, and the lift groaned to life with a sound like a yawning walrus. The cable pulled them upward at its usual glacial pace. Below, the valley spread out like a snow globe somebody had gently shaken — flakes drifting, pine trees frosted white, and tiny figures milling about the lodge.

"So," Piper said, kicking her feet. "Twenty-two minutes."

"Twenty-two minutes," Barnaby agreed.

For about ninety seconds, neither of them said anything. Piper drummed her flippers on the safety bar. Barnaby unwrapped a fish cracker and ate it thoughtfully.

"This is boring," Piper announced.

"Give it a second," Barnaby said.

"I gave it ninety seconds. That's ninety more than I planned."

Behind them, the next chair carried Cleo Coldwater and Magnus Meltsworth. Cleo had brought her sketchbook and was already drawing the way the light hit the icicles on Thistleback Ridge. Magnus sat with his arms folded, grumbling about the cold, even though he was a polar bear and the cold was literally his entire thing.

"Why is this lift so slow?" Magnus muttered.

"Because fast lifts don't give you time to notice things," Cleo said, not looking up from her sketch. "Look — the shadows on that snowbank are lavender. Actual lavender."

"I don't care about lavender shadows."

"You will in about ten minutes," Cleo said calmly.

Three chairs back, Willa Wobble sat beside Finnegan, who was already fidgeting so hard the chair was swaying.

"Finnegan," Willa said. "If you bounce us off this lift, we will land in a snowdrift, and I am too small for that."

"Sorry, sorry." He gripped the bar. "It's just — I could be *doing* things. Racing. Jumping. Building a snow fort. Inventing a new trick called the Finnegan Flip-and-Tumble."

"You could also be sitting still and being present," Willa suggested.

"Is that a trick?"

"It might be the hardest one."

Up ahead, Piper had dropped her mitten. It tumbled down, down, down through the cold air and landed on a pine branch far below, looking very small and very alone.

"My mitten!" Piper wailed. "My favorite mitten! The one with the little fish embroidered on the thumb!"

"Here," Barnaby said. He pulled off one of his own mittens — a lumpy, hand-knitted orange thing — and held it out.

"But then your hand will be cold," Piper said.

"I have fish crackers to keep my other hand busy," Barnaby said. "Busy hands don't get cold. That's science."

It was absolutely not science, but Piper took the mitten anyway, and something about the gesture made the slow ride feel a little less slow.

Minute eight. Piper started telling Barnaby about the time she tried to snowboard down Avalanche Alley blindfolded, and Barnaby laughed so hard a fish cracker came out of his nose, which made Piper laugh so hard she snorted, which startled a family of snow finches out of a nearby tree, which made them both laugh even harder.

Minute twelve. Behind them, something unexpected was happening. Cleo had finished her icicle sketch and started a new one — a portrait of Magnus, looking dignified and grumpy and secretly a little bit pleased. She held it up.

"That's me?" Magnus said.

"That's you."

He studied it for a long time. "You made my fur look... nice."

"Your fur *is* nice. You just forget because you're too busy complaining."

Magnus was quiet for almost a full minute, which was a personal record. Then he said, very softly, "Could you draw the lavender shadows too? I want to see them."

Minute fifteen. Willa and Finnegan had invented a game. Willa would point to something in the valley below — a snow-covered rooftop, a frozen creek, a cluster of red winterberries — and Finnegan would make up a ridiculous story about it.

"That rock right there," Willa said, pointing. "Go."

"That's actually a sleeping giant's elbow," Finnegan said immediately. "He's been napping since the last ice age. When he wakes up, he's going to ask for pancakes and everyone in the valley is going to pretend they didn't eat them all."

Willa giggled — a rare, bright sound like a tiny bell. "You're very good at this."

"I'm good at making stuff up," Finnegan said. "It's one of the few things sitting still is good for."

"Maybe sitting still is good for more than you think," Willa said.

Minute eighteen. Piper realized she'd stopped kicking her feet. She was leaning against the back of the chair, watching the valley turn golden as the afternoon light shifted. The snow on the far ridges glowed like amber. The whole world felt hushed and slow and somehow exactly right.

"Hey Barnaby?" she said.

"Yeah?"

"Thanks for the mitten."

"Thanks for the story about the blindfold."

"Barnaby?"

"Yeah?"

"This is actually... really nice. Just sitting here."

Barnaby smiled. "I know. That's the thing about slow rides. They give you room for the good stuff. Patience. Kindness. Cheese sandwiches."

"You haven't even eaten the cheese sandwich."

"I'm saving it. That's also patience."

Below them, Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff were keeping score on who could wave at the most riders from the ground, and they'd somehow turned it into a full competitive sport with judges and everything. Nora Snowmane stood nearby, shaking her head with a warm smile.

"Love doesn't keep score!" she called up to the chairlift riders.

"Tell that to Teddy!" Tilda shouted back. "He says he's winning!"

"Am not!" Teddy yelled. "Am too! Wait — which answer gets me more points?"

At the top of the lift, Herschel the Walrus waited with a steaming cart of cocoa, his magnificent mustache frosted with whipped cream. As each pair unloaded, he handed them cups and watched their faces.

Piper and Barnaby came off first, pink-cheeked and grinning.

"How was the ride?" Herschel asked.

"Too short," Piper said, and meant it.

Magnus and Cleo came off next. Magnus was holding the portrait carefully in both paws, like it was made of glass.

"She drew my fur nice," he told Herschel, as if this explained everything.

Finnegan and Willa came off last. Finnegan wasn't bouncing. He was walking slowly, looking around at the valley below with wide eyes, as if seeing it for the first time.

"You alright, Finnegan?" Herschel asked.

"I think so," Finnegan said. "I think I just learned something, but I'm not totally sure what."

Willa patted his leg — the highest point she could reach. "You learned that love is patient," she said. "And patience is a kind of love."

They all sat in a circle at the summit, watching the aurora begin its slow dance across the sky — green and violet and shimmering white. Nobody was in a rush. The cocoa was warm. The snow was soft. Barnaby still hadn't eaten his cheese sandwich, and he offered half to Piper, who took it without a word.

Below them, the chairlift continued its ancient, creaking, beautiful climb, carrying the next group of friends upward into the fading golden light.

"Same time tomorrow?" Piper asked.

"Same time tomorrow," everyone said.

And the slowest chairlift in the frozen world kept right on going, because some things are worth taking your time for.
