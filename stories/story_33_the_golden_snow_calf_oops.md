# Story 33: The Golden Snow-Calf Oops
### *Inspired by The Golden Calf — Exodus 32*
**Characters:** Glint Magpie, Cleo Coldwater  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Festival Plaza  
**Theme:** Don't trade truth for glitter

---

## Opening Wink

Glitter is lovely on a craft project. Glitter is NOT lovely when it replaces your whole personality. Tonight, somebody in Frostpeak Valley learns the difference the sparkly way.

## The Story

Bjorn Bigpaws had only been gone for two days.

Two days. Forty-eight hours. That's barely enough time to finish a decent nap, and polar bears know a thing or two about decent naps. He'd trekked up to Glacier Peak for the annual Ice Council meeting -- a very boring gathering of very large animals who discussed things like avalanche routes and where to put the new cocoa pipeline. He'd left clear instructions pinned to the warming lodge door:

*"Practice your turns. Keep the trails tidy. I'll be home by Thursday. -- Bjorn"*

And underneath, in smaller letters: *"Please don't do anything weird."*

They did something weird.

It started innocently enough. Cleo Coldwater was sketching in Festival Plaza on Tuesday morning, her macaroni crest feathers ruffling in the breeze, when she noticed how the early sunlight hit a particular snowdrift and turned it blazing gold.

"Oh!" she breathed. "That's the most beautiful thing I've ever seen. I have to sculpt it."

Now, Cleo's sculptures were always wonderful -- delicate ice rabbits, tiny snow owls, once an entire miniature version of the valley that fit on a tree stump. But this time, the golden light inspired her to go big. Really big.

She started shaping the snowdrift into a calf -- a round, jolly, calf-shaped snow sculpture with a wide grin and stubby legs. She packed the snow tight and smooth, and when the sun caught it just right, the whole thing glowed like it was made of actual gold.

"Ooooh," said Finnegan Flipsworth, skidding to a halt. "What's THAT?"

"Just a sculpture," Cleo said, tongue poking out as she smoothed the calf's ear.

"It's AMAZING. It's the most amazing thing in the valley. Everyone should see this." Before Cleo could protest, Finnegan had rocketed off to spread the word with the subtlety of a foghorn in a library.

Within an hour, half the valley had gathered in Festival Plaza, staring at the golden snow-calf like it was the eighth wonder of the world. The morning light kept doing its trick, making the sculpture shimmer and glow, and everyone's eyes went wide and dreamy.

"We should decorate it!" Teddy Powderpuff announced.

"We should build a stage around it!" Tilda added.

"We should cancel ski practice and just LOOK at it!" said a seal pup, which was a suspicious suggestion from an animal who never liked ski practice anyway.

Piper Paddlefoot frowned. "Wait, cancel practice? Bjorn left us a schedule--"

"Oh, the schedule can wait," Finnegan said, waving a flipper. "When was the last time we had something this sparkly? This is better than the schedule. This is ART."

Cleo's cheeks went pink under her feathers. "It's really just packed snow and good lighting, you guys--"

But nobody was listening to Cleo anymore. They were too busy draping the snow-calf with garlands of icicles and pine boughs. Barnaby Beaksworth set up a snack station next to it ("The Golden Calf Cafe -- Fish Crackers and Fizzy Snowmelt!"). Someone hung lanterns from its horns. Teddy and Tilda strung a banner that read "ALL HAIL THE SHINY COW" in wobbly letters.

The trails sat empty. The practice flags drooped in the wind, unvisited. The signposts along Summit Switchbacks gathered frost because nobody was riding past them to read the words.

Willa Wobble watched all of this from behind Herschel's cocoa cart, her small blue body tucked into a scarf so big it looked like the scarf was wearing her.

"Herschel," she said quietly, "this doesn't feel right."

The old walrus polished a cocoa mug with his flipper, his whiskers twitching thoughtfully. "What part, little one?"

"All of it. The snow-calf is pretty, but everyone's acting like it's... important. Like, more important than everything else. Nobody's done their trail chores. Nobody's checked the weather flags. Finnegan told me the sculpture is 'basically in charge now,' which doesn't even make sense because it's SNOW."

Herschel chuckled, a low rumble like a friendly earthquake. "Funny thing about shiny objects, Willa. They're easy to stare at, but they don't stare back. They don't know your name. They can't help you up when you fall."

Willa scrunched her beak. "So what do we do?"

"Sometimes," Herschel said, handing her a cocoa with extra marshmallows, "the bravest thing a small penguin can do is say what everybody else is too dazzled to notice."

That afternoon, the celebration around the snow-calf reached peak silliness. Finnegan had organized a parade. The seal pups were doing synchronized swimming moves on the ice in front of it. Magnus Meltsworth had been convinced to wear a sparkly scarf, which he clearly hated but tolerated because Tilda had asked with her biggest eyes.

And then Willa Wobble climbed up onto Herschel's cocoa cart, stood on her tippy-toes, and said, in her small but steady voice:

"Excuse me. EXCUSE ME."

Nobody heard her. The parade was too loud.

Willa took a deep breath and did something she had never done before. She put two flippers in her beak and whistled -- a piercing, spectacular, ear-splitting whistle that bounced off every building in Festival Plaza and startled a family of snow owls three ridges away.

Everyone froze.

"That sculpture," Willa said, her voice wobbling only a tiny bit, "is lovely. Cleo made something beautiful, and we should be proud of her. But we've been staring at it for two days and ignoring everything that actually matters. The trails need grooming. The weather flags haven't been checked. Nobody's refilled the first-aid kits. And we told Bjorn we'd practice our turns."

Silence. The kind of silence that tastes like the moment before you admit your little sister was right.

Piper's eyes went wide. "Oh no. The weather flags. I was supposed to check those yesterday."

"I forgot about the first-aid kits completely," Barnaby said, his snack bag drooping in his flipper.

Finnegan looked at the snow-calf, then at Willa, then back at the snow-calf. "But it's so... golden."

"It's snow," Willa said gently. "It'll melt. The things Bjorn asked us to take care of? Those matter longer than glitter."

Cleo stepped forward, and to everyone's surprise, she was smiling. "Willa's right. I made the sculpture for fun, not for... whatever this turned into. It's just frozen water and good angles. It was never supposed to replace real things."

She picked up her sketchbook and pencils. "Come on. I'll draw it from memory later. Let's go do our actual jobs."

And just like that, the spell broke.

The garlands came down. The lanterns went back to the lodge. Barnaby packed up the Golden Calf Cafe (though he kept the name for future snack-station use, "because it's catchy"). Everyone scattered to their tasks -- Piper raced to check the weather flags, Magnus grumbled his way to the first-aid shed, and Finnegan actually groomed a trail for the first time in his life, though he did it backwards and had to be turned around twice.

By the time Bjorn trudged home on Thursday evening, his big shoulders dusted with summit snow, the valley was humming along exactly as he'd hoped. Trails groomed. Flags flying. Cocoa steaming.

He paused at Festival Plaza, where a slightly lopsided, undecorated snow-calf sat melting gently in the last light of the day. One horn had drooped. Its grin looked goofy now instead of golden.

"What's this?" he asked.

"A long story," Willa said, tugging his paw toward the warming lodge.

"A silly story," Cleo added.

"A VERY sparkly story," Finnegan said, "but we figured out the ending."

Bjorn looked at the melting calf, then at his friends, then at the perfectly groomed trails glowing under the rising aurora. He didn't ask for details. He didn't need to. Whatever had happened, they'd found their way back.

"Cocoa?" Herschel offered from his cart.

"Please," said Bjorn.

They sat together as the stars came out, one by one, and the snow-calf slowly, quietly returned to being exactly what it always was -- just snow. Beautiful, ordinary, melting snow.

And somewhere in the lodge, Willa fell asleep first, her small blue body curled in her enormous scarf, dreaming of things that lasted longer than glitter -- like friendship, and cocoa, and the sound of Bjorn's big boots coming home.
