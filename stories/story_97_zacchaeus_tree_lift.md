# Story 97: Zacchaeus and the Tree Lift
### *Inspired by Zacchaeus — Luke 19*
**Characters:** Zack Zebra, Piper Paddlefoot  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Pine Lift  
**Theme:** Change of heart

---

## Opening Wink

If moonbeams had mittens, this would be the night they came out to clap for the valley. And if a certain small penguin had a ladder, well -- he wouldn't have needed the tree.

## The Story

Nobody in Frostpeak Valley liked **Zack Shortstack**.

That was the honest truth, and it sat in Zack's belly like a cold rock every single morning. He was a chinstrap penguin, but shorter than most -- barely taller than Willa Wobble, and Willa could fit inside a cocoa mug. He had beady little eyes that darted around nervously and a voice that came out louder than he meant it to, which made everything he said sound like an accusation even when he was just asking for the time.

But the real reason nobody liked Zack wasn't his size or his voice.

It was the tolls.

Zack ran the Pine Lift -- the rickety old chairlift that carried skiers and snowboarders up to Timberline Ridge. And somewhere along the way, Zack had started charging *extra*. A fish cracker here, a marshmallow there, an "equipment inspection fee" that he'd invented on a Tuesday afternoon because he was bored. If you wanted the good chair -- the one with the cushion and the wind guard -- that was two fish crackers AND a marshmallow. Nobody could prove the prices were unfair because Zack kept changing them, scribbling new numbers on a little chalkboard and then erasing them before anyone could complain.

"That said THREE marshmallows yesterday!" Finnegan Flipsworth protested one morning, his rockhopper crest standing straight up with outrage.

"It says five today," said Zack, not looking up from his chalkboard. "Inflation."

"You don't even know what inflation means!"

"It means I get more marshmallows," said Zack.

He had a whole stash behind the lift station -- towers of fish crackers, mountains of marshmallows, bags of candied snowberries. More snacks than any one penguin could eat in a lifetime. And yet every evening, Zack sat alone in his little booth, counting his hoard, and felt absolutely hollow inside.

He told himself it didn't matter.

It mattered.

---

One crisp winter morning, a rumor swept through Frostpeak Valley like wind through pine needles: **Piper Paddlefoot** was bringing a very special visitor to the valley. A legendary snowboarder from beyond the Eastern Peaks -- someone called the Rider, who had been traveling from valley to valley all winter, doing the most extraordinary things. The Rider had supposedly carved a path through a blizzard that nobody else could navigate. The Rider had taught a group of lost seal pups to find their way home by following the stars reflected in ice. Everywhere the Rider went, things got *better*.

"The Rider is coming through the main avenue at noon!" Barnaby Beaksworth announced, waddling through the village square with his megaphone (which was actually a rolled-up newspaper, but Barnaby committed to it). "Everyone to the avenue! This is going to be AMAZING!"

Zack heard the commotion from his lift booth. He pressed his beak against the frosty window. Animals were pouring toward the main avenue -- polar bears lumbering with their cubs on their shoulders, penguins sliding on their bellies to get there faster, even Captain Prism surfacing in the canal and bellowing, "MAKE WAY FOR THE ARRIVAL OF LEGEND! ALSO, SOMEONE TOSS ME A FISH CRACKER!"

Something flickered in Zack's chest. He wanted to see the Rider. He *needed* to see the Rider, though he couldn't have explained why. It was the same ache he felt every night sitting alone with his marshmallow mountain -- a hunger that snacks couldn't fill.

He locked the lift booth and hurried toward the avenue.

But when he got there, the crowd was enormous. Bjorn Bigpaws stood near the front, his great white bulk blocking the view of anyone behind him. Nora Snowmane stood beside Bjorn. Behind them were rows and rows of animals, packed tight, scarves and goggles and skis sticking out at every angle.

Zack pushed toward the front. An otter glanced down at him and shifted away -- not cruelly, but not warmly either. A snow fox looked at him and muttered, "Oh, it's the toll penguin." Nobody moved to make space. Nobody offered a shoulder to sit on.

Zack tried jumping. He could see nothing but fur and feathers and the backs of heads.

"Excuse me -- could I just --"

"Line's full," someone said, not unkindly.

Zack stood on his tiptoes. He could hear cheering in the distance. The Rider was coming and he was going to miss it. After all those evenings alone, after all those marshmallows that tasted like nothing, after all that hollow feeling -- he was going to miss the one thing that felt like it might matter.

Then he spotted the tree.

It was a massive Frostpeak pine at the edge of the avenue, its branches heavy with snow, its trunk rough and knotted with handholds. It was tall enough to see over every polar bear in the valley.

*I can't climb a tree*, Zack thought. *I'm a penguin. Penguins don't climb trees. This is ridiculous.*

He was already climbing.

His flippers slipped on the bark. Snow fell down his scarf in cold clumps. A branch poked him in the belly. He scrabbled and scrambled and slid backward twice and made a sound like "GNNNGHHH" that was deeply undignified, but he kept going. Barnaby spotted him from below and nearly choked on a fish cracker.

"Is that -- is Zack up a TREE?"

"I believe it is," said Willa Wobble, tilting her tiny head.

"Penguins don't climb trees!" said Finnegan.

"That one does," said Willa.

Zack reached a thick branch about fifteen feet up. He wrapped both flippers around it and held on, breathing hard, snow in his goggles, bark scratches on his belly. But he could see.

He could see *everything*.

And there, coming down the main avenue on a snowboard that gleamed like starlight on fresh powder, was the Rider. She moved with a grace that made the snow itself seem to hold its breath. She wore a simple silver scarf, and her eyes were warm and deep, and when she smiled, it was like the first sun of spring.

The crowd cheered. Animals waved scarves and threw snowflakes in the air. Captain Prism attempted a backflip in the canal and mostly succeeded.

The Rider carved a long, smooth arc down the avenue, spraying a gentle fan of powder -- and then she stopped.

She stopped right under Zack's tree.

She looked up.

The whole valley went quiet.

Zack's heart was hammering so hard he was sure the branch was shaking. *She's looking at ME. Why is she looking at ME? I'm the one nobody likes. I'm the toll penguin. I'm --*

The Rider smiled. It was the warmest thing Zack had ever felt, including hot cocoa.

"Zack," she said. "Come on down. I'm having dinner at your place tonight."

Zack stared. His beak fell open. A small chunk of bark fell out of his scarf.

"My -- my place?" he squeaked.

"Your place," she said.

The crowd rippled with whispers. "The TOLL penguin's house?" "She picked HIM?" "But he's the one who charges five marshmallows for the cushion chair!"

Zack scrambled down that tree so fast he left a trail of pine needles and penguin dignity behind him. He landed in a heap at the Rider's feet, popped up, brushed bark off his belly, and said, "I -- I'll make cocoa. I have a LOT of marshmallows."

The Rider laughed. It sounded like sleigh bells.

---

That evening, something happened in Zack's little booth that had never happened before.

It was full of warmth.

The Rider sat at his tiny table, and they talked. Really talked. She didn't lecture him or scold him or make him feel small -- well, smaller. She told him stories about the valleys she'd visited, about animals who had found their way back from being lost, about the joy of giving instead of collecting. She listened to him, too. She listened to the loneliness and the hollow feeling and the marshmallow mountain that never filled the empty space.

And somewhere between the second and third cup of cocoa, Zack felt something crack open inside his chest. Not painfully. More like a frozen window finally letting in the light.

"I've been terrible," he whispered.

"You've been lost," she said gently. "Those are different things."

Zack stood up. He walked to the back of the booth where his enormous stash was piled -- fish crackers and marshmallows and candied snowberries, all the things he'd overcharged for, all the things he'd hoarded while his neighbors went without.

He opened the booth door.

"BARNABY!" he shouted into the evening air. "FINNEGAN! EVERYONE! Come here!"

They came cautiously, the way you approach a penguin who has been up a tree and might still be unpredictable. Barnaby arrived first, because Barnaby always arrived first when snacks might be involved.

"I owe you all... a lot," Zack said. His voice was shaking, but he stood as tall as a very short penguin could stand. "Every extra marshmallow, every made-up fee, every time I charged you for the cushion chair -- I'm giving it all back. Double."

Silence.

"Did he say DOUBLE?" Teddy Powderpuff whispered.

"Double," Zack confirmed. He started handing out marshmallows by the fistful. Fish crackers rained from his flippers. He gave Finnegan back so many snowberries that Finnegan's pockets overflowed and left a trail of berries in the snow behind him.

"And the Pine Lift?" Zack said. "It's free now. For everyone. Forever. The cushion chair too."

Bjorn Bigpaws, watching from the edge of the crowd, took a slow sip from his thermos. "Well," he said quietly. "Well, well, well."

Willa Wobble smiled her small, steady smile. "That," she said, "is what it looks like when someone finds their way home."

The Rider stood in the doorway of Zack's booth, silver scarf catching the glow of the northern lights that had begun to dance above the valley. She watched Zack handing out treats with both flippers, laughing for what might have been the first time in months, surrounded by animals who were looking at him -- really *looking* at him -- with something new in their eyes.

"Today," the Rider said softly, "this little booth became the warmest place in the whole valley."

Magnus Meltsworth lumbered over, accepted a marshmallow, grunted, and said, "Don't think this means I like you." Then he came back for three more marshmallows and muttered, "Fine. I slightly like you."

That night, for the first time ever, Zack's booth was full of friends. Herschel brought a fresh pot of his legendary peppermint cocoa. Cleo Coldwater sketched the scene in her notebook -- the booth glowing golden, the crowd gathered close, the little penguin in the middle who looked like he'd grown three inches just from smiling. Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff fell asleep in a marshmallow pile, which Nora had to carefully extract them from.

And Zack, who had spent so many evenings alone counting things that didn't matter, sat surrounded by warmth that no amount of marshmallows could buy -- and found that the hollow feeling was completely, perfectly, wonderfully gone.
