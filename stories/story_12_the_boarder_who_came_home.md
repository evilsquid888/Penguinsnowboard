# Story 12: The Boarder Who Came Home
### *Inspired by The Prodigal Son — Luke 15:11–32*
**Characters:** Finnegan Flipsworth, Barnaby Beaksworth, Nora Snowmane  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, distant Icewind Ridge, Cocoa Warming Hut  
**Theme:** Grace, return, and being welcomed home
**Tone:** Fun, whimsical, cozy bedtime adventure

---

## The Story

Finnegan Flipsworth had an idea.

This was not unusual.

"I'm going on tour," he announced, standing on a barrel outside the Cocoa Warming Hut. "I shall conquer every slope beyond Frostpeak and become LEGENDARY!"

Barnaby blinked. "You've never packed socks for one night."

"Legends don't fold socks," Finnegan declared.

By sunset, he was gone: red board, overstuffed backpack, huge grin.

For the first day, everything felt grand. New ridges. New snow. New audiences of confused ptarmigans.

By the fourth day, his snacks were gone.

By the sixth day, he had traded his emergency mittens for a map he couldn't read.

By the eighth day, he was cold, tired, and very far from home, sleeping behind a windbreak made of old sled parts.

He missed Frostpeak's soft evening bells. He missed Barnaby's fish chowder. He even missed Magnus saying, "I told you so," before handing over a blanket.

Under a thin moon, Finnegan whispered into his scarf, "I should go home."

He expected shame to answer.

Instead, only snow answered — quiet and kind.

So he turned back.

The journey home felt longer than the journey out. He rehearsed apologies the whole way.

*I was foolish.*

*I was loud and proud and wrong.*

*Please let me sleep in the tool shed and I will sweep everything forever.*

When he finally reached Frostpeak Valley, he stood above the Cocoa Warming Hut at twilight and froze.

The windows glowed gold. Smoke curled from the chimney. Laughter floated up the hill.

He took one step forward.

Before he could take another, Barnaby burst out the hut door.

"FINNEGAN!"

Barnaby ran uphill at impossible speed for a round penguin, arms wide, backpack bouncing.

He crashed into Finnegan in a fish-scented hug so big it almost knocked them both over.

"You're home! You're home! You're HOME!" Barnaby kept saying.

Finnegan tried to begin his speech.

"Barnaby, I have prepared a formal statement regarding my poor choices and—"

"No speech," Barnaby said, tears in his eyes. "Just come in."

Inside, the whole valley had gathered.

Piper waved both flippers. Cleo cried openly. Teddy and Tilda yelled, "YOU OWE US A STORY!"

Nora Snowmane set a warm mug in Finnegan's paws. "Drink first," she said. "Apologize later if you still need to."

"But I messed up," Finnegan said, voice small.

Nora nodded. "Yes. And you came back."

Magnus grunted from the corner. "Most important part."

Finnegan stood there, snow still melting from his feathers, surrounded by familiar faces and the smell of cocoa and cedar, and felt something he had not expected:

He felt loved before he had earned it.

Later that evening, Barnaby hung a banner over the hut door: **WELCOME HOME, LEGENDARY MESS.**

Everyone cheered.

Finnegan laughed until he cried.

That night, he slept in his own bunk with two extra blankets and a bowl of crackers by the bed, just in case.

In the morning he woke up early, looked out over Frostpeak Valley, and whispered, "I thought I had to become somebody far away."

Then he smiled.

"Turns out I was already somebody, right here."
