# Story 81: Peter's Picnic Blanket Surprise
### *Inspired by Peter and Cornelius — Acts 10*
**Characters:** Pipin Penguin, Nora Snowmane  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Open Table Rink  
**Theme:** Welcome everyone

---

## Opening Wink

If moonbeams had mittens, this would be the night they came out to clap for the valley.

## The Story

**Pipin Penguin** had a strong opinion about the Open Table Rink, and the opinion was this: it was for the regular crowd.

Pipin was not mean about it. He didn't say it loudly or post it anywhere. It was just a working assumption that had settled into his habits the way assumptions do — quietly, and with the feeling of being obvious. The Open Table Rink's Friday evening skate was attended by the same crowd every week: Bjorn, Nora, Pete, Willa, Finnegan (chaotically), the Kingfisher Choir (harmoniously), Cleo with her sketchbook, Barnaby with his thermos. The regulars. The people who had been coming since the beginning.

When **Nora Snowmane** suggested inviting the Valley East community for the midwinter skate — a group of creatures who lived on the far side of the valley and used a different rink entirely — Pipin had said *oh, of course, sure* in the way that meant *I have questions but I won't voice them right now.*

He voiced them at the Warming Hut afterward.

"They don't skate the same way we do," he said. "Their rink has different culture. Different pacing. It'll change the whole — we have a system."

"A system," said Nora.

"A flow. A rhythm. Things run a certain way on Friday evenings."

Nora looked at him. Not critically — just watching.

"Do you know what they actually do at their rink?" she said.

Pipin thought about it. "Not specifically."

"Have you been?"

"No."

"Have you talked to any of them?"

He had not.

---

Nora took Pipin to Valley East the following Tuesday. Not to announce anything — just to see.

The Valley East rink was smaller than the Open Table but warmer-lit, with strings of small colored lanterns around the perimeter. They had a different style of skating — more improvisational, less pattern-based, with a cluster of older creatures in the center who moved slowly and talked while they circled, and younger ones doing something that looked almost like a dance, and a hot cider station at the south end that Pipin had to admit was an excellent idea.

A large otter named Theo saw them and skated over.

"Visitors!" he said, with the genuine warmth of someone for whom visitors are good news. "Come in, come in. Do you skate? Of course you skate — you have skates on. Come."

He swept them into the flow of things so naturally that Pipin barely noticed he was doing it. Within twenty minutes he was doing slow circles with the older creatures in the center, learning three things about the valley's drainage history he hadn't known, and someone had handed him a hot cider.

He skated back to Nora after half an hour.

"They're—" he started.

"Yes," said Nora.

"I thought it would be—"

"Different and wrong?" said Nora.

"I was going to say unfamiliar," said Pipin, with dignity.

"Was it?"

He looked at the rink. At Theo, already waving from across the ice. At the colored lanterns. At the cider station.

"Yes," said Pipin. "And—" he paused "—good."

"Both can be true," said Nora.

---

The midwinter combined skate at Open Table Rink went more smoothly than Pipin's working assumption had predicted.

Theo's group brought the hot cider station. Barnaby contributed an upgraded soup. The Kingfisher Choir and the Valley East musicians spent forty-five minutes arguing pleasantly about tempo and then produced something that everyone agreed was better than what either group did alone. Finnegan and a young Valley East otter named Splash spent the entire evening doing increasingly elaborate collaborative spins that were dangerous, technically magnificent, and absolutely unplanned.

At the end of the evening, Pipin found himself at the cider station talking to Theo about drainage history, trail maintenance, and what made the east-facing slopes different from the west-facing ones, and realized he'd been talking for forty minutes and was genuinely not ready to stop.

"Next month?" said Theo, as the lanterns came down.

"Next month," said Pipin.

He meant it. He was already looking forward to it.

The table is open. There's always room. The hardest part, usually, is just deciding that the people coming are actually welcome — and then being surprised by how right it feels when they arrive.
