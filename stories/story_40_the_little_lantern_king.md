# Story 40: The Little Lantern King
### *Inspired by Samuel Anoints David — 1 Samuel 16*
**Characters:** Davy Dormouse, Bjorn Bigpaws  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Lantern Lodge  
**Theme:** Heart over appearance

---

## Opening Wink

Funny thing about lanterns: the biggest ones aren't always the brightest. Sometimes the tiniest flame is the one that lights up the whole mountain.

## The Story

The trouble started, as trouble often did in Frostpeak Valley, with Bjorn Bigpaws making an announcement.

"I am stepping down," Bjorn said.

He said it at breakfast, in the middle of Lantern Lodge, while fourteen animals were mid-bite. Barnaby Beaksworth choked on a fish cracker. Piper Paddlefoot dropped her cocoa. Finnegan Flipsworth fell off his chair, though that might have been unrelated.

"WHAT?" said everyone, all at once.

Bjorn held up one massive paw. "I have led this valley for many seasons. My bones are tired. My skis are tired. Even my scarf is tired." He gestured to his scarf, which did, in fairness, look a bit droopy. "It is time to choose a new Lantern Keeper."

The Lantern Keeper was the most important role in Frostpeak Valley. They carried the Great Lantern at the head of every festival parade, made decisions when the trails got tricky, and -- most importantly -- kept the signal light burning on the peak of Lantern Lodge so that lost travelers could always find their way home.

"But who?" asked Nora Snowmane.

Bjorn looked out across the room. "Herschel," he said. "You will help me choose."

Herschel the Walrus set down his cocoa ladle, stroked one magnificent tusk, and nodded. "Bring me the candidates. I'll know the right one when I see them."

And so the tryouts began.

First came Magnus Meltsworth. He was the biggest bear in the valley after Bjorn, with shoulders like boulders and a growl that could rearrange furniture. He stomped up to Lantern Lodge, grabbed the Great Lantern with one paw, and held it over his head.

"I'm the strongest," Magnus declared. "Obviously I should lead."

"Strength is good," said Herschel. "But that's not what I'm looking for."

"What? Why not?" Magnus sputtered.

"Next," said Herschel.

Then came Piper Paddlefoot. She arrived on her snowboard, did a triple backflip off the lodge railing, stuck the landing, and struck a pose so dramatic that three seals gave her a standing ovation.

"Slide into it!" she shouted. "I'm the bravest! I'll lead this valley to glory!"

"Bravery is wonderful," said Herschel. "But that's not what I'm looking for either."

Piper's beak dropped open. "But I did a *triple backflip*."

"It was very impressive," Herschel agreed. "Next."

Finnegan tried next. His audition involved a complicated plan with seventeen steps, four backup plans, a smoke signal, and a map drawn on the back of a fish cracker wrapper. He explained it for twenty-two minutes. Nobody understood any of it, including Finnegan.

"I appreciate your... enthusiasm," said Herschel carefully.

"Was that a yes?" asked Finnegan.

"That was a next."

One by one, the valley's biggest, loudest, fastest, and most impressive animals tried out. Bjorn watched them all with his arms crossed and his expression unreadable. Herschel said "next" so many times it started to sound like a strange new word.

By late afternoon, the lodge was full of rejected candidates munching consolation crackers, and Herschel still hadn't chosen anyone.

"Perhaps," said Nora gently, "you're looking for something that isn't here."

"It's here," said Herschel, closing his eyes. "I can feel it. Like a warm spot in cold water. It's close."

That was when the lodge door opened -- just a crack -- and a tiny voice said, "Excuse me, has anyone seen my lantern? I left it on the porch this morning and I think the wind took it."

Everyone turned. Standing in the doorway, barely tall enough to see over the threshold, was Davy Dormouse. He was the smallest animal in Frostpeak Valley by a considerable margin. He wore a scarf that was twice as long as he was, and his whiskers were frosted with snowflakes. In one paw he held a thimble-sized mug of cocoa. The other paw was empty -- clearly where his lantern usually hung.

"Sorry to interrupt," said Davy. "You all look busy. I'll just check the porch and--"

"Wait," said Herschel.

Every head in the lodge swiveled to the walrus. Herschel's eyes were open now, and they were bright.

"You're Davy, aren't you? The one who tends the trail markers on the lower slopes?"

Davy blinked. "Yes, sir. Somebody has to. The snow covers them up and then the little ones -- the baby seals and the fox kits -- they get lost on the way to the sledding hill. So I dig them out every morning. It's not a big job."

"Every morning?" said Bjorn, raising one enormous eyebrow.

"Well, yes. Before dawn, usually. That's when the snow is easiest to brush away. And I relight the path lanterns if they've gone out. And sometimes I leave fish crackers at the trailhead for anyone who forgot breakfast. Barnaby gave me a big bag." He looked at Barnaby. "Thank you again for that, by the way."

Barnaby, who had tears forming in his eyes for the second time this season, whispered, "You're welcome, buddy."

Herschel turned to Bjorn. "This is the one."

The lodge erupted.

"HIM?" Magnus nearly knocked over a table. "He's the size of my *paw*!"

"He can't even reach the Great Lantern!" said Piper.

"His plan probably has zero steps!" said Finnegan, which he meant as an insult but which actually sounded quite appealing.

Davy looked bewildered. "The one what? I just want my lantern back."

Herschel waddled over to the tiny dormouse and looked down at him with ancient, gentle eyes. "Davy, I've been watching this valley for a long, long time. I've seen the strongest animals and the fastest animals and the flashiest animals. But do you know what I've been looking for?"

Davy shook his head.

"A heart that takes care of others when nobody is watching. A heart that digs out trail markers before dawn, not for applause, but because baby seals might get lost. A heart that shares fish crackers at the trailhead with no name attached." Herschel smiled -- a slow, tusked, warm smile. "That's not a small thing, Davy. That's the biggest thing there is."

Bjorn stepped forward. From behind his back, he produced a lantern -- not the Great Lantern, but something even more special. It was small, delicate, and golden, crafted from mountain crystal, and when he placed it in Davy's tiny paws, it blazed with a light so warm and steady that every animal in the lodge felt it in their chest.

"The Lantern Keeper's light," said Bjorn, "was never about being the biggest flame. It was about being the truest one."

Davy looked down at the golden lantern. Its glow reflected in his dark, round eyes. "But I'm just... I'm just me," he said.

"Yes," said Bjorn. "That is exactly the point."

The lodge was quiet. Then Willa Wobble started clapping. Then Barnaby. Then Cleo, who was already sketching the moment in her notebook. Then Finnegan, who clapped so hard he fell off his chair again. Even Magnus clapped, grudgingly at first, and then not grudgingly at all, because the golden light was very, very hard to argue with.

That evening, the valley held an impromptu parade. Davy rode on Bjorn's shoulder, holding the golden lantern high, and its light reached every corner of Frostpeak Valley -- all the way to the farthest trail marker on the lowest slope, where a family of baby seals looked up and smiled.

"I still can't believe it," Piper said to Willa, as they watched the parade wind through the snowy streets.

"Really?" said Willa. "I can. The ones who care the most are usually the ones who make the least noise about it."

Piper thought about that. Then she grinned. "Okay, but I'm still the best at backflips."

"Nobody is arguing with you about that."

Up on Bjorn's shoulder, Davy Dormouse held his lantern and looked out at the valley that was now, somehow, his to look after. The golden light spilled across the snow, and the aurora above seemed to lean down close, as if it wanted to see the little lantern king too.

"I'll do my best," Davy whispered.

And the light burned on, steady and true, all through the long, gentle night.
