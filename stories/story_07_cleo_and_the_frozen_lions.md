# Story 7: Cleo and the Frozen Lions
### *Inspired by Daniel in the Lions' Den — Daniel 6*
**Characters:** Cleo Coldwater, Magnus Meltsworth, the Valley Council
**Setting:** The Glacier Den, Starwatch Cliff
**Theme:** Courage to stay true to who you are; integrity when it costs something

---

> *Settle in, little one. This is a story about a penguin who wouldn't stop being herself — and why that mattered more than anything.*

---

## The Story

Cleo Coldwater had a habit.

Every evening, exactly at sunset, she would climb to Starwatch Cliff, open her sketchbook, and spend one quiet hour drawing what she had seen and giving thanks for the beauty of it. She had done this every day for as long as she could remember. It was her most important habit. It was the center of who she was.

She didn't make a fuss about it. She didn't announce it. She just went.

---

The trouble started with the Valley Council.

The Valley Council was made up of five animals who made decisions about valley life: Bjorn Bigpaws (who took it very seriously), two glacier foxes (who were mainly there for the snacks), a snow owl named Reginald (who was asleep for approximately 40% of every meeting), and Vole — a very small, very ambitious vole who had recently decided he was extremely important.

Vole did not like Cleo.

Specifically, Vole did not like the way Cleo was so clearly loved by everyone in the valley. Every time she passed through, animals stopped to look at her sketches and say warm things. Children wanted to sit beside her. Even Bjorn — who admired very few things besides his own skiing form — had been heard to say she was "extraordinary."

Vole did not like being next to extraordinary.

So Vole proposed a new rule.

"The Valley Efficiency Proclamation," he called it, reading from a scroll he had written himself, "states that all personal habits which are *not* officially approved activities shall be ceased immediately, including but not limited to: unauthorized cliff-sitting, unauthorized sketchbook usage during designated productive hours, and — " a tiny pause — "unauthorized sunset observance."

Bjorn raised one large eyebrow. "Unauthorized sunset observance?"

"*Very* disruptive," said Vole firmly.

"To whom?"

"To *order*," said Vole.

Bjorn looked at the glaciers foxes. The glacier foxes shrugged and ate a fish cracker. Reginald snored. And the proclamation, somehow, passed.

The word came to Cleo like a pebble into a still pond: *You must stop.*

She held the message for a long time. She read it. She thought about it.

Then, at sunset, she climbed to Starwatch Cliff, opened her sketchbook, and began to draw.

---

It did not take long for Vole to find out.

He had, he was very pleased to report to the full Council, been conducting *monitoring activities*. He had personally witnessed Cleo at the cliff. He demanded consequences.

The consequences, as determined by the Proclamation, were three days in the Glacier Den.

The Glacier Den was a deep natural cave in the ice on the north side of the mountain, and it was not dangerous, exactly, but it was cold and dark and not very fun. More relevantly: it was home to the Ice Lions — two large, old, sleepy snow leopards who were called "lions" because they were the largest cats in the valley and had impressively fluffy fur and made everyone slightly nervous.

Their names were Rebus and Clementine, and they were mostly interested in napping.

Cleo walked into the Glacier Den with her sketchbook under her wing.

---

Magnus Meltsworth watched all of this from the slope above with the expression of a bear who has seen everything go wrong and is personally offended on behalf of rightness.

*Fine,* he thought. *But I'm only doing this once.*

He spent the night at the entrance of the Glacier Den. He didn't go in — the rules said nobody could enter — but he sat on his battered old wooden skis in the dark with his arms crossed and his grumpy face pointed at the cave entrance in case anything went wrong.

Nothing went wrong.

In the morning, when the cave entrance was unsealed, Magnus was the first one there.

"Cleo," he called into the dark.

A beat of silence.

Then: *"Every slope tells a story."*

And out came Cleo Coldwater, golden feathers bright, lavender scarf — wait, that was Willa; Cleo's sketchbook, held up — open to a new page.

She had spent the night drawing the Ice Lions.

Rebus was asleep in the background, enormous and rumpled. Clementine was curled in the foreground, one paw over her nose. They were both rendered in extraordinary precise loving detail, every whisker, every fold of thick fur.

"They're beautiful," Cleo said simply. "I've never drawn a snow leopard before. I didn't want to waste the opportunity."

Magnus looked at the drawing for a long moment.

"Extraordinary," he said, in a voice very much like Bjorn's voice when Bjorn said it, and he would deny this forever.

---

The Valley Council reconvened.

Bjorn had spent the night not sleeping and had arrived to the meeting with his thermos and a very specific expression.

"The Proclamation," he said, "was foolish. It targeted one animal for being who she is. We will not do this again." He looked at Vole. "Furthermore, I would like to see the sketches."

Vole opened his mouth.

Bjorn raised one very large paw. "That was not a question."

---

The Proclamation was repealed.

And every evening after that, at sunset, Cleo climbed to Starwatch Cliff with her sketchbook, and she was never alone — because someone always seemed to find their way up there too, to sit beside her and watch the colors change, and she never minded, and the valley was better for it.

She drew everything.

She always would.

## Image Prompts

**Cover Illustration:**
> A macaroni penguin with golden feathers sits in a blue-lit ice cave, sketchbook open and glowing with warm light. On either side of her, two enormous snow leopards sleep — massive, fluffy, completely unmenacing. She draws them with calm focus. Deep blue cave walls, soft light from the sketchbook page. Style: intimate, luminous, slightly magical — a tiny figure of warmth surrounded by sleeping greatness.

**Scene 1 — Vole's Proclamation:**
> The Valley Council meeting: a large polar bear looking skeptical, a snow owl asleep, two foxes eating crackers, and one extremely self-important vole holding a long scroll and pointing at a clause. Style: warm satire, comedic expressions, slightly absurd officialness.

**Scene 2 — Cleo Climbs Anyway:**
> Sunset on the mountain path to Starwatch Cliff. Cleo walks up with sketchbook under her wing and golden feathers catching the light. Her expression: calm, decided, completely at peace. The sky behind her is orange and purple and glorious. Style: quiet defiance depicted as perfect peace — the most serene image in the story.

**Scene 3 — Magnus at the Door:**
> Night. The Glacier Den entrance is sealed with ice. Magnus Meltsworth sits outside on his battered wooden skis, arms crossed, determined grumpy face pointed at the cave. Stars above. Darkness. He is a very large bear being stubbornly present. Style: midnight blue tones, one warm lantern, a loyalty that needs no words.

Then Cleo sits down, opens her sketchbook, and begins to draw. Close-up on the paper: Clementine's whiskers appearing in precise pencil lines. Music: gentle wonder, almost reverential.

**Morning Release:**

Then Cleo appears, golden feathers catching first light, sketchbook raised to show the sleeping leopards. Magnus looks at it. Long beat. "Extraordinary." He says it quietly, looking away, pretending he didn't. Music: warm sunrise strings. Fade to full morning over the valley.
