# Story 103: Barnaby's Three Snow Talents
### *Inspired by The Parable of the Talents — Matthew 25:14–30*
**Characters:** Barnaby Beaksworth, Cleo Coldwater, Finnegan Flipsworth, Willa Wobble, Aurora the Arctic Fox
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, the Annual Skills Showcase
**Theme:** Use what you've been given; hiding your gifts helps no one

---

> *Settle in, sleepy one. This is a story about gifts — not the kind wrapped in paper, but the kind wrapped in you.*

---

## Opening Wink

*Revision Pass 3 Spark:* Finnegan's "gift" was chaos. He used it generously. No one asked him to.

Everyone has something. The trick is not to bury it in the snow.

## The Story

Once a year, Frostpeak Valley held the **Annual Skills Showcase**, and it was Aurora the Arctic Fox's favorite day of the whole calendar.

Aurora was the valley's unofficial mayor, event planner, and motivational speaker, all wrapped in one sleek white-and-silver fox. She organized everything — the races, the festivals, the weather preparedness drills. And the Skills Showcase was her masterpiece: a day where every animal in the valley got to share something they were good at.

This year, Aurora had a plan. She was going on a two-week expedition to the Northern Reaches to scout new ski trails. Before she left, she called three friends to the Warming Hut.

"I need you three to organize this year's Showcase while I'm gone," she said.

She looked at **Barnaby Beaksworth**. "Barnaby, you're in charge of the Food and Hospitality Pavilion. You have a gift for feeding people and making them feel welcome. Use it."

She looked at **Cleo Coldwater**. "Cleo, you're in charge of the Arts and Performance Stage. You see beauty everywhere. Make the stage beautiful."

She looked at **Finnegan Flipsworth**. "Finnegan, you're in charge of... the Games Tent."

"YESSSS!" shouted Finnegan.

"Please try not to set anything on fire."

"I make NO promises!"

Aurora smiled, adjusted her silver scarf, and headed for the Northern Reaches.

---

Cleo started immediately. She spent the first three days sketching designs for the Performance Stage — a curved amphitheater of packed snow with ice-crystal light fixtures and a backdrop painted with scenes from the valley's history. She recruited helpers. She taught the otter twins to carve ice lanterns. She convinced Reginald the snow owl to perform his dramatic reading of "Ode to Powder" (which was dreadful, but Reginald was so earnest about it that it looped back around to being wonderful).

By the end of week one, the Performance Stage was extraordinary.

Finnegan, meanwhile, had turned the Games Tent into something that could only be described as "controlled pandemonium." He'd invented seven new games, including Snowball Pinball (dangerous), Flipper Relay Extreme (chaotic), and something called "Guess What's In Finnegan's Hat" (nobody wanted to play this but everyone did anyway). He'd also somehow acquired a confetti cannon, which he tested four times, each time in a more inappropriate location.

"THAT ONE WAS AN ACCIDENT!" he shouted from inside a snow drift, covered in confetti.

"They were ALL accidents!" shouted Piper from a safe distance.

But the Games Tent was going to be incredible, in the way that natural disasters are incredible — overwhelming, unforgettable, and best viewed from a slight distance.

---

And then there was Barnaby.

Barnaby had a gift. Everyone knew it. He could take five fish, three crackers, and a handful of cloudberries and somehow produce a feast that made every animal feel like the most important guest at the most important dinner. His cocoa was legendary. His fish cakes were transcendent. His ability to make anyone feel welcome was, quite simply, the warmest thing in Frostpeak Valley, and that was saying something for a place with an actual warming hut.

But Barnaby was afraid.

Not afraid of cooking — he could cook in his sleep (and occasionally did, which explained the midnight fish cracker incidents). He was afraid of *the Showcase*. What if the Food Pavilion wasn't good enough? What if people compared it to last year's? What if he tried his hardest and it was still just... ordinary?

So Barnaby did something unusual for Barnaby.

He did nothing.

He told himself he was "planning." He told himself he was "waiting for inspiration." He told himself the fish supply was "uncertain" (it was not) and the cloudberry harvest was "questionable" (it was fine) and the whole Pavilion idea was "still in development" (it was not in development at all).

Days passed. A week passed. Willa noticed.

"Barnaby," she said, finding him sitting behind the Warming Hut, staring at his orange pizza-board like it held answers. "The Showcase is in three days. I walked past the Food Pavilion. It's... empty."

"I know," said Barnaby miserably. "I just — what if it's not good enough?"

"Good enough for what?"

"For... them. Everyone. What if I give everything I have and it's still not—"

Willa sat down next to him. She was quiet for a moment.

"Barnaby," she said. "Aurora didn't ask you to be perfect. She asked you to use your gift. Those are two very different things."

Barnaby looked at her.

"You have three days," said Willa. "That's enough. The smallest step is still a step."

---

Barnaby stood up. He rolled up his sleeves. He walked into the empty Food Pavilion and he *began*.

Three days. He worked like a penguin possessed. He built a long communal table from pine boards and covered it in checkered cloth. He set up a cocoa station with six different flavors (classic, peppermint, cloudberry, spiced cinnamon, double-fish — don't ask — and Herschel's avant-garde salt cocoa, because Barnaby was generous enough to include everyone's taste). He prepared trays of fish cakes and berry tarts and crackers and warm bread and a centerpiece made of carved ice fish that Cleo helped him with.

He hung lanterns. He put flowers (frost-daisies, the kind that bloomed in cold) in small jars on every table. He wrote a sign that said, in his big, round handwriting: **"THERE'S ALWAYS ENOUGH FOR ONE MORE."**

On Showcase day, Aurora returned from the Northern Reaches. She walked through the valley with growing wonder.

The Performance Stage was magnificent — Cleo's ice lanterns glowed like captured stars, and Reginald the owl was already warming up his dramatic reading voice.

The Games Tent was... vibrating. Literally. Something inside was making a rhythmic thumping noise that Aurora chose not to investigate. Confetti drifted from its entrance like snow.

And the Food Pavilion was beautiful.

Not fancy. Not overwhelming. Just warm. Just right. Just *Barnaby*.

Every table was full. Every mug was steaming. Every animal who walked in was greeted by Barnaby himself, who somehow remembered everyone's name and everyone's favorite flavor and made each one feel like the guest of honor.

Aurora found Barnaby behind the cocoa station, flour on his beak, utterly exhausted, utterly happy.

"You used your gift," she said.

Barnaby's eyes were wet. "Almost didn't," he admitted. "Almost buried it in the snow."

Aurora put her paw on his wing. "But you didn't. And look."

She gestured at the Pavilion. Animals eating, laughing, sharing food with strangers, passing dishes to neighbors. A glacier fox and a penguin splitting a berry tart. The twins riding a bread basket like a sled. Herschel drinking his salt cocoa with enormous satisfaction.

"You made this," said Aurora. "With what you had. And it's more than enough."

Barnaby looked at the sign above the door. *There's always enough for one more.*

"Yeah," he said, and wiped his eyes with a napkin. "Yeah, it is."
