# Story 69: Workers at Sunset Slope
### *Inspired by Workers in the Vineyard — Matthew 20*
**Characters:** Vinnie Vole, Barnaby Beaksworth  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Sunset Slope  
**Theme:** Generosity beyond fairness

---

## Opening Wink

Ever notice how the best sunsets happen right when you think the day is over? Tonight's story is a bit like that -- full of surprises at the very last minute.

## The Story

Sunset Slope had a problem, and the problem was ice.

Not the good kind of ice -- the smooth, glassy, perfect-for-skating kind. No. This was the lumpy, crunchy, ankle-twisting kind that appeared overnight after a freak warm spell followed by a snap freeze, leaving the entire slope looking like a frozen ocean caught mid-tantrum.

Bjorn Bigpaws stood at the top of Sunset Slope at dawn, his breath forming clouds the size of throw pillows. He surveyed the disaster with the calm expression of someone who had seen worse but would rather not have.

"We need groomers," he announced. "Lots of them. By sundown, or tomorrow's Junior Jamboree gets cancelled."

"CANCELLED?" Piper Paddlefoot materialized so fast she seemed to teleport. "Bjorn, we can't cancel the Jamboree! The little ones have been practicing for weeks! Teddy Powderpuff learned to stop without crashing into things! Well, most things."

"Then we groom the slope," Bjorn said simply. He reached into his vest pocket and pulled out a pawful of golden cocoa tokens -- the special ones that Herschel the Walrus only gave out for serious work. "Everyone who helps today gets five golden tokens. Good for a full week of unlimited cocoa."

Piper's eyes went wide. "Unlimited? Even the peppermint swirl?"

"Even the peppermint swirl."

"SLIDE INTO IT!" Piper bellowed, and took off down the mountain so fast she left a contrail of snow.

By seven o'clock, the early crew was hard at work. Piper attacked the ice ridges with a flat-bottomed sled, dragging it back and forth with the determination of a penguin possessed. Barnaby Beaksworth showed up with a shovel in one flipper and a sardine sandwich in the other, alternating between digging and eating with a rhythm that was oddly impressive. Cleo Coldwater brought her artistic eye, spotting dangerous bumps that others missed and marking them with little flags painted in watercolors because, as she put it, "Safety can still be beautiful."

By mid-morning, Willa Wobble arrived. She was small -- the smallest penguin in the valley, really -- and she'd had to waddle all the way from the far side of Snowdrift Meadow, which for a Little Blue Penguin was quite the trek.

"Sorry I'm late," she puffed, adjusting her scarf. "My alarm clock got buried in snow again."

"No worries, Willa!" Barnaby called. "Grab a rake and jump in!"

Willa did, working steadily and carefully, smoothing out patches that the bigger animals had missed because they were too high up to notice the small bumps.

At noon, Finnegan Flipsworth came rocketing in from who-knows-where, his yellow eyebrow tufts blowing sideways, dragging a contraption made of three brooms, a toboggan, and what appeared to be a fishing net.

"I INVENTED A GROOMING MACHINE!" he announced proudly.

The machine promptly fell apart.

"I'll just use a shovel," he said, picking one up from the pile.

The afternoon wore on. The sun crept across the sky, painting the snow in shades of peach and gold. Piper wiped sweat from her brow -- which is hard to do when you're a penguin, but she managed -- and looked at the progress. Sunset Slope was starting to look beautiful again. Smooth runs emerged from the chaos. The crunchy ice surrendered under their work.

At three o'clock, Magnus Meltsworth came trudging up the slope, grumpy as a thundercloud with a toothache. "Fine," he growled. "Nora told me to help, so I'm here. Where's a shovel?"

"You're four hours late!" Piper pointed out.

"I was napping," Magnus said, as though this were a perfectly reasonable explanation, which for a polar bear of his age and disposition, it sort of was.

And then, at the very last hour, just as the sun was beginning to kiss the tips of the western peaks, a group of young seal pups waddled up. They were small and round and their shovels were comically oversized.

"We heard you needed help?" the littlest one squeaked.

Piper looked at the slope. They were nearly done. "We're almost finished," she said. "But sure, grab a spot."

The seal pups dove in with such enthusiasm that they actually did help smooth out the final stretch, their round bodies acting as natural rollers on the remaining rough patches, which made everyone laugh so hard that Finnegan fell over sideways and slid halfway down the slope on his belly.

At sunset, Bjorn stood at the top of the perfectly groomed slope, the sky blazing orange and violet behind him. The whole crew gathered, panting, proud, leaning on their shovels and rakes. The slope gleamed like a ribbon of silk unrolling toward the valley below.

"Beautiful work," Bjorn said. He reached into his vest and began handing out golden cocoa tokens. "Five tokens each. Thank you all."

He gave five tokens to the seal pups who'd arrived in the last hour. Five tokens to Magnus, who'd shown up at three. Five tokens to Finnegan, who'd come at noon. And five tokens to Piper, Barnaby, and Cleo, who'd been there since dawn.

Piper stared at her tokens, then at the seal pups' tokens, then back at hers. "Wait a minute," she said. "Bjorn. We were here *all day*. Since sunrise. My flippers are practically frozen into shovel shapes. And the seal pups get the same as us?"

"Five tokens each," Bjorn confirmed.

"But that's not -- that's --" Piper sputtered.

"Not fair?" Bjorn raised an eyebrow -- a slow, deliberate raise that somehow conveyed an entire philosophy.

"Well... yeah!"

Bjorn sat down on a snow bank, which creaked but held. "Piper, did I promise you five tokens?"

"Yes."

"And did you get five tokens?"

"Yes, but --"

"Did I shortchange you?"

"No, but they only worked for *one hour* and --"

"And I chose to be generous with them." Bjorn's voice was gentle, like snow falling on snow. "Would you rather I was stingy?"

Piper opened her beak, but Willa got there first. She'd been sitting quietly on a snowbank, her tiny feet dangling.

"Piper," Willa said, "when Bjorn gave you five tokens this morning, you were thrilled. You called it the best deal in the valley. Remember?"

Piper's beak snapped shut. She did remember.

"The tokens didn't change," Willa continued. "They're still worth a whole week of cocoa. The only thing that changed is you saw someone else get the same gift, and suddenly it felt like less. But it isn't less. It's exactly what you were promised, and you were happy about it an hour ago."

The slope was very quiet. The sunset blazed. A single snowflake drifted down and landed on Piper's beak.

Barnaby, who had been listening while polishing off his fourth sardine sandwich of the day, cleared his throat. "You know, I got here at seven, and I'm not fussed. Five tokens buys a *lot* of cocoa. And those little seal pups looked so happy -- did you see them? Their whiskers were vibrating."

One of the seal pups waved a flipper. Its whiskers were, in fact, still vibrating.

Piper looked at the seal pup. She looked at her tokens. She looked at the gorgeous, gleaming slope they'd all built together -- the early birds and the latecomers and the grumpy bears and the chaos penguins and the tiny, round seal pups who'd rolled themselves into the work with pure joy.

"Okay," she said, and a grin crept across her face. "Okay, fine. Bjorn, you're annoyingly wise, you know that?"

Bjorn smiled. "I've been told."

"Generosity isn't about keeping score!" Finnegan declared, pumping a flipper in the air. Then, more quietly: "Wait, is that the lesson? I want to make sure I actually got it for once."

"You got it, Finn," Willa said warmly.

That evening, the entire crew -- dawn workers and sunset workers alike -- gathered at Herschel's cocoa cart. The walrus was in his element, mustache twitching with pride, ladle swirling through pots of dark chocolate, peppermint swirl, and his secret recipe cinnamon cloud cocoa that nobody could replicate no matter how many times they tried.

Piper sat between a seal pup and Magnus, who was grudgingly admitting that the cinnamon cloud flavor was "acceptable, I suppose," which from Magnus was practically a standing ovation.

"You know what?" Piper said, lifting her mug. "I think this cocoa tastes even better when everyone's drinking it together."

"Now *you're* being annoyingly wise," Barnaby said.

Piper grinned. Above them, the sunset faded into a sky full of stars, and the freshly groomed slope shimmered below like a promise waiting to be kept.
