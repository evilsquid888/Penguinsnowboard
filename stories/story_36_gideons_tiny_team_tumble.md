# Story 36: Gideon's Tiny Team Tumble
### *Inspired by Gideon's Small Army — Judges 7*
**Characters:** Gideon Goose, Willa Wobble  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Moon Basin  
**Theme:** God can use small teams

---

## Opening Wink

*Revision Pass 3 Spark:* When Gideon said "fewer is better," nobody expected him to mean THREE, and yet here they were — three creatures, one banner, and the most improbable victory in Frostpeak Valley history.

The annual Moon Basin relay was the most prestigious team race in the valley. Everyone wanted to win it. The trick was that winning it with a ridiculous small team nobody believed in turned out to be way more fun.

## The Story

The Moon Basin Night Relay was the race everyone in Frostpeak Valley wanted to win.

It happened every winter on the longest night — six teams of ten racers each, skiing a relay circuit around the full basin by torchlight, passing a glowing baton from paw to flipper to hoof in a long, looping chain that lit the mountain like a moving necklace of fire. The team that got all ten members around first won. Simple, grand, and deeply competitive.

**Gideon Goose** had been trying to enter a team for three years.

Gideon was a lanky goose with overlapping skis and a slightly crooked trail pass who nevertheless had the most determined expression in Frostpeak Valley. He believed, with the conviction of someone who had been laughed at a specific number of times and planned to be proved right, that size was not the point. Strategy was the point.

"I'm registering a team of three," he announced at the Warming Hut registration table, where Herschel was signing up participants.

Herschel looked up slowly. His walrus whiskers twitched. "Teams are ten racers."

"The rules say a minimum of three."

Herschel checked the rule sheet. He checked it again. "They do say that," he said, in the tone of someone who had written the rules years ago and now regretted a choice.

"Team Tiny," said Gideon. "Three members. Myself, Willa Wobble, and Finnegan Flipsworth."

Every head in the Warming Hut turned.

"Finnegan?" said Piper, from the corner. "You chose *Finnegan*? On purpose?"

"Finnegan is very fast," said Gideon.

"Finnegan crashed into a signpost twice this week," said Piper. "The same signpost. He came back the second time to see if the signpost had moved."

"Speed," said Gideon, "is speed."

"And Willa?" said Bjorn. "Willa is half the size of any other racer in this event."

**Willa Wobble** looked up from her cocoa calmly. "The smallest step is still a step. The fastest step counts the same as any other."

Bjorn blinked. He did not have a follow-up.

The larger teams — including the defending champions, Team Powder (ten strong, all former competitive skiers) — studied the three-creature entry with varying degrees of pity and amusement. The captain of Team Powder, a broad snow leopard named Sverre, smiled with the confident patience of someone who had no doubt about the outcome.

"Good luck," said Sverre.

"Thank you," said Gideon. "We will not need it, but I appreciate the thought."

---

On the night of the race, Moon Basin was lit with two hundred torches in holders along the circuit, turning the snow gold and orange, the sky above jet black and blazing with stars. Nine teams of ten waited at the start line in bright clusters. And then, at the very end, three creatures: one lanky goose, one very small blue penguin, and one enthusiastic Rockhopper penguin with a baton that he was already spinning like a baton twirler, badly.

"Finnegan, stop spinning the baton," said Gideon.

"It looks cool."

"It looks dangerous."

"It looks *fun*."

"Stop."

Finnegan stopped.

The start horn sounded.

What happened next, everyone who was there would describe differently. Team Powder went out fast and efficient, their ten racers clicking through the relay handoffs like a well-oiled machine. The other teams spread out along the circuit in overlapping waves.

And Team Tiny went *first*. Gideon had put this in the strategy: start first, run the full circuit, and when you reach the handoff zone, don't stop — use the baton as a second leg marker, meaning each racer would run the full course alone and tag themselves.

It was technically legal. Herschel had confirmed this, looking slightly pained.

Gideon took the first circuit at an efficient, metronomic pace — he was not the fastest thing on the slope, but he was precise and he knew every curve of the circuit. He kept the baton steady. He didn't fall. When he came through the handoff zone, Willa was ready.

Now — here is the thing about Willa Wobble.

She was small. Very small. Her legs were short. Her board was tiny. She carved in tight, efficient arcs that the larger racers couldn't match because they were simply too big to bend the course the same way. In the stretches where the circuit curved sharply left and right through a gate sequence, Willa was through and gone before Team Powder's fourth racer had found the right angle.

"Is the tiny penguin actually—" said someone in the crowd.

"She is," said Bjorn.

"How is she—"

"Very precise ankles," said Bjorn.

Willa handed off to Finnegan with Team Tiny in second place, half a circuit behind Team Powder.

Finnegan grabbed the baton.

He looked at the circuit ahead. He looked at the sky. He looked at Gideon, who gave him one single nod.

"WOOOHOOOOO—" Finnegan launched himself down the slope with a sound that echoed off the basin walls and made the torches flicker.

He was fast. He was chaotic. He fell twice and recovered both times with the specific momentum of someone whose falling style is actually forward progress in disguise. He almost clipped a gate marker, yelled "SORRY, GATE!" at it while skiing past. He came through the narrow section where other racers had to brake and just didn't brake at all, which was technically incorrect form and absolutely worked.

He crossed the finish line thirty seconds after Team Powder's last racer.

Team Tiny finished second.

Out of nine teams.

With three racers.

The crowd was silent for about two seconds. Then it erupted.

Sverre the snow leopard skied over, expression changed entirely, and extended a paw. "That," he said, "was not what I expected."

"No," said Gideon cheerfully. "It usually isn't."

Willa sat in the snow, legs extended, completely exhausted and completely content. "Small and fast," she said, looking at her tiny board. "They're actually a good team."

"The smallest step," said Gideon, sitting beside her, "is still a step."

Willa smiled. "You've been talking to me too much."

"Probably," said Gideon. "It's working."

Finnegan appeared between them, baton raised, crest lopsided, absolutely covered in snow. "WE GOT SECOND. THAT IS VERY GOOD. I HAVE STRONG FEELINGS ABOUT THIS."

"We know," said Gideon.

"I MAY CRY."

"That's allowed," said Willa.

Under the aurora, the Moon Basin blazed with torches and victory and the specific joy of people who were supposed to lose and didn't, which is the sweetest kind of joy there is.
