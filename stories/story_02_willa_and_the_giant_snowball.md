# Story 2: Willa and the Giant Snowball
### *Inspired by David and Goliath — 1 Samuel 17*
**Characters:** Willa Wobble, Aurora Frostholm, Bjorn Bigpaws, and Grumble the Colossal Snow Creature
**Setting:** The Glitter Gully, The Peak of Plenty
**Theme:** Courage, faith over size, the heart that doesn't quit

---

> *Settle in. Pull your blanket up. This is a story about the smallest penguin in the valley — and why smallest doesn't mean least.*

---

## Opening Wink

If courage were measured in inches, Willa would still break the mountain’s scoreboard.

## The Story

**Willa Wobble** was the tiniest penguin in all of Frostpeak Valley.

We are talking *tiny*. She was so small that her purple snowboard was barely bigger than a dinner tray. She was so small that her lavender scarf — knitted by her grandmother, who loved her dearly — wrapped around her neck four times and still had enough left over to trip on. She was so small that when she stood next to Bjorn Bigpaws, she looked like a footnote next to a very large book.

But Willa had the most powerful heart in the valley.

She just didn't know it yet.

---

One winter morning, the animals of Frostpeak Valley woke to a terrible sound.

It was a sound like boulders being knocked together, like an avalanche deciding to take a nap, like thunder that had eaten too much for breakfast. It rattled the Cocoa Warming Hut windows. It shook the pine trees in the Deep Powder Forest. It sent little cascades of snow sliding off every rooftop in the valley.

*KRRRR-BOOOOOM.*

Everyone gathered at the base of Glitter Gully — the sparkling ravine where frozen waterfalls made rainbow prisms on sunny days — and stared upward.

There, blocking the entire entrance to the Gully, was **Grumble**.

Grumble was a snow creature. Not a snowman — snowmen were friendly and wore scarves and had carrot noses and smiled. Grumble was something else entirely. He was a towering mass of packed ice and ancient snow, as tall as four polar bears stacked up, with icicle fingers as long as ski poles and a face like a cliff that had decided to be annoyed. His eyes were two frozen blue stones. His voice, when he spoke, sounded like a mountain talking.

"GLITTER GULLY," boomed Grumble, "IS **MINE** NOW. NO MORE RACING. NO MORE CARVING. NO MORE OF YOUR SILLY GLITTERY PRISM NONSENSE. I HAVE MOVED IN AND I AM NOT LEAVING."

The animals of Frostpeak Valley looked at each other.

Bjorn stepped forward — he was the biggest, after all. He adjusted his ear (the bent one) with dignity, puffed up his chest to its most impressive size, and said, in his most imposing polar bear voice:

"Now see here—"

Grumble flicked one enormous icicle finger and Bjorn's thermos went flying into a snowdrift thirty feet away.

Bjorn retrieved his thermos. He stood very still. "I see," he said quietly, and stepped back.

Aurora tried next. She whooshed up on her iridescent skis, laughing, her eyes bright as ice.

"Hey, big fella! Race you to the bottom of — "

Grumble blew a breath like a blizzard and Aurora's skis got stuck in a sudden sheet of fresh ice.

"Hmm," said Aurora, still smiling, but with slightly more respect.

One by one, the animals tried. The snow foxes were too scared. Finnegan tried a running charge and tripped over his own snowboard before getting anywhere near Grumble. Barnaby offered snacks, which Grumble knocked away with a BOOM that sent fish crackers raining over half the valley.

Nobody could move him.

Nobody could even get close.

The animals sat in a dejected huddle at the base of the Gully, and the prism rainbows that usually danced across the snow were gone, blocked by Grumble's enormous shadow.

Then a very small voice said:

*"I'll go."*

Everyone turned.

Willa Wobble stood at the back of the crowd, lavender scarf wrapped four times around her neck, purple board under her arm, brown eyes perfectly steady.

The animals stared at her.

"Willa," said Aurora gently, "he's... rather large."

"Yes," said Willa.

"And you're rather..." Aurora tried to find a kind word.

"Small," said Willa. "I know." She adjusted her scarf. *"The smallest step is still a step."*

Bjorn put one large paw on her shoulder. "Little one, you don't have to—"

"I know I don't have to," said Willa. "I *want* to." She looked up at him with those steady, quiet eyes. "Will you trust me?"

Bjorn looked at her for a long moment. Then he stepped aside.

---

Willa didn't charge at Grumble. She didn't shout or posture or try to look bigger than she was.

She *studied* him.

She walked slowly around the edge of the Gully — very small, very quiet, very careful — watching the way the ice had formed in his body. She noticed something. Where the frozen waterfall had once flowed, there was a seam — a thin line in the ice running from the ground all the way up to just beneath Grumble's left shoulder. Old ice meets new ice. A crack that hadn't fully sealed.

Willa reached into her vest pocket and pulled out five smooth, perfectly round snowballs she had been keeping cold there all morning. She had packed them herself, dense and precise. Her grandmother had taught her: *A small thing, thrown with care, can do what a large thing thrown with fury cannot.*

She took a deep breath.

She wound back her tiny flipper.

The first snowball hit the seam.

A sound like a whisper. A hairline crack appeared.

Grumble looked down. "...what was—"

*Thwack.* Second snowball. Right on the crack. It spread an inch.

"HEY—"

*Thwack. Thwack.*

The crack raced upward like lightning through the ice — up Grumble's left side, across his shoulder, and with a sound like a thousand icicles all deciding to laugh at once—

**CRASH.**

Grumble's left side calved off like a glacier, tumbling into a sparkling heap of harmless ice chunks that scattered across the snow and immediately caught the morning light and threw rainbow prisms in every direction.

What remained was much smaller. Still grumbly. But smaller.

And somehow, in the explosion of light and ice, Grumble looked... confused. He looked down at his own reduced self. He looked at the tiny penguin in the lavender scarf standing in front of him, snowball still in flipper.

*"...how,"* he said, in a voice no longer like boulders — more like one boulder, sitting quietly.

"I looked for the truth in you," said Willa simply. "And the truth was that you weren't as solid as you seemed."

Grumble was quiet for a long time.

Then, in a voice very small for something his size, he said: *"I'm... lonely, I think. That's mostly why I was being so dreadful."*

Willa sat down in the snow beside what was left of him. "Would you like to have some cocoa?"

She didn't say *I told you so.* She didn't take a victory lap. She just sat.

The prisms came back across the snow, dancing, glittering, lighting up the whole Gully.

Bjorn, watching from a distance with his thermos halfway to his lips, said quietly: *"Extraordinary."*

---

That afternoon, Grumble — considerably smaller now, and wearing one of Barnaby's spare scarves — sat at the bottom of Glitter Gully and cheered for every animal who came carving through the prisms.

He turned out to be an excellent cheerer.

And the tiniest penguin in the valley? She went back to the top of the Gully, strapped on her tiny purple board, and dropped in with perfect, quiet precision.

Not the loudest. Not the biggest.

Just the truest.
