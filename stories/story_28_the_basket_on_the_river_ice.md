# Story 28: The Basket on the River Ice
### *Inspired by Baby Moses — Exodus 2*
**Characters:** Nora Snowmane, Mina Muskrat, Willa Wobble  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Riverglass Channel  
**Theme:** Gentle courage and protection

---

## Opening Wink

Some treasures wash up on shore. Some float down a frozen river in a birch-bark basket. Tonight, Frostpeak Valley finds the best treasure of all.

## The Story

The morning started the way all important mornings do in Frostpeak Valley -- quietly.

Mina Muskrat was out early on the Riverglass Channel, skating slow, lazy loops in the blue-gray dawn. Mina was not a fast skater or a fancy one. She just liked the sound her blades made on the ice -- *shhhk, shhhk, shhhk* -- like the river was whispering secrets meant only for her.

The channel wound through the heart of the valley like a silver ribbon, frozen solid in winter but still moving underneath, a secret current murmuring below the glass. Frost ferns grew along the banks, and the pine trees on either side were so heavy with snow they looked like old grandmothers in white shawls, hunched and wise.

Mina was mid-loop when she saw it.

Something was drifting along the edge of the ice where the channel narrowed and the current pushed slush up against the banks. It was a basket -- a little birch-bark basket, round as a soup bowl, lined with what looked like the softest moss in the valley. And inside the basket, wrapped in a tiny blue blanket, was a penguin chick.

Mina stopped so fast her skates threw up a spray of ice crystals.

"Oh my whiskers," she breathed.

The chick was impossibly small -- no bigger than Mina's two paws held together. Its downy feathers were still baby-gray, and its eyes were closed, and it was making the tiniest sound Mina had ever heard, a little *peep peep peep* that was more vibration than voice, like a teacup trying to sing.

"Oh my *whiskers*," Mina said again, because some situations require saying it twice.

She knelt on the ice and very, very carefully lifted the basket out of the slush. The chick's eyes opened -- two bright dark beads that looked up at Mina with an expression of absolute trust, as if floating down an icy river in a basket was a perfectly normal way to spend a morning and Mina was exactly the person it had been expecting to meet.

"Where did you come from, little one?" Mina whispered.

The chick peeped.

Mina tucked the basket inside her coat, pressing it against her warm belly, and skated as fast as her legs could carry her toward the lodge.

She burst through the door of the cocoa lodge just as Nora Snowmane was settling her enormous polar-bear frame into her favorite chair by the fire. Herschel was behind the counter, grinding cocoa beans. Willa Wobble was studying a map in the corner, her tiny blue-penguin body almost hidden behind the paper.

"I found something," Mina panted. "On the river ice. In a basket."

She opened her coat, and the chick peeped.

Every head in the lodge turned.

"Oh," said Nora Snowmane, and her voice went soft as fresh snowfall. The great polar bear rose from her chair and crossed the room in two steps, and when she looked down into the basket, something shifted in her face -- something deep and old and fierce and tender all at once, like a lantern being lit inside her chest.

"A baby," Nora whispered. "A little penguin baby."

"Who would put a baby in a basket on the river?" Willa asked, climbing onto a stool to see. Her round eyes went wide behind her spectacles. "Oh, it's so *small*."

"Maybe someone who couldn't keep it safe themselves," Herschel said quietly from behind the counter. The old walrus set down his cocoa grinder and came around to look. "Sometimes the bravest thing a parent can do is trust that someone else will find what they love most."

They all stood there for a moment, looking at the tiny chick in its birch-bark boat, and the lodge was very quiet except for the crackle of the fire and the soft *peep peep peep* of a brand-new life asking for warmth.

"It needs milk," said Nora. "Warm milk with a drop of honey. And a proper blanket. And a name."

"I have extra blankets in the back," Herschel offered, already moving.

"I can make a formula," said Mina. "My aunt taught me -- warm milk, a pinch of ground fish meal, a tiny bit of mashed berry for sweetness."

"And I," said Willa, pushing her spectacles up her beak, "will check the records to see if any families upriver have reported a missing chick. Just in case."

They worked together like a team that had been doing this forever. Mina heated milk on Herschel's stove, testing the temperature on her wrist the way her grandmother had taught her. Herschel produced blankets so soft they might have been made from clouds. Willa sent messages upriver by way of the mail-hawk network. And Nora -- Nora simply held the baby.

She held it against her broad, warm chest, where her heartbeat was steady and slow as a drum. The chick stopped peeping almost immediately. It nuzzled into her fur, found a warm spot between her paws, and let out a sigh so tiny it was basically just a thought.

"There now," Nora murmured. "There now, little one. You're safe."

By midmorning, word had spread through the valley. Piper Paddlefoot showed up with a pair of the tiniest booties anyone had ever seen -- "I knitted them last winter and never knew why," she said, looking amazed. Barnaby arrived with a basket of fish crackers and the announcement that he was "available for uncle duty, effective immediately." Cleo came with her paints and quietly began working on a portrait of the chick, capturing the exact shade of its downy fuzz.

Finnegan burst through the door last, covered in snow, one ski on and one ski missing. "I heard there's a baby! Is it true? Can I hold it? I promise I won't drop it. I only drop things that aren't alive. Usually."

"Maybe later, Finnegan," Nora said gently.

"Fair," said Finnegan.

As the day went on, Willa's messages came back one by one. No one upriver had reported a missing chick. No one downstream either. Wherever this little penguin had come from, the trail had vanished like footprints in a blizzard.

"What do we do?" Mina asked, sitting by the fire with her skates still on.

Nora looked down at the chick sleeping in her arms. It had one tiny flipper wrapped around her claw, holding on with a grip that seemed impossible for something so small.

"We keep it," Nora said. Not loudly. Not with drama. Just the way you'd say *the sun is up* or *the snow is falling*. A fact. A certainty. A thing that was already true.

"Are you sure?" Willa asked. "You already look after Teddy and Tilda. That's a lot of cubs and chicks for one bear."

Nora smiled. "My heart has room. It always has room."

"What about a name?" Piper asked.

Everyone started talking at once.

"Snowflake!" suggested Finnegan.

"River!" offered Mina.

"Bartholomew!" said Barnaby. Everyone looked at him. "What? It's a distinguished name."

"How about Pip?" said Willa quietly.

The room went still. The chick made a sound -- not quite a peep, more like a *pip* -- and everyone laughed.

"Pip," said Nora, testing the word. "Pip Snowmane."

The chick opened one eye, looked directly at Nora, and pipped again, louder this time, as if to say: *Yes, that's me. That's my name. Can I go back to sleep now?*

And so Pip Snowmane came to Frostpeak Valley -- not by the slopes or the sky or the well-worn trails, but by the water, in a birch-bark basket lined with moss, carried by a current that knew exactly where it was going even if nobody else did.

That evening, the whole valley gathered at the lodge. Herschel made his best cocoa -- the recipe with the cinnamon and the tiny marshmallows shaped like stars. Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff sat on either side of their mother, peering at the new arrival with enormous eyes.

"It's very small," said Teddy.

"It's very loud," said Tilda, as Pip let out a mighty peep.

"It's very ours," said Nora.

Bjorn raised his mug. "To Pip," he said. "May the valley always be your home."

"To Mina," added Herschel, "who was skating at just the right place at just the right time."

Mina blushed so hard her whiskers trembled.

Outside, the Riverglass Channel glinted in the starlight, still and silver, keeping its secrets under the ice. And in Nora Snowmane's arms, wrapped in the softest blanket in the valley, Pip Snowmane slept the deep, perfect sleep of someone who has been found by exactly the right family at exactly the right time.

Willa Wobble wrote one last line in her notebook before closing it for the night: *Sometimes the river knows things we don't. Trust the current.*

Then she finished her cocoa, climbed down from her stool, and waddled home through the softly falling snow.
