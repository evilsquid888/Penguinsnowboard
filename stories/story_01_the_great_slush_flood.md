### *Inspired by Noah's Ark — Genesis 6–9*
**Characters:** Piper Paddlefoot, Bjorn Bigpaws, Teddy & Tilda Powderpuff, and many friends
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, The Peak of Plenty, Frozen Mirror Lake

## The Story

High above Frostpeak Valley, where the clouds bumped gently against the mountaintops like sleepy pillows, there lived a penguin named **Piper Paddlefoot**.

Piper was the most adventurous boarder on the whole mountain. Every morning before the sun was fully awake, she would strap on her glittery blue snowboard, pull her goggles up onto her forehead — never over her eyes, because that would make *too* much sense — and shout, *"Slide into it!"* before launching herself down whatever slope looked most thrillingly terrifying.

She was very good at starting things.

She was slightly less good at planning ahead.

One spring morning — and in Frostpeak Valley, spring was sneaky — Piper noticed something odd. The snow on the lower slopes had gone a little... *mushy*. Squishier than usual. When she carved a turn near the Cocoa Warming Hut, her board sank an inch deeper than it should have.

"Hm," said Piper. She poked the snow with her flipper. It went *squish*.

Old Herschel the Walrus looked up from behind the cocoa counter. Herschel had enormous bristly whiskers and wore a red knitted hat that had once been a much larger hat before it shrank in the wash.

"Notice something?" he asked.

"The snow is... melting?" said Piper.

Herschel nodded slowly. "Been watching the ridgeline for three days. You see those clouds stacked up behind the Eastern Peak?" He pointed with a ladle. "That's not a regular snowstorm, little one. That's a *slush storm*. A big one. All that snow up top is going to melt and come rushing down within a week, and this whole lower valley is going to become a lake."

Piper's eyes went wide. "A lake?!"

"A *very* wet lake," said Herschel, and went back to stirring.

Piper didn't waste a single second. She pushed off with her back foot and sped up the mountain to find **Bjorn Bigpaws**, because if anyone could help with a Very Large Problem, it was Bjorn.

She found him on the intermediate slope, skiing in long, dignified S-curves, nose held high.

"**Bjorn!**" Piper skidded to a stop in a spray of soft snow.

Bjorn raised one enormous eyebrow. "Must you always arrive like a small explosion?"

"The valley is going to flood!" said Piper. "Herschel says the slush storm will melt everything and Frozen Mirror Lake will overflow and — Bjorn, we have to do something!"

Bjorn stood very still. He looked at the clouds behind the Eastern Peak. He looked at the slushy snow around his skis. He unscrewed his thermos, took one slow sip of chamomile tea, and said:

"We will need a very large raft."

---

And so the building began.

Piper and Bjorn told everyone in the valley. Some animals laughed. The ice-skating otters said, *"Slush storm? We love slush!"* The snow foxes said, *"We'll just climb higher — don't be so dramatic."* Even Finnegan Flipsworth said, *"WOOOHOOO, a flood sounds AWESOME — wait, should I be scared?"*

But Piper kept working.

They gathered the sturdiest fallen pine logs from the Deep Powder Forest. Bjorn carried three logs at a time on his great wide shoulders. Cleo Coldwater designed the whole thing in her sketchbook — a broad, beautiful raft with high curved sides and a little flag on top (Cleo insisted on the flag). Barnaby Beaksworth brought snacks so nobody got hangry. Willa Wobble tied every knot, because her tiny flippers were the most precise.

The twin bear cubs, **Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff**, were supposed to be helping by carrying small logs.

They were mostly riding the small logs like tiny surfboards.

"Teddy, that log is load-bearing," said Willa patiently.

"It's also FUN-bearing!" said Teddy.

"We didn't mean to—" said Tilda.

"—but it was worth it," they finished together.

Seven days they worked. Seven sunsets painted the sky in pink and gold over Frostpeak Valley, and seven times Piper fell asleep exhausted and woke up ready to do it again, because she *trusted* what old Herschel had told her, even when others didn't.

On the seventh morning, the clouds behind the Eastern Peak turned purple-grey.

And then they heard it.

A deep, low rumble. Like the whole mountain clearing its throat.

"EVERYONE ON THE RAFT!" shouted Piper.

And they came — the penguins and the polar bears, the ice-skating otters (who admitted maybe this was a little serious), the snow foxes (who had climbed higher but found it very cold and lonely up there), and even a pair of bewildered arctic hares who had simply been passing through and got swept up in the whole affair.

Bjorn lifted the smallest animals aboard in one sweep of his great arms. Finnegan tripped getting on and knocked four penguins sideways and apologized to all of them simultaneously. Cleo grabbed her sketchbook. Barnaby made sure the snacks were loaded last — *obviously* the most important cargo.

Then the slush came.

It wasn't terrifying — it was more like the mountain taking a very large, very warm bath. Water and soft snow rushed down the slopes in rivers, filling the valley below, rising and rising until Frozen Mirror Lake was no longer a lake but the center of a great, still, perfectly calm sea.

And their raft — creaky and slightly lopsided and decorated with Cleo's flag — **floated**.

---

They drifted for three days and three nights.

At night, the northern lights came out and danced above them — green and violet and gold — and all the animals lay on their backs watching, not afraid at all. Barnaby handed out cocoa (he had packed an unreasonable amount of cocoa and everyone was grateful). Teddy and Tilda fell asleep holding flippers.

On the third morning, Piper climbed to the top of the raft's little flag-pole and looked out.

*There it was.*

The Peak of Plenty, dry and shining, a brand-new ribbon of untouched snow curling down from its summit like a gift.

"LAND!" she shrieked, and dove flat onto her belly in pure excitement, which is how penguins celebrate good news.

The raft grounded gently on fresh powder. Everyone stepped off onto snow that had never been carved before — perfectly smooth, impossibly white, glittering like someone had shaken a jar of stars onto it.

Bjorn took a very slow breath. His eyes were wet, but polar bears sometimes said that was just the cold air.

*"Beautiful,"* he said quietly.

Cleo already had her sketchbook open.

Piper stood at the top of the new run, her glittery blue board strapped to her feet, goggles pushed up on her head, looking out over a valley reborn and gleaming and *theirs*.

She grinned her biggest grin.

*"Slide into it,"* she whispered.

And she did.

## Cinematic Prompt Enhancers (Phase 3)

Use these add-ons with every image/video prompt in this story to keep visuals consistent and lively.

- **Character Lock:** Keep **Piper + Bjorn** visually consistent across shots (same outfit colors, board/ski design, and size relationships).
- **Style Lock:** cozy storybook fantasy, soft volumetric snowfall, aurora glow, warm rim-light, expressive faces, playful motion arcs.
- **Camera Mix:** include at least one **wide establishing shot**, one **medium character performance shot**, and one **low-angle action shot**.
- **Motion Cues:** snow spray, scarf flutter, tiny slips + recoveries, joyful group reactions.
- **Kid-Safe Tone:** wonder + comedy, never frightening; challenges feel adventurous, not scary.
- **Negative Prompt (optional):** no horror, no photoreal harshness, no gloomy desaturated palette, no weapons, no realistic peril.
