# Story 85: Fruit of the Snow Spirit
### *Inspired by Fruit of the Spirit — Galatians 5*
**Characters:** Frida Fox, Willa Wobble  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Orchard Iceway  
**Theme:** Character that grows

---

## Opening Wink

If moonbeams had mittens, this would be the night they came out to clap for the valley.

## The Story

**Frida Fox** had been keeping a journal all winter, and what she'd noticed was this: character grew like a tree, not like a snowdrift.

Snowdrifts appeared overnight. One morning, nothing; next morning, a remarkable pile of snow taller than you were. Character didn't work like that. You didn't wake up one day suddenly patient, or kind, or at peace. It grew in layers, slowly, sometimes invisibly, requiring good roots and regular seasons and occasionally a hard winter that turned out to strengthen things more than the easy years had.

Frida was a red fox who was thoughtful in the systematic way of someone who takes notes about their own life. She had been at Frostpeak Valley for two winters. She was training with **Willa Wobble** on the Orchard Iceway — a long, winding course through the valley's ancient orchard on the southeast slope, where the old apple and pear trees grew in rows, their branches bent into beautiful ice-sculpture shapes each winter.

One day, skating the Orchard Iceway with Willa, Frida told her about the journal.

"I've been trying to grow specific things," said Frida. "Love, joy, peace, patience — I listed them out. I'm trying to practice each one."

Willa skated beside her, quiet for a moment.

"What does 'practice love' look like?" she said.

Frida consulted her mental notes. "I've been making a point to notice when someone needs something and doing the thing. Even small things."

"And is it working?"

"I think so. Yesterday I helped Wida Wren carry her supplies up the hill without being asked, and it felt — easy. Natural. It used to feel more effortful."

"That's the root deepening," said Willa. "The first times you practice anything, it's effortful. When the root goes deep, it becomes part of how you move."

---

They skated through the orchard as the afternoon light came at a low angle, turning the ice-branched trees into something from a dream. Frida asked Willa about patience, which was the one she found hardest.

"I'm not patient by nature," Frida admitted. "I notice when things are wrong. I want them fixed quickly."

"What have you tried?"

"I've been practicing pausing before I respond when I'm frustrated. Counting to five."

"How's that going?"

"Sometimes I get to three," said Frida.

Willa laughed. "Three is more than zero."

"It doesn't feel like much."

"Frida," said Willa, "the tree doesn't look different between day one and day five. It looks different between year one and year five. Three counts is a root deepening."

They came around the far bend of the Orchard Iceway where the oldest apple tree stood — the one Cleo always sketched, the one that was perfectly round in summer and perfectly sculpted in ice in winter, that had been growing in the orchard for sixty years.

"How long do you think this took?" said Willa, looking at the tree.

Frida thought about it. "Sixty years of slow."

"Sixty years of slow," Willa agreed. "Good roots, regular seasons, some hard winters, some good ones. Now it's the most beautiful tree in the orchard and every creature in the valley knows it."

Frida looked at the tree for a long moment.

"You're saying I'm at year two."

"I'm saying you're at year two. Which is still years ahead of year one, and decades before year sixty." Willa pushed off. "Also: you noticed Wida Wren's supplies without being asked. That's not nothing. That's the tree making its first recognizable branch."

Frida skated after her.

She thought about her journal entries. The small daily things: the pauses, the noticings, the three-counts of patience, the moments of choosing kindness over convenience. None of them dramatic. All of them real.

By the time they came back around to the start of the Orchard Iceway, the sun was setting and the tree-shadows were long and blue across the snow and Frida thought: *next winter I will be a little further along than this winter.* Not there. Just further.

That was enough. That was, in fact, exactly right.

Character that grows is patient with its own growing. It keeps the roots down and waits for the seasons to do their work.
