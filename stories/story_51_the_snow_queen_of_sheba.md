# Story 51: The Snow Queen of Sheba
### *Inspired by Queen of Sheba Visits Solomon — 1 Kings 10*
**Characters:** Sheba Seal, Cleo Coldwater  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Crystal Court  
**Theme:** Curiosity and learning

---

## Opening Wink

Word travels fast across frozen valleys -- especially when the word is "someone extraordinary is coming," and ESPECIALLY when that someone travels by narwhal-drawn ice barge.

## The Story

The letter arrived on a Tuesday, tied to the leg of a very cold, very dramatic seagull who swooped through the window of Herschel's cocoa shop and landed beak-first in a bowl of whipped cream.

"MESSAGE!" the seagull sputtered, shaking cream off its wings. "FROM THE FARAWAY VALLEY OF SHIMMER GLACIER!"

Herschel untied the tiny scroll with his great walrus flippers, which took considerable effort and patience and only knocked over two mugs. He unrolled it, squinted, and read aloud:

*"To the creatures of Frostpeak Valley: Her Royal Frostiness, Queen Sheba of Shimmer Glacier, has heard tales of your valley's wisdom and wonders. She will arrive in three days to see for herself. Sincerely, The Royal Seal (who is an actual seal)."*

The cocoa shop exploded into chaos.

"A QUEEN?" shrieked Finnegan Flipsworth, toppling backward off his stool. "An actual queen? Do we BOW? Do we CURTSY? I don't know how to curtsy -- my knees bend the wrong way!"

"Everyone's knees bend the wrong way when they curtsy," Barnaby Beaksworth said wisely, then added, "Do we have enough fish crackers for a queen?"

Cleo Coldwater's golden macaroni crest was practically vibrating with excitement. "I've heard of Shimmer Glacier! They say Queen Sheba is the most brilliant, curious, well-traveled creature in all the frozen lands. She's visited a hundred valleys! She's solved riddles that stumped entire mountain ranges!"

"And she's coming HERE," Willa Wobble said softly from her perch on the sugar bowl. "To see if Frostpeak is as special as everyone says."

A nervous silence fell. Because here was the thing about Frostpeak Valley: it was wonderful, yes. It had the best cocoa (Herschel's), the prettiest slopes (everyone agreed), and the kindest creatures (on most days). But it was also small, and cozy, and a little bit messy. The lodge had a wobbly step. The trail markers were mostly just Finnegan's scarves tied to sticks. The Crystal Court -- their grandest gathering place, an open-air oval of natural ice surrounded by pillars of frozen crystal -- had a crack running through the middle that everyone just politely skied around.

"We need to make everything PERFECT," Piper Paddlefoot declared, slamming her flipper on the table with the confidence of a penguin who has never once done anything perfectly in her life.

And so the Great Frostpeak Spruce-Up began.

For three days, the valley was a blur of activity. Bjorn Bigpaws personally polished every crystal pillar in the court until they shone like diamonds. Barnaby organized a snack table so elaborate it had its own ZIP CODE. Cleo painted a welcome banner that was genuinely beautiful -- sweeping letters in ice-blue and gold that read WELCOME, QUEEN SHEBA in script so elegant it made Finnegan cry a little.

"I'm not crying," Finnegan said, wiping his eyes. "It's the wind."

There was no wind. They were indoors.

Magnus Meltsworth was put in charge of fixing the wobbly lodge step. He grumbled the entire time. "Fool step," he muttered. "Fool visitors. Fool everything." Then he fixed it so perfectly that it didn't even creak, and he grumbled about that too.

Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff were assigned to "decoration duty," which mostly meant they threw glitter at everything and each other until Nora Snowmane gently redirected them to the garland-hanging crew, where they could throw glitter at things that WANTED to be sparkly.

On the morning of the third day, the valley held its breath.

She arrived at noon, gliding across the frozen lake on an ice barge carved from a single piece of blue glacier crystal. It was pulled by six silver-scaled fish, and at the prow stood a seal in a tiny top hat who announced, "HER ROYAL FROSTINESS, QUEEN SHEBA OF SHIMMER GLACIER!"

And there she was.

Queen Sheba was a harbor seal, but not like any seal the valley had ever seen. Her fur shimmered like it had been dusted with starlight. She wore a cape of woven snowflake-silk that trailed behind her like a whisper. Her eyes were dark, bright, and curious -- the kind of eyes that didn't just look AT things but looked INTO them.

She was also, everyone noticed, smiling. Not a polite, royal, I'm-being-nice-because-I-have-to smile. A real one. Warm and wide and a little bit mischievous.

"So," Queen Sheba said, looking around at the polished pillars, the elaborate snack table, the sparkling garlands, and the entire nervous population of Frostpeak Valley standing in a wobbly line. "This is the famous Frostpeak. Show me everything."

Cleo Coldwater stepped forward first, because curiosity recognizes curiosity. "Your Frostiness, may I show you the Crystal Court?"

"Please," said Sheba. "And call me Sheba. 'Your Frostiness' makes me sound like a popsicle."

Cleo walked her through the court, pointing out how the crystal pillars caught the light at different angles throughout the day, creating tiny rainbows that danced across the ice. Sheba listened. She asked questions -- not polite, surface questions, but deep, interested ones. "Why does this pillar sing when the wind hits it?" "How old is this ice?" "Who discovered that crack in the center?"

"Oh, the crack," Cleo said, flushing. "We tried to fix it, but--"

"Don't fix it," Sheba said, kneeling to trace the crack with one flipper. "Look. The ice crystals grew differently along the fault line. They're shaped like tiny flowers. You can only see them because the ice broke. It's the most beautiful thing here."

Cleo stared. Then she pulled out her sketchbook and started drawing, because Sheba was RIGHT, and Cleo had skated past that crack a thousand times without ever really looking.

Next, Herschel served his finest cocoa. Sheba took one sip, closed her eyes, and said nothing for a full thirty seconds. Herschel almost fainted from anxiety.

"This," Sheba finally said, "is the best cocoa I have ever tasted. And I have tasted cocoa in a hundred valleys."

Herschel's whiskers trembled with joy. "It's the cinnamon," he whispered. "I add it last. Most people add it first."

"Most people are wrong," said Sheba.

She watched the cubs ski. She watched Piper snowboard down Ridgeback Run, shouting "SLIDE INTO IT!" the whole way. She watched Finnegan attempt a triple backflip, crash into a snowbank, pop out covered in powder, and take a bow as if he'd meant to do it. She laughed so hard her cape fell off.

But the moment everyone remembered most came at the end of the day.

Willa Wobble had been quiet the whole visit, which was normal for Willa. She'd been watching from the edges, too small to be seen in the crowd. But as Sheba was preparing to leave, she spotted the tiny blue penguin sitting alone on a frozen log, gazing at the sunset.

Sheba waddled over and sat beside her. "You've been quiet," she said.

"I'm always quiet," Willa said. "I was watching you."

"What did you see?"

Willa thought for a moment. "I saw someone who asks questions instead of giving answers. I think that's why you're wise. Not because you know everything, but because you want to."

Sheba was still. The sunset painted them both in shades of peach and gold. Then the queen smiled -- not her wide, mischievous smile, but something softer. Something surprised.

"In a hundred valleys," Sheba said, "no one has ever told me that. They all show me their biggest slopes, their fanciest lodges, their most impressive tricks. But this valley showed me a crack in the ice that grows flowers, cocoa made with love and cinnamon, a rockhopper who turns crashes into bows, and the smallest penguin who sees the deepest things." She shook her head slowly. "What they said about Frostpeak Valley was wrong."

Willa's heart sank. "Wrong?"

"Yes," said Sheba. "They said it was wonderful. It's not wonderful. It's EXTRAORDINARY."

From the frozen pond, Captain Prism surfaced with a magnificent splash. "DID SOMEONE SAY EXTRAORDINARY? I AM ALSO EXTRAORDINARY! OBSERVE MY HORN!" He struck a pose. A small fish fell off his tusk. Nobody minded.

As the ice barge pulled away under a sky full of stars, Sheba waved from the prow. "I'll be back!" she called across the glittering water. "And I'm bringing my whole court! They need to try that cocoa!"

Herschel immediately began planning a bigger pot.

That night, the friends gathered in the lodge. The fire crackled. The cocoa steamed. Cleo was still sketching the ice-crack flowers. Finnegan was still telling everyone about how the queen had laughed at his crash, as if he'd planned it all along.

"She wasn't impressed by the polished pillars or the fancy snack table," Piper said thoughtfully.

"She was impressed by us just being US," Barnaby agreed, munching a fish cracker.

Willa smiled from her blanket pile. "The best thing you can show anyone," she said sleepily, "is the truth about who you are. Even the cracks. Especially the cracks."

One by one, the lanterns dimmed. One by one, the friends closed their eyes. And somewhere across the frozen water, a queen was already dreaming of coming back.
