# Story 45: Naaman's Seven Splashes
### *Inspired by Naaman Healed — 2 Kings 5*
**Characters:** Nemo Narwhal, Magnus Meltsworth  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Jordan Pool  
**Theme:** Humility heals

---

## Opening Wink

If pride were a snowball, tonight's story would roll it right down the hill and into a very cold pond.

## The Story

Magnus Meltsworth was the finest skier in all of Frostpeak Valley, and he wanted to make sure everybody knew it.

He had seventeen trophies lined up on his mantelpiece. He had a custom pair of skis with his name engraved in silver. He had a scarf so long and so magnificently red that it trailed behind him on the slopes like the tail of a very fashionable comet. When Magnus carved a turn, snow sprayed in a perfect arc that caught the light and turned into tiny rainbows, and he would glance over his shoulder to make sure at least three creatures were watching.

"Did you see that?" he would ask, even though everyone always saw it because Magnus only performed where there was an audience.

So when Magnus woke up one Tuesday morning with a strange, itchy, splotchy rash of bright purple polka dots covering his entire left arm, he was not pleased.

"WHAT," Magnus bellowed, staring at his arm, "IS THIS."

The dots were vivid. They were unmistakable. They clashed horribly with his red scarf. And worst of all, they itched like he'd rolled in pine needles and then hugged a cactus.

He tried hiding them under his ski jacket. The dots glowed right through the fabric. He tried covering them with snow. They melted the snow on contact and somehow got brighter. He tried pretending they weren't there, which worked for about four minutes until Teddy Powderpuff pointed at him and shouted, "MR. MAGNUS, YOU'RE PURPLE!"

"I am NOT purple," Magnus growled. "I am dignified."

"You're purple AND dignified!" Tilda Powderpuff added helpfully.

Magnus stomped to Herschel the Walrus's cocoa shop, because Herschel was the valley elder and knew something about everything. The old walrus peered at Magnus's arm through his spectacles, twirled one magnificent whisker, and said, "Hmm. Frost-Spot Rash. Very rare. Very stubborn."

"Fix it," Magnus demanded.

"Oh, I can't fix it," Herschel said, pouring himself a leisurely cup of peppermint cocoa. "But I know who can help. Go see little Willa Wobble down at Jordan Pool."

Magnus stared at him. "*Willa Wobble?* The tiny penguin? She's barely tall enough to see over a snowdrift!"

"She's also the only creature in this valley who's studied the old frost remedies," Herschel said mildly. "But suit yourself. I'm sure the purple will match your spring wardrobe nicely."

Magnus grumbled all the way to Jordan Pool, which sat in a quiet clearing surrounded by snow-dusted pine trees. The pool was spring-fed and never fully froze, though thin lace-patterns of ice crept around its edges like doilies on a grandmother's table. Willa Wobble stood at the bank, her tiny blue frame barely visible against the snow, organizing a collection of smooth stones by size.

"I've been told," Magnus announced, looming over her like a furry mountain, "that you can fix this." He thrust out his spotty arm.

Willa examined the dots. She tapped one gently. She sniffed it. She held a tiny magnifying glass up to it and said, "Oh yes. Classic Frost-Spot. Very fixable."

"Excellent. What potion do I need? What rare herb? What ancient ceremony?"

"You need to jump in the pool," Willa said. "Seven times."

Magnus waited for the rest of the instructions. They did not come.

"Jump in the pool," he repeated.

"Seven times," Willa confirmed. "All the way under. Then come back up. Then do it again. Seven splashes total."

"That's the cure? Jumping in a POND? Like a DUCKLING?"

"Like a very brave bear who wants to get rid of purple polka dots," Willa said, not unkindly.

Magnus drew himself up to his full height, which was considerable. "I am the greatest skier in Frostpeak Valley. I have seventeen trophies. I do not SPLASH in PONDS."

"Eighteen trophies won't cure Frost-Spot either," Willa said. She went back to sorting her stones.

Magnus stormed away. He tried three other remedies that afternoon. He rubbed snow on the dots (they turned magenta). He asked Cleo Coldwater to paint over them (the paint slid right off and the dots underneath were now glittery). He even let Finnegan Flipsworth try his "experimental cure," which involved Finnegan throwing pinecones at Magnus's arm while yelling motivational phrases. This did not help at all and also left small bruises.

By sunset, Magnus had magenta, glittery, bruised polka dots, and his pride was in worse shape than his arm.

He found Barnaby Beaksworth sitting outside the cocoa shop, happily munching fish crackers and watching the sky turn peach and lavender.

"Barnaby," Magnus said heavily, sitting down next to him. "Am I... am I too proud?"

Barnaby chewed thoughtfully. He was the kind of penguin who took snack-related pauses seriously. "Well," he said, "you are very good at skiing. That part's just true. But sometimes being good at something makes a bear think he's too good for simple things. And simple things are usually the best things." He held out a fish cracker. "Like these. Simple. Perfect. No trophies required."

Magnus took the cracker. He ate it slowly. It was, he had to admit, very good.

"She just said to jump in a pool," Magnus murmured. "Seven times. It seems too easy."

"Maybe that's the point," Barnaby said. "Maybe the hard part isn't the jumping. Maybe the hard part is being willing to look silly doing it."

The moon was rising when Magnus walked back to Jordan Pool. Willa was still there, as if she'd known he would return. Piper Paddlefoot had joined her, along with Nora Snowmane, who sat at the water's edge like a great calm boulder.

"I'm going to look ridiculous," Magnus said.

"Probably," Piper agreed cheerfully.

Magnus took a deep breath. He removed his magnificent red scarf and folded it carefully. He removed his ski jacket. He stood at the edge of Jordan Pool in the moonlight, a large polar bear covered in glittery magenta polka dots, and he thought about seventeen trophies and silver-engraved skis and the way everyone always watched him.

Then he jumped.

SPLASH! The water was breathtakingly cold—the kind of cold that grabs you by the bones and shakes. He burst back to the surface gasping. The dots were still there.

"One!" Willa called.

He jumped again. SPLASH! Still dotty. "Two!"

Three. Four. Five. Each time the water shocked him and each time he came up sputtering and undignified and looking exactly like a polar bear who had been jumping in a pond, which is to say: absurd. By the fifth splash, Piper was giggling. By the sixth, even Nora was smiling behind her paw.

"Six!" Willa called.

Magnus stood in the shallows, water streaming from his fur, moonlight catching the droplets. He looked down at his arm. Still spotted.

He took one more breath. He didn't jump this time—he let himself fall backward into the water, arms spread wide, like a snow angel made of trust.

SPLASH.

When he surfaced, the spots were gone. Every single one. His fur was clean and white and soft, and the water around him glowed faintly silver, as if the pool itself was pleased.

"Seven," Willa said softly.

Magnus climbed out of the pool and stood dripping in the moonlight. He looked at his arms. He looked at Willa—this tiny penguin he could hold in one paw, who had known the answer all along.

"Thank you," he said. And his voice was different. Not smaller—just gentler. Like a bear who had discovered that the bravest thing he'd ever done wasn't winning a trophy. It was letting himself be helped by someone small.

Willa handed him his scarf. "The pool just needed you to trust it," she said. "All seven times."

Later, around the bonfire, wrapped in a warm blanket with a mug of Herschel's best cocoa, Magnus sat quietly among his friends. Finnegan was telling a wildly exaggerated story about the pinecone cure. Barnaby was on his fourth fish cracker. The Powderpuff twins were trying to count the stars and kept losing track after twelve.

"Mr. Magnus," Teddy said, crawling into his lap, "you're not purple anymore."

"No," Magnus said, pulling the blanket around the little cub. "I'm just me."

And for the first time in a long time, that felt like more than enough.
