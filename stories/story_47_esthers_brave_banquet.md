# Story 47: Esther's Brave Banquet
### *Inspired by Esther's Courage — Esther 4–7*
**Characters:** Esti Stoat, Aurora Frostholm  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Winter Palace  
**Theme:** Courage for others

---

## Opening Wink

*Revision Pass 3 Spark:* The bravest creatures in Frostpeak Valley were not always the biggest ones. Sometimes they were small, nervous stoats in their best scarves, shaking slightly but walking forward anyway.

Some nights the wind whistles; tonight it hummed like it knew a secret worth sharing.

## The Story

**Esti Stoat** was not, by nature, a creature who spoke up in meetings.

She was small — even for a stoat — with a winter-white coat, a hand-knitted amber scarf that came almost to her knees, and the kind of quiet, watchful quality that meant she noticed everything and said very little. She worked in the Winter Palace's equipment room, keeping records of rentals and maintaining the boot-fitting inventory, and she was very good at her job, and almost nobody knew her name.

Which made what she was about to do considerably more terrifying.

The problem had started two weeks ago, when the Winter Palace Games Committee — a seven-member body of senior animals who organized all official valley competitions — had passed a new rule. The rule was called the **Valley Residency Requirement**, and what it said was this: only creatures who had lived in Frostpeak Valley for five or more years could enter the Winter Palace's official skating events.

This sounded reasonable until you realized what it meant in practice.

The Nestling Flock — a group of seventeen young birds who had arrived in the valley eighteen months ago, refugees from a storm that had destroyed their original home — loved skating. They had learned at the Winter Palace's morning sessions. They had gotten good. Several of them were the best junior skaters in the valley. And now, under the new rule, they could not compete.

Nobody on the Games Committee had meant to hurt anyone. They'd been trying to solve a different problem — crowded qualification rounds — and they hadn't thought through who the residency rule would affect. But the effect was real, and the Nestling Flock was heartbroken, and nobody seemed to be doing anything about it.

**Aurora Frostholm** found Esti sitting at the equipment counter with her forehead resting on the rental ledger.

"You know about the rule," said Aurora.

"Everyone knows about the rule."

"You're the only person in the valley who has personally fitted skates for every single member of the Nestling Flock," said Aurora. "You know their names. You know which ones have been working on their footwork for six months straight. You know little Pip Starling practiced until her ankles ached."

"I do know that," said Esti quietly.

"The Games Committee listens to people who work in the Palace. You have access they don't."

Esti looked up. "They're very senior animals. I'm the equipment records keeper. They're not going to listen to—"

"You won't know until you try," said Aurora. "And if you don't try, you already know the answer."

A silence.

"What if I say the wrong thing? What if they dismiss me before I finish? What if I get nervous and forget my words and just sort of—" Esti made a gesture that suggested dissolving into the floor.

"Then you get nervous and you still say it," said Aurora. "The Nestling Flock didn't choose to come here without five years of residency. They arrived in a storm. Pip Starling has been here eighteen months and she works harder than anyone. That's the truth. You know that truth. You just have to be willing to say it in a room that feels too big."

---

Esti spent three days preparing.

She read the original committee meeting notes. She made a list of every member of the Nestling Flock and their training hours and their skill progression. She wrote out what she wanted to say four times, crossed it out three times, and kept the fourth version because it was the only honest one — the one where she stopped trying to sound important and just said what was true.

On the morning of the committee's regular meeting, she put on her amber scarf. She took a very deep breath. She walked to the meeting room door.

She almost turned around twice. Once at the door, and once just inside it, when seven senior animals looked up from their agenda and she became suddenly, intensely aware that she was small and they were large and she had not been invited.

"I'm sorry to interrupt," she said. Her voice was steadier than she expected. "My name is Esti Stoat. I work in the equipment room. I have something to ask the committee about the residency rule."

The committee chairperson — a large beaver named Aldric — peered at her over his reading glasses. "The agenda is full today."

"I know," said Esti. "I'll be brief."

She was not, in the end, particularly brief. But she was thorough and she was honest, and she said things like *Pip Starling has logged more practice hours in eighteen months than most five-year residents log in three* and *they didn't choose to arrive without history; the storm chose for them*, and she said them looking directly at Aldric without looking away, even when her knees were uncertain about the whole enterprise.

When she finished, there was a silence.

Then Aldric took off his reading glasses.

"How many members of the Nestling Flock are currently registered for the spring competition?"

"Eleven," said Esti. "They registered before the rule passed. They've been training for four months."

Another silence. The committee members looked at each other.

"We could add a training-hours provision," said one of the members slowly. "Equivalent standing for creatures who can demonstrate equivalent dedication, regardless of years of residency."

"That would address the problem," said Aldric.

"And it's fairer than the original rule," said a second member.

"It's considerably fairer," said Aldric. He looked at Esti. "Thank you, Miss Stoat. This was — useful."

Esti nodded. She walked out.

In the hallway, she sat down on a bench and her knees finished being uncertain all at once, because they had been waiting politely while she needed them.

---

**Aurora** found her there five minutes later.

"Well?" said Aurora.

"They're going to change it," said Esti. "I think. They didn't say for certain, but they're going to change it."

Aurora sat down beside her. "You went in."

"I went in."

"Was it terrifying?"

"Extremely," said Esti. "The whole time. I was terrified the entire time."

"But you said the true thing."

Esti thought about Pip Starling's ankles. She thought about seventeen birds who had arrived in a storm and built a home from nothing and laced up skates they'd rented from her counter every single Tuesday and Thursday morning for a year and a half.

"It wasn't really about me," she said. "When you realize it's not about you, it gets easier. Just slightly."

Three days later, the committee posted the revised rule. The training-hours provision was there, clearly worded, fair. Eleven members of the Nestling Flock would be competing in the spring event.

When Pip Starling came to pick up her rental skates that Thursday morning, Esti handed them over as usual.

"I heard the rule changed," said Pip.

"Yes," said Esti.

"Do you know who asked them to change it?"

Esti straightened the rental ledger. "Someone who knew your training hours," she said.

Pip looked at her.

"The skates are ready," said Esti. "Don't be late for the ice."
