# Story 9: The Little Lost Boarder
### *Inspired by The Parable of the Lost Sheep — Luke 15:1–7*
**Characters:** Nora Snowmane, Willa Wobble, and a very small, very lost penguin named Dot
**Setting:** The Deep Powder Forest, evening into night
**Theme:** You are always worth finding. No one is left behind.

---

> *This one is for anyone who has ever felt lost. Lie down. Let the dark be safe tonight.*

---

## Opening Wink

When one tiny boarder goes missing, the whole valley turns into a determined, snow-booted search party.

## The Story

**Nora Snowmane** looked after a great many animals.

This was not an official position. No one had appointed her. It had simply happened the way some things happen — gradually, naturally, inevitably — because Nora was the kind of bear who noticed things. She noticed when someone was sitting alone at the Warming Hut. She noticed when someone hadn't come to dinner. She noticed when something was missing.

She always noticed.

---

On an evening in deep winter, the valley animals came in from the slopes one by one, stomping snow off boots and skis in the Warming Hut porch, filling the hut with noise and steam and the smell of cocoa and wet wool.

Nora counted as they came in. She always counted. Not loudly, not in a way anyone noticed. Just kept a soft running tally, name by name, in the back of her mind.

*Bjorn — yes. Finnegan — yes (crashed through the door). Cleo — yes. Barnaby — yes (brought extra fish crackers). Aurora — yes. Magnus — yes (grumbling about something). The glacier foxes — yes. Teddy — yes. Tilda — yes.*

The hut was warm and full and noisy.

Nora's count was one short.

She stood very still. She ran through it again. *One short.* Not one of the familiar faces she'd been counting for years — someone different. Someone new.

That afternoon, a group of young penguins had come down from the Eastern Ridge for their first day on the valley slopes. Nora had watched them arrive — six small penguins in a cluster, wide-eyed and excited. She had counted six coming in.

She counted five in the hut.

*Dot.*

The smallest one. The one with the secondhand snowboard and the red mittens three sizes too big. The one who'd gone quiet when the bigger penguins started showing off, and drifted slightly to the edge of the group, and—

Nora was already putting her coat on.

---

"Where are you going?" said Magnus, from his corner.

"One is missing," said Nora.

Magnus looked at the full, warm, noisy hut. "It looks like everyone's here."

"One is missing," said Nora again. She buttoned her coat.

Magnus stood up. *Fine. But I'm only doing this once.* He was already putting on his jacket.

Nora looked at him. "You don't have to—"

"Obviously I'm coming," Magnus said, not looking at her.

---

They went out into the dark.

The valley at night in deep winter was a different world: blue-black, still, the snow glowing faintly in the starlight, the trees standing like dark columns in the Deep Powder Forest. The temperature had dropped sharply. The trail markers were half-buried in new powder.

Nora followed the edge of the Eastern Ridge trail. Magnus followed Nora. They didn't speak much. The snow was soft and absorbed their footsteps.

After twenty minutes, Nora stopped.

She had heard something. Very small. Almost nothing.

*"...hello?"*

They followed the sound.

In a hollow at the base of a large pine, tucked into the snow on all sides, was **Dot**: the smallest penguin, red mittens too large for her flippers, secondhand snowboard lying flat beside her. She had taken a wrong turn when the trail forked and gotten turned around in the forest. She'd been sitting in the hollow for two hours, getting colder, hoping someone would come.

"Hello, Dot," said Nora.

Dot looked up. Her eyes were red around the edges. "How did you — how did you know I was—"

"I always count," said Nora simply.

---

She wrapped Dot in her great warm polar bear coat. Magnus carried Dot's snowboard under one enormous arm — because his hands were free, he said, not for any other reason. They walked back through the dark forest, and Nora murmured to Dot about the trees and the stars and the way the snow looked different at night, and Dot, slowly, stopped shaking.

When they came back through the Warming Hut door, the noise briefly stopped.

Nora set Dot down carefully in front of the fire. Barnaby immediately appeared with cocoa. Willa, who had been quietly worried, put a spare lavender scarf around Dot's neck. The other five Eastern Ridge penguins crowded around.

Herschel looked at Nora over the tops of his whiskers with an expression of deep, quiet recognition.

"One short?" he said softly.

"One short," said Nora. "Not anymore."

---

Later, after Dot was warm and fed and the whole hut had returned to its comfortable uproar, Dot tugged on Nora's sleeve.

"You came for *me*?" she said. She made it sound like the most surprising thing in the world. "You didn't even know me. You came looking in the dark for *me*?"

Nora looked at her for a moment.

"You were missing," she said. "Of course I came."

Dot thought about this.

"But there were" — she gestured at the full, warm, loud hut — "so many others. You could have just—"

"No," said Nora gently. "I couldn't."

She tucked the great coat tighter around Dot's small shoulders.

*"You can't not look for the one who is lost,"* she said. *"That's not how love works."*

---

Dot fell asleep in Nora's coat, in front of the fire, in the warmth and noise of the Warming Hut, and she slept so deeply and so safely that she didn't wake until morning — and when she woke, someone had found her mittens in the pocket of the coat and folded them carefully next to her head.

She never did find out who.

She suspected everyone.

## Image Prompts

**Cover Illustration:**
> Night in the Deep Powder Forest. A tiny penguin sits in a hollow at the base of a pine, red mittens too big for her flippers, snowboard beside her, snow falling gently. In the distance, through the dark trees: two warm glowing lanterns approaching — a great polar bear and a grumpy polar bear, coming toward her through the blue-dark. Style: intimate, tender, the contrast between small lost figure and approaching warmth.

**Scene 1 — Nora Counts:**
> Inside the warm, crowded Warming Hut. From Nora's perspective: animal after animal entering, each one mentally ticked off. The hut is golden and noisy. Then: Nora's face. Still. The moment she knows. One count short. Style: the warm chaos of the hut against Nora's quiet, certain focus.

**Scene 2 — The Night Forest:**
> Nora and Magnus walking through the Deep Powder Forest at night. Tall dark pines, snow glowing faintly blue in starlight. Two small lanterns bobbing. The forest is vast and quiet. They walk steadily, purposefully. Style: beautiful and slightly eerie — the dark is safe in their company.

**Scene 3 — The Return:**
> Nora carrying Dot wrapped in her huge coat, back through the Warming Hut door. Light and warmth pouring out. The crowd inside parting and crowding in. Barnaby with cocoa. Willa with a scarf. Five little penguins crowding around. Magnus quietly setting down the snowboard. Everything warm and right. Style: golden, joyful, complete.

## Video Prompts

**The Count:**
> Close-up sequence: animals entering the Warming Hut one by one. Nora's quiet, watching eyes. Each one checked off in the soft counter of her gaze. The door closes. Silence. Nora's face. Beat. She stands. Puts on coat. Music shifts from warm busy hut sounds to a single quiet note.

**The Forest:**
> The two bears walking through the dark forest. Footsteps in fresh powder. Lanterns swinging. The forest is blue and still and vast. Then: the tiny voice. "...hello?" The bears stop. Nora tilts her head. They follow the sound. Music: one searching melody, tender and unhurried.

**The Discovery and Return:**
> Nora finds Dot in the hollow. Wraps her. Carries her. The forest walk back is warm despite the cold — Nora murmuring, Dot's eyes clearing, Magnus carrying the snowboard with studied casualness. The Warming Hut in the distance, warm light in the windows. The door opens. The golden light falls out. Music: the warmest, fullest swell of the whole story. Then gently down as Dot falls asleep in the coat.

## Cinematic Prompt Enhancers (Phase 3)

Use these add-ons with every image/video prompt in this story to keep visuals consistent and lively.

- **Character Lock:** Keep **Willa + Nora** visually consistent across shots (same outfit colors, board/ski design, and size relationships).
- **Style Lock:** cozy storybook fantasy, soft volumetric snowfall, aurora glow, warm rim-light, expressive faces, playful motion arcs.
- **Camera Mix:** include at least one **wide establishing shot**, one **medium character performance shot**, and one **low-angle action shot**.
- **Motion Cues:** snow spray, scarf flutter, tiny slips + recoveries, joyful group reactions.
- **Kid-Safe Tone:** wonder + comedy, never frightening; challenges feel adventurous, not scary.
- **Negative Prompt (optional):** no horror, no photoreal harshness, no gloomy desaturated palette, no weapons, no realistic peril.
