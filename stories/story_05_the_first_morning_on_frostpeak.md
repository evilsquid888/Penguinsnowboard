# Story 5: The First Morning on Frostpeak
### *Inspired by The Creation — Genesis 1–2*
**Characters:** Cleo Coldwater, Teddy & Tilda Powderpuff, Old Herschel the Walrus
**Setting:** The whole of Frostpeak Valley — before it had a name
**Theme:** Wonder, gratitude for beauty, rest is part of the gift

---

> *Lie back. Let your body get heavy. This is the story of how everything beautiful began.*

---

## The Story

Before there were slopes, there was silence.

Before there was silence, there was *nothing at all* — not even the dark, because even dark needs space to exist in, and there was no space yet.

And then — *oh then* — something breathtaking happened.

But we'll get to that.

---

**Cleo Coldwater** was a macaroni penguin with spectacular golden-orange feathers above each eye like a tiny crown, and she had a gift that was different from all the other animals in the valley.

Cleo could *see*.

Not just see with her eyes — all animals did that. She could *really* see: the precise blue of ice two feet deep versus ice three feet deep; the way afternoon light hit fresh powder at exactly 4 o'clock and made every individual snowflake visible for just thirty seconds before the angle changed; the way Bjorn Bigpaws tried to look dignified while secretly watching baby birds.

Cleo wrote down everything she saw.

Every evening, she would find a spot at the Starwatch Cliff and fill her little sketchbook with drawings and words and colors she tried to mix from snow and bark and berries to match exactly what she'd seen that day.

One morning — a morning in late autumn when the whole valley was shifting from gold to white — Cleo arrived at Starwatch Cliff before sunrise and found the two bear cubs, **Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff**, already there.

"We couldn't sleep," said Teddy, snow on his nose.

"We sneaked out," said Tilda, snow in her ears.

"We always do," they said together.

"Sit with me then," said Cleo, making room on the ledge. "Something's about to happen."

---

The three of them sat in the dark — one macaroni penguin with her sketchbook open and two small bears pressed in close on either side — and they watched the eastern ridge.

First came the light. Not the sun yet — just the *idea* of light, a pale wash of grey along the highest peaks that said: *something is coming.*

"What is that?" whispered Teddy.

"That's the beginning," said Cleo softly.

Then the grey became silver. The silver became pink. The pink became orange and gold and the most extraordinary red that Cleo had never quite managed to capture in any pigment, though she kept trying. And then — there — *there* — over the edge of the Eastern Peak, the sun.

Tilda made a sound. It wasn't a word. It was just a sound you make when something is too beautiful for words.

Cleo was already drawing. Her flipper moved fast across the page. But she paused, pencil in the air, and just *looked* for a moment, and it was the looking that mattered more than the drawing.

---

Old **Herschel the Walrus** had been watching sunrises over Frostpeak Valley for so many years that he had lost count. His great bristled whiskers had caught snowflakes from every storm the mountain had ever made. He knew the valley the way you know a very old and very dear friend.

That afternoon, he found the three of them — penguin, cub, cub — on the cliff, Cleo still drawing, the bears asleep on either side of her, and he sat down with his usual heaviness and his chamomile and his red hat.

"Drawing the valley again?" he asked.

"Always," said Cleo. She showed him the sketchbook. Page after page: the way pine shadows fell long and blue at evening; the exact crystalline structure of the frost on the Warming Hut window; the northern lights reflected in Frozen Mirror Lake.

Herschel turned the pages slowly. His old eyes were very kind.

"You know," he said, "when this valley was first made — I mean *really* first made, before any of us were here — someone looked at it all and said it was *good*."

"Good?" said Cleo.

"*Very* good," said Herschel. "Each thing as it was finished — the ice, the sky, the peaks, the powder, the creatures — and then at the end, rest." He turned the last page. "The rest was part of it. Not an afterthought. Part of the making."

Cleo looked at the two bears asleep in her sketchbook's warm shadow.

"The rest is part of it," she repeated softly.

"Everything you see," said Herschel, gesturing out over the whole valley — the glittering slopes, the dark pines, the frozen lake, the sky turning its evening shade of violet — "was made on purpose. Every *specific* beautiful thing." He tapped the sketchbook page. "You see them. That matters."

---

That evening, Cleo sat at the bottom of the Starwatch Cliff and didn't draw.

She just watched the stars come out — one at a time, then two, then suddenly *all at once* — the whole ridiculous impossible scattered brilliance of them — and she breathed in the cold, pine-scented, mountain-clean air of Frostpeak Valley.

*Made on purpose,* she thought.

*Every specific beautiful thing.*

*Including me.*

Teddy woke up just long enough to murmur: "Cleo? Are the stars out?"

"Yes," she said quietly.

"Good," he said, and went back to sleep.

Tilda, still asleep, said: "We didn't mean to—"

Cleo smiled. "I know," she said to no one in particular.

And the valley held them — all the made things, all the on-purpose things — in the blue and silver and quiet of the first stars.

## Image Prompts

**Cover Illustration:**
> Pre-dawn on a mountain cliff: a macaroni penguin with a glowing golden sketchbook sits flanked by two tiny bear cubs. The Eastern Peak glows with the first light of sunrise — pale gold bleeding into pink and violet. Stars still visible overhead. The whole valley spread below in blue shadow. Style: luminous, awe-inspiring watercolor, soft and sacred feeling.

**Scene 1 — The Sunrise:**
> Three-panel sequence: (1) the dark ridge, one pale silver line; (2) pink and gold spreading; (3) the full sunrise erupting over the peak — the valley lit in warm gold, every snowflake distinct, pine shadows long and blue. The three small figures on the cliff are silhouetted against it all. Style: spectacular color, sense of something enormous and beautiful happening.

**Scene 2 — The Sketchbook Pages:**
> Close-up of an open sketchbook across two pages. Detailed watercolor sketches: frost patterns, northern lights over a frozen lake, a polar bear cub asleep, pine shadow on snow, a single perfect snowflake. A golden feather serves as a bookmark. Style: detailed, warm, intimate — like looking at something private and precious.

**Scene 3 — The Stars Coming Out:**
> From above: a small penguin sitting alone on a cliff ledge, two sleeping bears on either side, looking up. The sky above is deep blue-black and filling, star by star, with light. The valley below is dark and peaceful. One small warm lantern from the cliff path. Style: quiet, vast, the most peaceful illustration in the book.

## Video Prompts

**The Sunrise Sequence:**
> Real-time animation of a mountain sunrise from a cliff. The sky changes color in slow, deliberate waves. Each stage beautiful. The three small figures are silhouetted and still. No dialogue. Music: gentle strings and single piano, swelling slowly with the light. The climax: full sunrise, the whole valley gold, the bear cub's involuntary sound of wonder. Music becomes full and joyful. Then goes quiet again.

**Herschel's Story:**
> Herschel sits on the cliff, turning sketchbook pages. As he speaks of the valley being made, brief dream-like sequences: ice forming on a still lake; pines growing from seedlings; northern lights first blooming above empty snowy peaks; animals arriving for the first time and looking around in wonder. Watercolor dream style. Warm, ancient, beautiful.

**The Stars:**
> Time-lapse but gentle: the sky above Frostpeak Valley dims from violet to dark blue. Stars appear — first one, then in pairs, then in cascades. The camera slowly rotates to show the whole canopy of stars reflected in the frozen lake below. Two skies of stars, one above and one below. Music: a simple, ancient-feeling lullaby melody. Slowly fades to silence and dark.
