# Story 104: The Donkey Who Spoke Up
### *Inspired by Balaam's Donkey — Numbers 22:21–39*
**Characters:** Finnegan Flipsworth, Magnus Mountainguard (polar bear), Piper Paddlefoot
**Setting:** The Shortcut Trail through Icicle Pass
**Theme:** Sometimes the smallest, silliest friend sees the danger you can't

---

> *One more story before sleep. This one is about listening to the friend everyone else ignores — especially when they're trying very hard to save you.*

---

## Opening Wink

*Revision Pass 3 Spark:* A seal at the bottom of the pass clapped at nothing in particular. This turned out to be the most helpful contribution anyone made all day.

Magnus had a plan. Finnegan had a bad feeling. Only one of them was right, and it wasn't the one with the plan.

## The Story

**Magnus Mountainguard** was the biggest polar bear in Frostpeak Valley. He was also the most determined. When Magnus decided something, it stayed decided. When Magnus chose a direction, he walked in that direction. When Magnus made a plan, the plan happened, because Magnus was very large and the plan was not going to argue.

Today, Magnus had decided to take the Shortcut Trail through Icicle Pass.

"Magnus," said Piper carefully, "nobody takes the Shortcut Trail in March. The ice shelves are unstable."

"I've taken it forty times," said Magnus.

"In *January*," said Piper. "When everything's frozen solid. It's March now. Things are melty."

"I'll be fine," said Magnus, and began walking.

**Finnegan Flipsworth** was going with him. Not because Finnegan had been invited — Magnus had specifically said, "Finnegan, please do not come, you are a hazard to yourself and others" — but because Finnegan had been standing nearby when Magnus left and had simply... followed. The way a leaf follows a river. Not on purpose, but inevitably.

They made an odd pair on the trail: Magnus, enormous and purposeful, skis tucked under one arm, striding through the snow like a furry glacier. And Finnegan, small and chaotic, on his red snowboard with fish and lightning stickers, wobbling along behind, occasionally crashing into things that weren't even in his path.

"Finnegan," said Magnus, without turning around, "why are you here?"

"Great question!" said Finnegan. "I have no idea! But I feel like I should be!"

Magnus sighed the sigh of a bear who has accepted his fate.

---

They entered Icicle Pass — a narrow canyon between two ice cliffs, where enormous icicles hung from the walls like frozen chandeliers. It was beautiful. It was also, Finnegan noticed, making some very concerning sounds.

*Creak. Pop. Drip.*

"Magnus?" said Finnegan.

"What."

"The ice is talking."

"Ice doesn't talk, Finnegan."

"It's making noises. Creaky noises. The kind of noises that come right before—"

"Finnegan. I have walked this pass forty times."

"In JANUARY!"

Magnus kept walking.

Finnegan's flippers were tingling. Not the fun kind of tingling, like when you're about to hit a really good jump. The bad kind. The kind that said *something is wrong and you should pay attention even though nobody is listening to you because you are small and silly and you once got your beak stuck in a cocoa mug*.

He looked up at the icicles. One of them — a big one, directly above the trail about fifty yards ahead — had a crack running through its base. Water dripped from the crack in a steady stream.

"MAGNUS STOP!" Finnegan shouted.

Magnus stopped. Not because he wanted to, but because Finnegan had thrown himself directly in front of Magnus's skis, lying flat on the ground like a feathery speed bump.

"Finnegan, what are you—"

"LOOK UP."

Magnus looked up.

The icicle with the crack was directly above where he would have been standing in about ten more steps. As they watched, the crack widened. A chunk of ice the size of a beach ball broke free and crashed onto the trail with a sound like a cannon shot.

Right where Magnus would have been.

Magnus stared at the shattered ice. He stared at Finnegan, still lying flat on the ground.

"...How did you know?" he asked.

Finnegan sat up. Snow was in his ear holes, his crest was sideways, and he had the dazed look of someone who had just done something brave entirely by accident.

"I didn't *know* know," he said. "I just... felt it. The creaking. The dripping. Everything was saying *stop* and I couldn't not listen."

---

They found another way around — a longer path that wound through a safe, solid section of the pass. It took an extra hour. Magnus was quiet for most of it.

When they emerged on the other side of Icicle Pass, the sun was setting and the whole valley was painted in pink and gold.

Magnus stopped. He turned to Finnegan.

This was unusual. Magnus did not generally stop and turn to people. Magnus generally went forward and expected people to keep up.

"Finnegan," he said.

"Yeah?"

"I didn't listen to you."

"That's okay, nobody ever—"

"No," said Magnus. "It's not okay. You saw something I didn't. You tried to tell me. I ignored you because I thought I knew better, and I was wrong."

Finnegan blinked. This was possibly the most serious anyone had ever been with him.

"I'm sorry," said Magnus. "And thank you."

Finnegan's eyes went wide. Then his beak quivered. Then he launched himself at Magnus and hugged one of his enormous legs, because that was as high as he could reach.

"You're WELCOME!" he shouted into Magnus's kneecap. "I'm SO GLAD you're NOT SQUISHED!"

Magnus, very carefully, patted Finnegan on the head with one massive paw.

"Me too," he said.

---

When they got back to the valley, Piper was waiting with her arms crossed.

"You took the Shortcut Trail, didn't you."

"I did," said Magnus.

"And?"

"And Finnegan saved my life," said Magnus, as if this were a perfectly normal sentence.

Piper looked at Finnegan. Finnegan was picking ice out of his crest and humming.

"*Finnegan* saved your life," she repeated.

"The smallest friend," said Magnus, "sometimes sees the biggest danger."

Finnegan beamed. "I also fell down seven times on the way back! And got my board stuck in a tree! And accidentally scared a family of snow rabbits! It was the BEST DAY!"

Magnus closed his eyes. A very small smile crossed his very large face.

"It really was," he said.
