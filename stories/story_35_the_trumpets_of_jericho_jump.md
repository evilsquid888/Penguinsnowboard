# Story 35: The Trumpets of Jericho Jump
### *Inspired by Jericho — Joshua 6*
**Characters:** Jory Ibex, Barnaby Beaksworth  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Jericho Jump Arena  
**Theme:** Trusting unusual instructions

## Opening Wink

What do you get when you combine seven laps, zero patience, and one very enthusiastic tuba? Tonight you'll find out. Grab your earmuffs -- this one gets loud.

## The Story

The ice wall appeared overnight, and nobody could explain it.

One evening the path to Jericho Jump Arena -- the valley's best terrain park, with its magnificent half-pipe and its legendary corkscrew drop -- was perfectly open. The next morning, it was blocked by a wall of solid blue ice twenty feet high, eight feet thick, and stretching from cliff face to cliff face like a frozen fortress.

"What in the name of hot cocoa," said Piper Paddlefoot, standing at the base of the wall with her snowboard under her flipper and her beak hanging open, "is THAT?"

"Ice," said Willa Wobble helpfully.

"I can SEE it's ice, Willa. I mean where did it come from?"

Nobody knew. Herschel the Walrus stroked his whiskers and said he'd seen ice dams before, when meltwater from the upper glaciers refroze in narrow passages during a cold snap. "The gorge must have channeled a waterfall and it froze solid in the night," he said. "Happens once in a generation. Last time was before you were born."

"Can we go around it?" Barnaby asked, craning his neck. The wall filled the gorge completely, smooth and blue and sparkling in the morning light, as immovable as a glacier's handshake.

"No." Bjorn Bigpaws studied the cliffs on either side. "The walls of the gorge are vertical. There's no way around, no way over."

"So we go THROUGH it!" Finnegan Flipsworth declared, already running at the wall with his head down like a very small, very feathered battering ram.

He hit the ice with a thud that echoed across the valley.

The wall did not move.

Finnegan slid slowly down the ice face and landed in a heap at the bottom. "Ow."

"Perhaps," Nora Snowmane said, helping Finnegan to his feet, "we could try something other than headbutting."

Over the next two hours, the valley threw everything it had at the ice wall. Magnus Meltsworth brought his biggest ice saw -- the blade bounced off. Bjorn tried shoulder-charging it, which accomplished nothing except making Bjorn's shoulder sore. Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff built a catapult out of ski poles and launched snowballs at it, which was tremendously fun but structurally useless. The seal pups tried to tunnel under it and got stuck, requiring a twenty-minute rescue operation involving warm towels and encouraging words.

"This is hopeless," Piper groaned, slumping against the wall. The Jericho Jump terrain park was RIGHT THERE on the other side -- she could practically hear the wind whistling through the half-pipe. The Grand Freestyle Competition was in three days, and if they couldn't get through, the whole event would have to be canceled.

Cleo Coldwater sat on a rock, sketching the wall thoughtfully. She wasn't trying to solve the problem. She was just drawing. But as she drew, she noticed something.

"Look at this," she said, holding up her sketchbook. She'd drawn the wall from the side, showing its cross-section. "See how the ice is layered? It's not one solid block. It's hundreds of thin sheets, frozen together. Like a stack of pancakes made of ice."

"Pancakes," Barnaby said dreamily.

"Focus, Barnaby. My point is -- solid force won't break it because the layers absorb the shock. But vibration? Resonance? That's different. If you could vibrate the ice at the right frequency, the layers would separate. The whole thing would shatter."

Everyone stared at Cleo.

"I paint pictures," she said. "I also read a lot."

"Vibration," Bjorn repeated slowly. "You mean... sound?"

"Loud sound," Cleo said. "Sustained, rhythmic, building over time. Like... music."

Finnegan's eyes went wide. "I HAVE A TUBA!"

"You do NOT have a tuba," Piper said.

"I do! It's in my den! I found it in a snowdrift last spring and I've been practicing! Well, I've been making noises into it. Whether those noises constitute 'practice' is a matter of debate--"

"Get the tuba," Bjorn said.

What happened next was the most ridiculous, glorious, ear-splitting musical event in the history of Frostpeak Valley.

Finnegan retrieved his tuba -- a battered brass instrument with a dent on one side and a fish cracker lodged in the bell that nobody wanted to investigate. Barnaby found a snare drum in the warming lodge. Piper grabbed two ski poles and declared herself a percussionist. Cleo discovered she could hum at a pitch that made icicles tremble. The seal pups clapped their flippers in rhythm. Teddy and Tilda banged pot lids together with the enthusiasm of creatures who have been waiting their entire short lives for permission to bang pot lids together.

And Willa? Willa had a penny whistle. A tiny, silver penny whistle that her grandmother had given her, and when she played it, the note was so pure and high and clear that it cut through the noise like a beam of light through fog.

"Here's the plan," Bjorn announced, his deep voice carrying over the ragtag orchestra. "We march around the wall. Seven laps. Each lap, we play louder. On the seventh lap, we give it everything we've got."

"That sounds completely absurd," Magnus said.

"Do you have a better idea?"

Magnus did not have a better idea. Magnus did, however, have a cowbell, which he produced from his coat with the air of a bear who would deny owning a cowbell under any other circumstances.

They lined up. Bjorn at the front, setting the pace with his massive ski-pole strides. Behind him, a parade of penguins, bears, seals, and one walrus pushing his cocoa cart because "morale support requires hot beverages."

Lap one. They marched in a wide circle around the base of the ice wall, playing softly. Finnegan's tuba produced a sound like a seasick elephant. The drum kept time. Willa's penny whistle floated above it all like a silver thread.

The wall did nothing.

Lap two. A little louder. Barnaby found his rhythm on the drum. The pot lids clanged. The seal pups clapped faster.

The wall shimmered. Just barely. A tiny dusting of frost fell from the top.

"It's working," Cleo whispered. "Keep going!"

Lap three. Lap four. The music grew. Finnegan figured out how to make his tuba produce an actual note instead of a dying-whale impersonation, and the deep brass tone rumbled through the gorge like thunder. The ice wall was humming now -- literally humming, the layers vibrating against each other, a high thin whine that harmonized with Willa's penny whistle in a way that made everyone's fur stand on end.

Lap five. The cracks appeared. Tiny hairline fractures, spidering across the surface of the wall like frost patterns on a window. Fragments of ice tinkled down like wind chimes.

Lap six. The music was deafening now. Magnificent and terrible and absolutely joyful. Herschel was banging his cocoa ladle against the cart. Magnus was swinging his cowbell with an expression that could only be described as reluctant ecstasy. The bear cubs were howling with delight. The seal pups had added a synchronized clapping routine that was genuinely impressive.

And the wall was shaking. Visibly, unmistakably shaking, the layers grinding against each other, the cracks widening, chunks of ice calving off the top and shattering on the ground in explosions of blue-white crystal.

"SEVENTH LAP!" Bjorn roared. "EVERYTHING YOU'VE GOT!"

They marched. They played. Finnegan blew into his tuba so hard that the fish cracker finally shot out of the bell and hit Barnaby in the back of the head. Piper drummed her ski poles on every rock and tree trunk she passed. The seal pups clapped so fast their flippers blurred.

And Willa played one long, clear, impossibly high note on her penny whistle -- a note that climbed and climbed until it seemed to touch the sky itself.

The ice wall hung there for one breathless second, trembling, singing, alive with vibration--

And then it fell.

Not violently. Not dangerously. It collapsed inward like a curtain of crystal beads, each layer separating from the next and cascading down in a waterfall of ice that caught the sunlight and shattered it into a million rainbows. The sound was like ten thousand wind chimes ringing at once, bright and sharp and beautiful, and when the last piece settled, the path to Jericho Jump Arena lay open, gleaming and clear, with the half-pipe sparkling beyond it like a promise.

Nobody moved for a full five seconds.

Then Finnegan raised his tuba over his head and screamed, "WE DID THAT! WITH MUSIC! WE DESTROYED A WALL WITH A TUBA AND POT LIDS AND A VERY SMALL WHISTLE!"

The celebration that followed was legendary. Piper was the first through the gap, snowboarding over the ice rubble with a whoop that echoed off the cliffs. Barnaby set up a victory snack station. The seal pups did a lap of honor. Magnus tried to quietly return his cowbell to his coat, but Tilda saw him and yelled, "MAGNUS HAS A SECRET COWBELL!" and he had to endure ten minutes of affectionate teasing.

That evening, they sat in the arena itself -- the half-pipe glowing blue in the twilight, the stars appearing one by one overhead, the shattered remains of the ice wall glittering all around them like scattered diamonds.

"You know what I keep thinking?" Piper said, leaning against Bjorn's side. "We tried to break that wall with strength. With tools. With force. None of it worked."

"But music did," Willa said.

"Patience did," Bjorn corrected gently. "Seven laps. Not one. Not two. Seven. The wall didn't fall because we were strong. It fell because we were steady, and together, and willing to trust that the strange plan would work."

Finnegan cradled his tuba like a baby. "I'm naming her Jericho."

"You cannot name a tuba," Magnus said.

"I already did. Jericho and I are inseparable now."

Willa yawned -- a yawn so big it seemed to take up her whole tiny body -- and leaned against Piper's side. "Sometimes," she murmured sleepily, "the impossible thing just needs a different kind of trying."

The aurora bloomed overhead, green and gold, and the valley settled into the soft, deep quiet that comes after a day of extraordinary noise. Somewhere in the distance, a snow owl hooted. The cocoa cooled in their mugs. The ice diamonds sparkled.

And if you listened very carefully, you could still hear the echo of a penny whistle, high and clear and brave, ringing off the mountains like a lullaby.
