# Story 3: The Penguin Left on the Slope
### *Inspired by The Good Samaritan — Luke 10:25–37*
**Characters:** Barnaby Beaksworth, Nora Snowmane, a stranger penguin named Pip
**Setting:** The Deep Powder Forest trail, mid-mountain
**Theme:** Kindness to strangers, love your neighbor whoever they are

---

> *Snuggle in. This is a story about a penguin nobody stopped for — until someone did.*

## Opening Wink

Some heroes wear capes. Others carry emergency cocoa and know how to stop on black ice without dramatic flipper-waving.

## The Story

One afternoon, a little penguin named **Pip** was snowboarding through the Deep Powder Forest trail alone.

Pip was from the South Ridge — the other side of the mountain, far away. The penguins from South Ridge wore green scarves instead of blue ones, and they did things slightly differently, and the valley animals didn't always understand them, and so — as sometimes happens between creatures who don't know each other well — there was a certain amount of *not paying attention* to one another.

Pip had been trying the Forest Trail for the first time. It was more winding than she expected. One sharp corner came up faster than it should have, and Pip's snowboard hit a hidden root under the powder, and she went tumbling — *whomp, whump, THUD* — into a snowdrift three feet deep, bonking her head on a pine branch on the way down.

When she stopped tumbling, she was sitting in cold powder up to her shoulders with a sore wing, a dazed expression, and a snowboard that had gone somewhere entirely separate.

She tried to call for help. Her voice came out very small. The powder muffled it further.

She waited.

---

The first animal to come by was Fjord, the fastest snow fox in the valley.

Fjord was sleek and silver and very busy. He had a race to get to. He was already running late. He zoomed down the Forest Trail on his skis, spotted Pip sitting in the snowdrift, and — in all honesty — thought: *she'll be fine, it's just a tumble, someone else will stop* — and kept going.

The second animal to come by was an extremely respected penguin elder named Professor Waddle Bartholomew III, who wore a very impressive ascot and was on his way to an important meeting about the Annual Snowboard Waxing Conference, which was, he had told everyone several times, *extremely* important.

The Professor saw Pip in the snowdrift. He slowed down. He tilted his head. He checked his watch. He sped up.

*Someone else will stop,* he thought. *I'm late for the waxing.*

Pip sat in the cold powder. Her wing throbbed. The snow down the back of her collar was exceptionally cold. The pine trees were very tall and very quiet.

She waited some more.

---

Then she heard humming.

It was a round, cheerful, rumbling sort of hum — the kind of hum that belongs to someone who finds most things genuinely delightful. And around the curve of the Forest Trail came **Barnaby Beaksworth**, snowboarding on his wide pizza-slice orange board, enormous backpack on his back, humming a song about fish.

Barnaby was from the Valley Floor — the old neighborhood. He and Pip had never met. They were from different parts of the mountain. Some animals would have said that didn't matter.

Barnaby stopped the instant he saw her.

Not slowed down. *Stopped.*

He stepped off his board in the snow with a *crunch* and waddled through the knee-deep powder over to Pip without a single moment of hesitation.

"Oh, you poor thing!" he said warmly. "Are you hurt? Here, let me — careful, I've got you—" and he very gently helped Pip up out of the snowdrift, brushing snow off her wings with the practiced care of someone who helped tumbled things all the time.

"My board," Pip said faintly, "it went— over there, I think—"

"Don't worry about the board," said Barnaby, already kneeling to look at her wing. "We'll find it. First: are you warm enough? Here—" He was already unclipping his backpack with an efficiency that suggested it had many pockets for many situations. Out came a small wool blanket (slightly fish-scented but wonderfully warm). Out came a thermos of cocoa. Out came a little wrapped parcel of fish crackers.

*"There's always enough for one more,"* said Barnaby.

"I'm from South Ridge," said Pip, because it seemed important to say, because she wasn't sure if it mattered.

"Oh, I've heard the South Ridge trails are wonderful," said Barnaby cheerfully, wrapping the blanket around her shoulders. "Do you go through the birch grove or around it? I've always wanted to know."

He genuinely wanted to know. His eyes were completely sincere.

Pip blinked. "Through it," she said softly. "It's faster. But around it is prettier."

"*Around it is prettier,*" repeated Barnaby with great satisfaction, as though she'd told him something genuinely wonderful. "I knew it."

---

They sat in the snow together — this round valley penguin and this small South Ridge penguin she had never met — drinking cocoa from Barnaby's thermos while the pine trees let soft pillows of snow fall from their branches around them. Barnaby found her snowboard fifteen feet away, stuck in a bush, none the worse for wear. He checked her wing and declared it bruised but not broken and told her to keep it tucked for the rest of the afternoon.

He walked with her all the way to Nora Snowmane's warming den at the edge of the forest — because **Nora** was the valley's greatest healer, and her den was warm and smelled of pine and honey, and nobody ever left it without feeling better.

**Nora** opened the door and looked down at them with her warm brown eyes.

"She took a tumble in the Forest Trail," said Barnaby. "Wing's bruised. She could use a proper warming up."

Nora looked at Pip. She looked at Barnaby. She had that look of someone who was not surprised by kindness but was always deeply pleased to see it.

"Come in, both of you," said Nora. "There's soup."

"There's SOUP?" said Barnaby, and he might have actually started crying a little.

---

Later, warm and fed and wrapped in one of Nora's spare blankets — a very large polar bear blanket that swallowed Pip entirely and she didn't mind at all — Pip looked up at Barnaby and said:

*"You didn't know me. You didn't have to stop."*

Barnaby looked at her as though this was the most puzzling thing anyone had ever said.

"Of course I stopped," he said simply. "You needed help. You were right there." He offered her another fish cracker. "Besides. Now I have a new friend from South Ridge who knows the birch grove route. That seems like a very good use of an afternoon."

---

Nora smiled into her soup.

Outside, the Deep Powder Forest was soft and still and silent, and the last of the afternoon light fell through the pines in long golden columns, and somewhere a small bird was doing its best to sing the day to sleep.

## Cinematic Prompt Enhancers (Phase 3)

Use these add-ons with every image/video prompt in this story to keep visuals consistent and lively.

- **Character Lock:** Keep **Nora + rescue crew** visually consistent across shots (same outfit colors, board/ski design, and size relationships).
- **Style Lock:** cozy storybook fantasy, soft volumetric snowfall, aurora glow, warm rim-light, expressive faces, playful motion arcs.
- **Camera Mix:** include at least one **wide establishing shot**, one **medium character performance shot**, and one **low-angle action shot**.
- **Motion Cues:** snow spray, scarf flutter, tiny slips + recoveries, joyful group reactions.
- **Kid-Safe Tone:** wonder + comedy, never frightening; challenges feel adventurous, not scary.
- **Negative Prompt (optional):** no horror, no photoreal harshness, no gloomy desaturated palette, no weapons, no realistic peril.
