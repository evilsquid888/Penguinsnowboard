# Story 19: Cleo and the Lost Coin Bell
### *Inspired by The Lost Coin — Luke 15:8–10*
**Characters:** Cleo Coldwater, Teddy & Tilda Powderpuff, Nora Snowmane  
**Setting:** Cocoa Warming Hut  
**Theme:** Joy in finding what is precious
**Tone:** Fun, whimsical, cozy bedtime adventure

---

## Opening Wink

*Revision Pass 3 Spark:* When the bell chimed, three seals clapped in sync like they had rehearsed all afternoon.

Cleo Coldwater has never lost anything without turning the search into an adventure. This is either a gift or a personality trait, depending on how long you’ve been helping her look.

## The Story

The bell had been with **Cleo Coldwater** for as long as anyone in Frostpeak Valley could remember.

It was tiny — the size of a blueberry, barely — hammered from silver in the old way, with a tongue so small the sound it made was less a ring and more a *suggestion* of a ring. A breath of a sound. A winter whisper. When Cleo walked, it hung from the corner of her sketchbook by a braided silver cord, and it chimed softly with every step: *ting-ting, ting-ting*, like a distant clock marking the minutes with remarkable cheerfulness.

Her mother had pressed it into her flipper on her first morning in Frostpeak Valley, a cold and overwhelming morning when everything was new and a little frightening.

"Whenever you can’t find your way," her mother had said, "listen for the bell. It will remind you where you are."

Cleo had carried it every day since.

---

The evening it disappeared was a Tuesday, which Cleo would later point out was a very rude day of the week to lose something precious.

The Cocoa Warming Hut was packed. It was the first truly cold night of deep winter, and half the valley had crowded in for warmth and company and **Herschel the Walrus’s** famous cinnamon cocoa. Scarves draped every chair. Boots were stacked in precarious towers by the door. The air smelled of pine wood and warm milk and Barnaby Beaksworth’s experimental fish crackers (which he had seasoned with "a little something extra" that everyone agreed was a little too much of something extra).

Cleo had been sketching all evening — a portrait of Nibbles the seal, who had been posing magnificently with a cocoa mug balanced on her nose and the expression of someone who knew they were being immortalized. When Cleo stood up to get more tea, she reached for her sketchbook and felt—

Nothing.

The familiar gentle weight was gone. The braided cord swung empty.

No bell.

Cleo turned very still. Then she checked her front pocket. Her back pocket. Her scarf loops. Her second front pocket (she had a lot of pockets; she was an artist). She turned her sketchbook upside down and shook it gently. She looked under her chair. She looked on top of her chair. She checked her pockets again in case she’d been wrong the first two times.

"Everything all right?" asked **Nora Snowmane**, who had been sitting nearby and noticed the silence.

"My bell is gone," Cleo said. Her voice was careful and quiet, the way voices get when they’re working very hard to stay that way.

Nora put down her cocoa immediately. "We’ll find it. When did you last hear it?"

"When I came in, maybe? It was chiming when I crossed the threshold."

"Then it’s in here," Nora said, and her calm certainty was more comforting than a whole extra scarf. "Let’s look."

---

The search began quietly, as searches in a crowded room must, with Nora and Cleo checking the space around Cleo’s usual table. Then Nora mentioned it to **Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff**, which was both helpful and inadvisable.

"THERE IS A MISSING BELL," Teddy announced to the room with the urgency of a weather emergency.

"SMALL, SILVER, PRECIOUS, GONE," added Tilda, equally loud.

Within thirty seconds, the entire hut was involved.

Lanterns were unhooked from the walls and held low to illuminate the floor gaps. Boot piles were dismantled with great ceremony and then put back less carefully. Piper Paddlefoot got on her belly and peered under every table, which meant she spent most of the search covered in dust and slightly stuck, narrating her findings: "Dust. Also dust. A cracker. Possibly Barnaby’s cracker. More dust. Oh, this might be a sardine tin — no. That’s a button."

Barnaby himself checked his fish cracker supply with tremendous thoroughness on the off-chance the bell had been accidentally incorporated into a cracker, which everyone agreed was impossible but nobody stopped him from checking.

Finnegan Flipsworth searched the broom closet from top to bottom, emerged with a broom, two forgotten mittens, and a look of tremendous pride, and announced: "No bell, but we solved the mitten crisis."

**Magnus Meltsworth** checked the broom bucket, grimly, and found only one very disgruntled potato that had been aging in there since October. He removed it without comment.

**Bjorn Bigpaws** checked the coat hooks methodically, examining each pocket of every coat with the thoroughness of a bear who did not do things by halves. He found fourteen items that had been lost over the past three weeks — keys, hair clips, a folded note, two small coins, and someone’s second-best mitten — which sparked a whole separate small reunion, but no bell.

Cleo moved through it all quietly, holding a lantern, checking each corner the others had missed. She looked in the woodpile. Between the window ledge and the frame. In the cup rack. In the space where the floorboards met the wall and made a thin shadow that could hide tiny things.

Every few minutes, one of the twins would pop up from a new location and shout "NOT HERE EITHER" with the energy of someone who had never once felt the difference between helpful and merely loud.

The minutes stretched. The search widened. Someone turned the cushions over. Someone else checked the fire grate (briefly, very briefly, hoping for a miracle).

Nothing.

Cleo was beginning to feel the loss settle more heavily on her chest, the way cold settles into hands when you’ve been outside too long. The bell wasn’t just a bell. It was the thread between here and her mother’s voice. Between *now* and the morning in Frostpeak when she’d first arrived and been frightened and then told to *listen*.

She sat down on a stool near the broom closet door and wrapped her flippers around a warm mug.

"Maybe tomorrow," Nora said gently, sitting beside her.

"Maybe," Cleo said.

Then, from inside the broom closet — which Finnegan had already searched, and declared officially bell-free — came a sound.

*ting.*

Everyone froze.

*ting-ting.*

**Tilda Powderpuff** dove back into the closet headfirst, there was a tremendous crash and a cascade of brooms, and then — from within the avalanche of cleaning supplies — a roar of triumph:

"**TINY DING FOUND!**"

She emerged, fur extremely disheveled and one broom handle balanced across her nose, holding up one silver bell between two claws, triumphant as a polar explorer planting a flag.

The bell had rolled under the broom closet’s raised threshold — that half-inch gap where old wood had shrunk away from the floor — and wedged itself in the narrow gap behind a loose plank. When Tilda had crashed into the back wall the first time, she’d just missed it. When she’d dove in again, she’d gone in farther and one falling broom handle had *ting-tinged* the bell loose.

Cleo crossed the room in three steps and took the bell in both flippers.

She held it up. She looked at it for a long, quiet moment. Then she pressed it to her beak and closed her eyes.

"Welcome back," she whispered.

Then she burst into happy tears — the loud, surprised kind that you don’t plan, that come from somewhere deep and warm — and hugged Tilda, who was still wearing a broom on her nose, which made the hug both touching and structurally complicated.

Then she hugged Teddy.

Then Nora.

Then Finnegan ("I helped!" he said. "I moved the brooms *earlier*, which made them fall at exactly the right—" "Thank you, Finnegan." "Genuinely I feel responsible for—" "Thank you.").

Then Piper, who had emerged from under the tables at the sound of triumph and was still slightly dusty.

Then Bjorn, who accepted the hug with great dignity.

Then Barnaby, who was crying a little because he found most things emotional.

Then she worked her way systematically around the room until she had hugged everyone, including a bewildered snow fox who had come in for cocoa and had no idea who anyone was but felt it would be rude not to hug back.

---

Herschel made a fresh round of cinnamon cocoa — the good kind, with the extra cinnamon that he only brought out for special occasions.

Cleo retied the bell to her sketchbook with new cord: braided, doubled, triple-knotted.

"Every slope tells a story," she said, looking at it in the warm light. "I think this bell’s story just got better."

Teddy and Tilda launched into what they called their "discovery dance," which involved no choreography whatsoever but a remarkable amount of enthusiasm and knocked over exactly one chair.

When the bell chimed as Cleo lifted her sketchbook, the whole room went quiet for just a moment — a good quiet, the kind that comes after relief — and then three seals by the window (Nibbles, Splash, and a visiting cousin) clapped in perfect synchrony like they’d been rehearsing for just this occasion.

Maybe they had.

In Frostpeak Valley, stranger things had happened.
