# Story 70: The Little Donkey Snow Parade
### *Inspired by Triumphal Entry — Matthew 21*
**Characters:** Dottie Donkey, Aurora Frostholm  
**Setting:** Frostpeak Valley, Palm Pass  
**Theme:** Gentle leadership

---

## Opening Wink

Frostpeak had one rule for evenings like this: cocoa first, courage second, and snacks always nearby.

## The Story

The Annual Palm Pass Parade was the most anticipated procession of the winter, and this year the committee had chosen the most unexpected possible leader: **Dottie Donkey**.

Dottie was a small, steady donkey with big ears and a gentle, unhurried way of moving through the world. She had been hauling supply sleds between the warming huts for four seasons, which meant she knew every path and pass in Frostpeak Valley better than almost anyone — she had walked them all, in every weather, carrying things for other creatures, without any fanfare or announcement.

Nobody had nominated her for the parade lead. **Aurora Frostholm** had.

"Dottie?" said the parade committee chairperson, Aldric the beaver, peering at Aurora's nomination form. "The supply donkey?"

"The creature who knows every inch of Palm Pass," said Aurora, "has never once missed a delivery in four winters, and is kind to every single creature she meets along her route, including the difficult ones and the ones who forget to say thank you."

"The parade lead is ceremonial," said Aldric. "Usually we choose—"

"Someone important-looking?" said Aurora.

"Someone prominent," said Aldric. "Someone with a certain—"

"Dottie," said Aurora. "Final answer."

---

Dottie found out about the nomination on a Tuesday.

She found out because Pip Starling came running up the supply path to tell her, out of breath, with the quality of someone who has exciting news and cannot contain it.

"You're leading the Palm Pass Parade," said Pip.

Dottie stopped. She set down the supply sled she was pulling. She looked at Pip.

"That doesn't seem right," said Dottie.

"Aurora nominated you. The committee agreed."

"They usually choose someone much more—" Dottie gestured broadly in the direction of spectacular.

"More flashy?" said Pip.

"I was going to say prominent."

"Aurora said you were the right choice." Pip looked at her with the clear eyes of someone who found this obvious. "You know the whole route. You've been on every path in the pass. You'll lead it correctly."

"A parade isn't about correct," said Dottie. "It's about—" she tried to think of the word "—grand."

"Maybe this year it's about kind," said Pip.

---

On the day of the parade, Dottie led the procession through Palm Pass at a steady, unhurried pace.

She did not gallop. She did not perform. She moved along the route the way she always moved — paying attention, checking the path, making way for the smallest members of the procession to stay comfortable, stopping once when she noticed a young snow hare at the side of the path who'd been jostled out of position, and waiting while someone helped the hare find a better spot.

The crowd on either side of Palm Pass cheered. Not the roaring cheer of championship events — something gentler, warmer, more sustained.

Aurora walked beside her partway through.

"Why are they cheering?" Dottie asked, genuinely puzzled. "I'm not doing anything extraordinary."

"You're doing it exactly right," said Aurora. "That's rarer than you'd think."

"I'm just walking the path I always walk."

"Yes," said Aurora. "But you're walking it in front of everyone, and everyone can see how you do it. And the way you do it is — it's the right way to go through Frostpeak Valley." She paused. "Some creatures lead by being the most magnificent thing in the room. You lead by showing what it actually looks like to be good."

Dottie considered this for several steps.

"The small hare," she said. "Who got jostled."

"Yes."

"Someone needed to wait. So I waited."

"Yes," said Aurora. "That's the part."

The procession came through the far end of Palm Pass as the afternoon light turned the snow gold, and the whole valley felt, for the length of the walk, exactly like it was supposed to — warm and attended to and led with care by someone who'd been learning the route for four winters without ever knowing she was practicing for this.

Dottie returned to the supply route the next morning. Third delivery of the day, first good snow of the week, same paths she'd always taken. She said hello to everyone she passed, which was also what she'd always done.

It is possible to lead by being exactly and genuinely yourself. Not louder, not grander — just that. Just yourself, done well.
